## KQ1. Direct substitutes for record review: what the buyer actually gets, units, trials, commitments, discounts, add-ons, upsell triggers, verification/liability handling, and value framing

### Takeaway
Nobody in the set sells "a read of a criminal trial record that returns issue-spotted, record-cited findings" to lawyers as a priced unit; the closest packaged analogues are (a) CaseMark's $25-per-workflow "Appeal Summary" memo (issues mapped to standard of review, preservation tracked, record cites required) and $50 full-trial-transcript summaries, (b) LawLM's $49-per-deposition evidence analysis with $200 free credits, (c) Alexi's $299 pay-as-you-go research memo, and (d) Clearbrief's $300-per-brief option and TypeLaw's $895-per-brief + $400 record-hyperlinking service. Everyone else packages record/transcript analysis as a feature inside a seat subscription (CoCounsel, Lexis+/CaseMap+, Vincent, Paxton, Harvey, Clio Manage AI), a per-GB hosting fee (Everlaw, DISCO), or a bundled freebie tied to buying the transcript itself (Steno Transcript Genius). The dominant unit for small-firm AI is still the seat at $100–$500/user/month with annual terms and 12–18% multi-year discounts; per-matter and per-document units cluster at $25–$300 for software and $895–$1,500+ when a human is in the loop.

### Inferences
- The market has three packaging archetypes for "read this record": (1) feature-inside-a-seat (CoCounsel, Vincent, Paxton, Clio, Lexis) where the record read is "free" at the margin but the seat costs $100–$640/month on an annual term; (2) per-GB hosting (Everlaw, DISCO) where a few hundred MB of PDFs is a rounding error but minimums/credits make it a poor fit for a solo; (3) per-unit (CaseMark $25–$50, LawLM $49, Alexi $299, Clearbrief $300, TypeLaw $895+$400). Snot Nose's $299/review sits at the top of the pure-software per-unit band and well below human-in-the-loop units; the natural attorney positioning is "CaseMark-style unit price, TypeLaw-style scope (whole record, Texas-specific), Clearbrief-style cost recovery".
- Two mechanics recur in the per-unit tier and are cheap to copy: free starter credits ($200 LawLM, $5 CaseMark, 3 Clio credits, first summary free at SmartDepo) and "billable to the client"/separate invoicing (LawLM, Clearbrief, Supio). Pay-only-on-acceptance (TypeLaw) is the strongest trust signal found and is unique to a human-reviewed service.
- Vendors handle liability contractually (not-legal-advice, lawyer verifies, fees-paid cap) and reputationally (page/line pins, mandatory cite-or-flag). A record-review product should expect to ship both; no vendor found offers an accuracy warranty.

### Gaps
- No official Clearbrief page could be read; the $300/brief option is reported only via LawNext's directory and may be discontinued.
- CaseMark's appeal-workflow unit price is inferred from the generic $25/workflow; whether a multi-thousand-page record counts as one workflow (or is page-metered) is unknown.
- No seat limits, page limits or upload caps for CoCounsel, Vincent, Paxton or Clio Manage AI were found.
- Matey/CrimD, Steno, CaseMap+ AI and Alexi 2026 prices are unpublished.

## KQ2. Per-matter pricing precedents in legal tech and in the human-services substitutes (per demand, per case, per brief, per page, per record)

### Takeaway
Per-matter pricing is now mainstream in plaintiff-side legal AI (EvenUp moved to "one clear per-case price" in May 2025; Supio sells a case-based subscription plus an "unlimited" tier with a per-case invoice for client pass-through), and per-unit pricing is standard in the summary/transcript trades ($1–$10/page human deposition summaries; $25–$75 per AI summary; $4.00–$4.40/page federal transcript originals; $6/page Texas indigent reporter's records in Tarrant County). The human substitutes for a record review price far higher: LawClerk freelance appellate work at $1,500–$7,500 per brief (one posted job: $1,500 to "review a very extensive record, spot the strongest appellate issues and draft" the brief), a $15,000 flat fee for an appeal-assessment memo in an Arizona declaration, and $5,000–$15,000 flat-fee post-conviction case reviews.

### Inferences
- Cost-per-record of the substitutes (inferred from cited rates; a 3,000-page Texas reporter's record is assumed): (a) the record itself costs the county roughly $18,000 at Tarrant's $6/page indigent rate, which makes a $299 software read ~1.7% of the record's own production cost; (b) a freelance appellate lawyer reading and spotting issues is bundled into $1,500–$7,500 brief flat fees, i.e. the issue-spotting read alone is worth on the order of $500–$2,500 at LawClerk's 35–40%-of-billable guidance; (c) a paralegal digest at $45/hr × 40 hrs ≈ $1,800 (civil deposition analogue); (d) an outsourced page-line summary at $3–$10/page ≈ $9,000–$30,000; (e) the lawyer doing it themselves at the Texas criminal average $221/hr (market_and_gtm KQ5) for 20–40 hours ≈ $4,400–$8,800, most of which is non-recoverable above county caps.
- The per-case precedents (EvenUp, Supio) show that firms will accept a per-matter software charge when it is (i) all-inclusive (no feature tiers), (ii) invoiced per case for client pass-through, and (iii) justified against paralegal hours on that matter. The same three features map directly onto appointed-counsel vouchering in Texas (market_and_gtm KQ5).
- SmartDepo's volume curve ($75 → $50 per summary at 40+) and Alexi's 10-pack discount (13%) are the per-unit industry's standard "commitment" lever; an attorney tier could offer a 10-record pack rather than a seat.

### Gaps
- No Texas county order other than Tarrant's was found for indigent reporter's-record page rates; Harris, Dallas, Travis and Bexar orders were not located. Non-indigent (retained) per-page rates are set reporter-by-reporter and were not found.
- No published freelance rate specifically for "record review and issue memo" (without a brief) exists; the LawClerk $1,500 job and the $15,000 Arizona memo bracket it.
- No actual EvenUp or Supio per-case dollar figure is public.

## KQ4. Family-side substitutes and price anchors: what they deliver for the money

### Takeaway
Families today choose among (a) free but gated innocence organizations (IPTX accepts only felony, Texas-court, post-direct-appeal, actual-innocence claims, by mail with a TDCJ number, with replies taking months), (b) lawyer case evaluations priced from a $400 consult to $2,500–$15,000 flat reviews that produce a written assessment and are credited toward engagement, and (c) prison consultants at $2,500–$25,000 (market_and_gtm KQ4). No non-lawyer "transcript review service" for families with a published price was found; the only sub-$500 anchors are software (LawLM $49, CaseMark $25–$50), none of which markets to families.

### Cited Findings
- Official (Innocence Project of Texas): "You must be claiming actual innocence. Either no crime occurred or a crime did happen but you did not have a role in it"; felony only; Texas court; "You have been convicted and have completed your direct appeals"; cannot help with general legal requests, pre-conviction cases, educational materials or non-innocence matters; requests "must be mailed with the person's TDCJ number", no email/phone/fax; replies "can take up to several months because of volume" — [IPTX, Submit a Case](https://innocencetexas.org/submit-a-case/); Texas state prisoners only (third-party summary) — [HowToJustice IPTX](https://howtojustice.org/directory/innocence-project-of-texas/); intake volumes (~1,000–1,250/yr) and TIDC reports are in market_and_gtm KQ1
- Pennsylvania Innocence Project requires the letter to come from the convicted person — market_and_gtm KQ6
- Lawyer evaluations: $400 consult then $5,000–$15,000 written assessment credited to engagement (Franklin-Best); "reduced-cost case evaluation" (Burnham & Gorokhov); $2,500–$5,000 non-refundable investigative fee after free screening (Reaves) — KQ2 above; families are advised to confirm "whether the fee covers only initial review, a complete filing, or representation through a potential hearing" — [Daeryun Law post-conviction](https://www.daeryunlaw.com/us/insights/appeals-and-post-conviction-relief-lawyer-nyc)
- Appeal-cost guide: "an appellate attorney will be also able to make an analysis of your case so you can see if there are appealable problems, usually for a small fee" (no figure) — [ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-an-appeal-cost/)
- Law-school clinics provide free habeas preparation in some states (e.g., Kansas) — [KU Project for Innocence](https://law.ku.edu/public-resources/innocence-project)
- No source was found for non-lawyer paralegal "post-conviction review" services with published prices; the search surfaced only firm marketing and supervised paralegal job descriptions.

### Inferences
- The family market's effective price ladder is $0 (innocence orgs, months of wait, narrow eligibility) → $400 (one-hour lawyer consult) → $2,500–$15,000 (written case assessment) → $25,000+ (engagement). Snot Nose's $299 sits between the free tier and the consult, and its deliverable (record-cited findings + counsel priority list) resembles the $5,000–$15,000 "written assessment" in form, which is both the commercial opportunity and the UPL exposure flagged in market_and_gtm KQ6.
- Because IPTX and peers reject anything that is not an actual-innocence claim and require completed direct appeals, a large share of family demand (ineffective assistance, Brady, sentencing, pre-appeal) has no free outlet at all.

### Gaps
- No Texas-specific lawyer "appeal evaluation" flat fee under $2,500 was found; the $400 consult figure is from a federal post-conviction firm in South Carolina.
- Prison-consultant deliverables for "post-conviction" (as opposed to sentencing/designation) work were not separately verified.

## KQ5. How legal-AI vendors justify price (hours saved, ROI, guarantees, pilots) and 2025–2026 reporting on seat utilization, churn and "AI fatigue"

### Takeaway
Price justification is almost entirely vendor-commissioned hours-saved arithmetic (TR/Forrester: 222–400% ROI, 0.5–1.5 hrs/day, "6 weeks a year"; CaseMark: 6 hours → 8 minutes; Matey: 90% time saved, $40k/matter; Supio: $500–$1,000/case; JusticeText: ~50% time) and pass-through logic (Supio/LawLM "billable to the client"); no money-back guarantee or outcome warranty was found at any legal-AI vendor, and the only pay-on-acceptance model is TypeLaw's human service. On the demand side, Harvey's own Q3 2025 disclosure of 77% seat utilization (98% GRR, 167% NDR), third-party reports of 30–50% idle seats and 10–25% renewal uplifts, Clio's finding that legal-specific AI use fell from 58% to 40% in 2025 while overall AI use flatlined at 79%, and TR's 2026 finding that most firms have not changed pricing to reflect AI and only 18% collect AI ROI metrics, together describe seat fatigue rather than churn — buyers keep paying but use less, and vendors are drifting toward consumption pricing.

### Cited Findings

**Value framing**
- TR/Forrester ROI and hours-saved figures — KQ1 (vendor-commissioned)
- CaseMark "6 hours manual versus 8 minutes" — KQ1; Supio "$500-1000 per case", "80+ hours" — KQ2; Matey "up to 90 percent time savings on discovery review ... more than $40,000 per matter" — [Pulse 2.0 Matey](https://pulse2.com/matey-7-5-million-raised-to-bring-ai-to-criminal-defense); JusticeText "nearly 50% of their time" (TR Institute, company-sourced) — tools_landscape KQ3
- Generic ROI arithmetic used in buyer guides: "an attorney billing $300/hour who saves 10 hours a month on research using a $200/month AI tool ... 15x return" — [AI Vortex, how much does legal AI cost](https://www.aivortex.io/legal/guides/how-much-does-legal-ai-cost/); some tools are "priced as a percentage (typically 30-50%) of the equivalent human attorney hourly rate" — [Monetizely, AI legal pricing models](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/pricing-models-for-ai-legal-tools-accuracy-guarantees-vs-hourly-rate-structures); seat pricing "assume[s] every attorney uses it daily, and most do not" — [ibl.ai law firm AI cost model](https://ibl.ai/solutions/legal/course/law-firm-ai-cost-model.md); no vendor found using an explicit "less than one billable hour per month" tagline
- LawClerk's buyer-facing framing: price freelance work at "35-40% of the estimated billable value" — KQ2
- Ethics constraint on the "hours saved" pitch: Texas Opinion 705 and ABA 512 bar billing clients for time saved by AI, while allowing per-use AI costs to be passed through at cost (tools_landscape KQ6) — which favors per-matter, pass-through units over seat subscriptions for retained criminal work

**Guarantees, trials and pilots**
- No legal-AI vendor money-back guarantee found; the only "money-back guarantee" hit was an IT managed-services provider (Aug 2024) — [Innovative Computing](https://www.innovativecomp.com/category/managed-services); vendors "accommodate pilots, trials, or other programs" — [Billables.ai, selecting AI tools (June 2025)](https://billables.ai/blog/how-to-select-ai-tools-for-your-law-firm); Bloomberg Law (June 2026): firms "are pulling their tech vendors in close—but aren't ready to commit long-term at this point in the AI wave" — [Bloomberg Law, tech contracts](https://news.bgov.com/legal-ops-and-tech/tech-contracts-for-a-limited-time-only-legal-ops-tech)
- Trial mechanics observed: Paxton 7-day card-required trial; vLex free trial auto-converting to paid; Clearbrief contradictory; CaseMark/LawLM/Clio-CaseMark free credits; TypeLaw pay-on-acceptance — KQ1
- Harvey evaluation-period terms disclaim accuracy ("may contain errors and misstatements") — KQ1

**Seat utilization, renewal and "AI fatigue" (2025–2026)**
- Harvey (company figures, Q3 2025): "77% seat utilization, 98% gross revenue retention, and 167% net dollar retention" — reported by [Claude for Lawyers, Harvey reviews](https://claudeforlawyers.com/blog/harvey-ai-customer-reviews) (secondary; Harvey's own post not retrieved); third-party: "30-50% of licensed seats go underutilized" (unsourced) — [AI Vortex Harvey review](https://www.aivortex.io/legal/ai-tools/harvey-ai/); "annual renewal uplifts of 10 to 25% where no contractual cap was negotiated" — [Vaquill, Harvey price increase](https://www.vaquill.ai/blog/harvey-ai-price-increase-per-seat); complaints of "unused licensed seats" alongside "opaque pricing" — [TheLegalPrompts Harvey reviews](https://thelegalprompts.com/blog/harvey-ai-reviews); vendors "moving away from flat per-seat licenses toward consumption-based pricing" (Harvey token usage reportedly up 14x, unverified) — [My AI Strategist, The Meter Is On](https://myaistrategist.substack.com/p/the-meter-is-on-law-firms-are-using); a Substack argues consumption pricing is forcing firms toward value pricing — [Pimbetist Substack](https://pimbetist.substack.com/p/consumption-based-pricing-is-forcing)

- Thomson Reuters Future of Professionals 2026 (survey of 1,816 professionals, March–April 2026; 736 law-firm and 203 corporate legal respondents): "most have not adjusted their pricing structures to reflect AI-driven efficiencies"; "91% ... experiencing a value gap"; "64% ... say AI meets or exceeds expectations where a clear AI strategy is in place, compared to just 29% where there is none"; "34% of law firm professionals use AI their organization hasn't approved"; "32% of in-house legal professionals are already reconsidering or will reconsider relationships with firms that do not demonstrate clear AI-enabled value within the next 12 months" — [TR Future of Professionals law firms 2026](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute/reports/future-of-professionals-law-firms-paper-2026); [TR press release, June 22 2026](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2026/june/ai-is-ready-but-firms-are-not-how-falling-behind-on-ai-implementation-is-costing-clients-and-talent); TR blog: "only 22% of firms have visible AI strategies, and 30% admit they're moving too slowly" — [TR, why law firms struggle with AI ROI](https://legal.thomsonreuters.com/blog/why-law-firms-are-struggling-with-ai-roi/); critique: "18% of organisations collect AI ROI metrics, 42% do not, and 40% do not know" — [Bad Faith newsletter, AI ROI](https://badfaith.beehiiv.com/p/ai-roi-more-than-a-feeling)
- Buyer-side: CLOC/Harbor survey — "26% expect to cut spending on law firms in 2026" — [Harbor/Artificial Lawyer](https://harborglobal.com/articles/some-clients-see-law-firm-spend-drop-just-as-ai-embeds-artificial-lawyer/); ACC/Everlaw: in-house AI adoption 23% → 52% in a year; 64% expect to depend less on outside counsel — [ABA, ten AI predictions 2026](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/newsletters/corporate-counsel/ten-ai-predictions-2026-leading-analysts-say-legal-teams-expect/)
- Transcript-adjacent funding signal: Steno $49M Series C (Mar 2026) for AI transcript analysis — KQ1

### Inferences
- The evidence supports "seat fatigue, not churn": vendors report high gross retention but ~one in four seats idle, buyers report price uplifts at renewal, and solo/small firms are substituting free general-purpose AI for paid legal AI. A per-record unit with no seat avoids the idle-seat objection entirely and converts the "we can't measure ROI" problem into a per-matter cost the lawyer can voucher or pass through.
- Because no legal-AI vendor offers an accuracy or money-back guarantee, a modest acceptance guarantee (e.g., TypeLaw-style "don't pay if the findings are unusable" or a re-run credit) would be differentiating but must be paired with not-legal-advice/verify-before-filing terms that every vendor uses.
