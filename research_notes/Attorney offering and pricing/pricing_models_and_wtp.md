## Key Question 1 — Legal-tech pricing-model outcomes 2024–2026: seat vs usage vs per-matter (adoption, churn, utilization, what buyers say)

### Takeaway
Per-seat remains the dominant legal-AI model but is openly criticized by buyers (seat counts get rationed; small firms say they are "priced out"); the visible 2025–2026 movement is toward per-matter/per-case and consumption pricing in niches where the unit of work is obvious (EvenUp per case, Everlaw per GB, deposition-summary vendors per page or per summary, Clearbrief per brief), and no independent survey quantifying churn or utilization by pricing model was found.

### Cited Findings
- Bloomberg Law (2025, "AI Legal Software Flails on Pricing Models, Frustrating Buyers"): "traditional software pricing, where vendors charge on a per-seat basis, remains dominant"; some vendors are moving to usage-based models, which "makes costs harder to compare across tools"; Ivo prices contract review per seat and increasingly by consumption; Agiloft sells per seat but its AI platform Astra "is experimenting with consumption-based pricing"; a former head of legal ops at Hewlett-Packard: "When companies are paying per seat, they tend to limit the number of licenses they buy" (reported; article date not retrievable, host blocked) — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/legal-ops-and-tech/ai-legal-software-flails-on-pricing-models-frustrating-buyers)
- EvenUp (personal-injury demand-letter AI) announced "a new per-case pricing model… One clear, predictable cost per case" for access to its full Claims Intelligence Platform, replacing per-output pricing (official press release, 2025; amount not disclosed) — [EvenUp release via Silicon UK](https://www.silicon.co.uk/press-release/evenup-launches-ai-drafts-suite-smart-workflows-medical-bill-summary-and-case-based-pricing-to-redefine-legal-automation-for-personal-injury-firms); third-party aggregators report the prior per-demand price as "starting at $300 per demand" rising to "$500 to $800+", with monthly totals "$500–$2,000/month for most PI firms" (reported, unverified) — [Softwarefinder](https://softwarefinder.com/legal/evenup); [Sacra, "Per-Case Pricing for Plaintiff Firms"](https://sacra.com/chat/h/ee321cf5-4f90-45ee-9780-47a457514a02/)
- Everlaw (e-discovery) prices by data volume with unlimited users: "core review, processing, and many single-document AI features are included in the per-GB rate; batch GenAI actions require credits"; no published rate card; a procurement-benchmark site estimates $20–$35/GB/month mid-size and $30–$45/GB all-in high-volume (reported, labelled internal estimates) — [Spendbase Everlaw pricing analysis](https://www.spendbase.co/?p=37460); [RFP.wiki Everlaw vs Reveal](https://www.rfp.wiki/legal-compliance/e-discovery/everlaw/reveal)
- Deposition-summary vendors show the per-page/per-unit end of the spectrum: SmartDepo lists "$1 per page" after a free first summary (and, inconsistently, flat tiers of $75 down to $50 per summary) (vendor page, 2025–26) — [SmartDepo pricing](https://www.smartdepo.law/pricing); [SmartDepo home](https://www.smartdepo.law/); CaseMark "starts at $25 per summary" (reported) — [ABA Journal column](https://www.abajournal.com/columns/article/ai-in-litigation-streamlining-deposition-summarization-and-analysis); Deposely launched free gen-AI deposition tools (Feb 2025) and is "targeting about $300 to $350 per deposition" for its full product (reported) — [LawNext, Feb 2025](https://www.lawnext.com/2025/02/deposely-launches-free-gen-ai-tools-for-deposition-work-previews-comprehensive-ai-deposition-platform.html); a vendor guide puts manual/outsourced summaries at $3–$10 per page vs ~$0.02/page AI cost (vendor claim) — [howaiworks.ai](https://howaiworks.ai/use-cases/ai-for-deposition-summaries)
- Enterprise legal-AI seat prices (all reported by vendor-adjacent comparison blogs, not rate cards): Harvey ≈ $1,200–$2,000+ per user per month with "25-seat minimum on a 12-month term"; CoCounsel Legal ≈ $225–$400+ per user per month, ≈ $639 bundled with Westlaw; Lexis+ AI/Protégé ≈ $250–$500 per seat per month, "from ~$400/month for a solo attorney on full research" — [Vaquill, Harvey/Legora/CoCounsel pricing reality 2026](https://www.vaquill.ai/blog/harvey-legora-cocounsel-pricing-reality-2026); [Vaquill, Lexis+ AI pricing 2026](https://www.vaquill.ai/blog/lexis-plus-ai-pricing-2026); [Elephas pricing comparison](https://elephas.app/resources/legal-ai-tools-pricing-comparison); a healthcare GC interviewed by Sacra argues Harvey and peers "consistently fail to justify their per-seat pricing against enterprise ChatGPT plus a research tool like Westlaw" (one buyer's opinion) — [Sacra, "Per-Seat Legal AI Pricing Gap"](https://sacra.com/chat/h/646af413-6a75-475a-b758-b2251d80b804/)
- Thomson Reuters/Georgetown 2026 State of the US Legal Market (Jan 2026): law-firm spending on technology grew 9.7% and on knowledge-management tools 10.5% in 2025; TR Legal Tracker data show "90% of legal dollars still flow through hourly billing arrangements" — [Legaltech Monitor, Jan 2026](https://www.legaltechmonitor.com/2026/01/legal-tech-spending-surges-9-7-as-firms-race-to-integrate-ai-says-report-on-state-of-legal-market/); [TR blog on the 2026 report](https://legal.thomsonreuters.com/blog/5-critical-insights-from-the-2026-state-of-the-us-legal-market-report-what-in-house-legal-teams-must-do-now/)
- Smaller firms say AI vendors are pricing them out: The Logic (Nov 2025) reports "smaller rivals warn they're getting priced out by AI vendors" while legal-tech spend was up 11%+ in Q3 2025 per TR's Law Firm Financial Index; by May 2026 TR said it has "a strategy to expand down market" because "smaller and solo players" are now buying — [The Logic, Nov 2025](https://thelogic.co/news/big-laws-ai-spending-blitz-threatens-to-crowd-out-smaller-firms/); [The Logic, May 2026](https://thelogic.co/briefing/thomson-reuters-faces-questions-about-ai-spend/)
- Clio 2024 Legal Trends Report (9th ed.): AI use jumped to 79% of legal professionals from 19% in 2023; firms bill 34% more of their cases flat-fee than in 2016; 71% of clients favor flat fees for an entire case and 51% for individual activities; Clio's CEO frames AI efficiency as pushing firms to flat fees that "better align with the value they deliver" — [Clio press release](https://www.clio.com/about/press/clio-latest-legal-trends-report/); [Law360](https://law360.com/articles/1887174/most-legal-professionals-say-they-have-adopted-some-ai); Clio 2025 Legal Trends Report: 59% of firms used flat fees exclusively or alongside hourly in 2024; among heavy AI users 20% report trouble meeting billable targets and 45% have adjusted pricing — [2Civility summary of 2025 LTR](https://www.2civility.org/2025-clio-legal-trends-report/)
- Legal-tech investment context: $4.08 billion invested in legal tech in 2025 and $2.34 billion in Q1 2026; Harvey raised $200M at an $11B valuation (Mar 2026); a 2026 buyers' guide notes "almost everything was built for BigLaw and enterprise legal departments" — [Broadband Breakfast, 2026](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/legal-tech-valuations-surge-in-2026-because-of-ai/); [Relevant Audience guide](https://www.relevantaudience.com/ai/best-ai-tools-law-firms-2026/)
- Practical Law's whole-firm (not per-seat) pricing is cited by Legal Technology Hub as the model that "got right" adoption economics for content/AI tools (article could not be fetched; title only) — [Legal Technology Hub](https://www.legaltechnologyhub.com/contents/per-seat-or-whole-firm-what-practical-law-got-right-about-legal-tech-pricing/)

### Inferences
- The unit of value for a record review is the matter (one record, one packet), which is exactly the condition under which 2025–26 vendors moved to per-case pricing (EvenUp) and where buyers report the least friction; a seat model would force a solo appellate lawyer with 6–12 appointed appeals a year to pay for idle months and would trigger the "limit the licenses" behaviour the HP quote describes in offices.
- A per-page meter (SmartDepo-style) is legible to appellate lawyers because reporter's records are already priced per page (see Q5), but EvenUp's retreat from per-output to per-case suggests a flat per-record price with page bands (e.g., ≤800 pages / >800 pages, mirroring Harris County's own fee-schedule break) is the more predictable presentation.
- "Whole-office" licenses (unlimited users, priced on volume) have precedent in Everlaw and Practical Law and fit PD appellate divisions of 5–10 lawyers better than seats.

### Gaps
- No independent 2024–2026 survey quantifying churn, utilization or shelfware rates by pricing model in legal AI was found; Bloomberg Law's piece is qualitative. The ILTA and TR benchmark reports that might hold such data were not accessible.
- The Bloomberg Law article date and full text, and the Legal Technology Hub article, could not be retrieved (hosts blocked).
- EvenUp's actual per-case price and the share of PI firms that converted are not public.

## Key Question 2 — Solo/small criminal-defense firm budgets and willingness to pay for AI

### Takeaway
Solos budget little for technology (41–45% have any tech budget; small firms spend ~2% of expenses on software; solo practice-management spend <$3,000/yr), adopt AI slowly (8% of solos "widely"; 18–23% any firm-level AI use in 2024), and in Texas say cost is a shrinking but real concern (18% in 2024 → 10% in 2026) while explicitly wanting bar-vetted, non-training tools at "a tiny fraction" of Lexis AI's price; no survey isolates criminal-defense solos' AI willingness to pay.

- ABA Journal consultants' rules of thumb for a law-firm tech budget: 3%, 4–7% or 5–7% of total budget (not AI-specific, not small-firm specific) — [ABA Journal, "Creating a legal tech budget"](https://www.abajournal.com/web/article/creating-a-legal-tech-budget)

### Inferences
- A Texas solo appellate lawyer grossing roughly the state median ($165k) who follows the 2%-of-expenses software norm has on the order of $2,000–$4,000/yr for all software; a $100–$300/month seat would consume most of it, whereas a per-record fee that is billed through to the client or county sits outside that budget line. This is the strongest quantitative argument for per-matter pricing to solos.
- The AffiniPay finding (43% buy AI because it arrived inside software they already trust) and the Texas Bar quotes favour distribution through trusted channels (bar-endorsed, TCDLA partner page) and a "no data retention, bar-vetted" positioning over price alone.
- Public-sector anchors already in the market put one research/AI tool at ≈$1,300–$1,500 per attorney per year (Lexis/Westlaw in PD offices; JusticeText/Virginia ≈$1,280 from the prior note); an office license priced inside that band per appellate attorney will look normal to a chief defender.

- No survey isolates criminal-defense solos' software budgets or AI willingness to pay; all figures are all-practice-area.
- The Clio 2025 solo/small report and the Clio 2025/2026 Legal Trends Reports could not be opened (host blocked); the "2% of expenses on software" figure comes from a search extract of Clio's highlights page and should be verified.
- No 2025 ABA TechReport budgeting figures were retrievable.
- The State Bar 2025 rate report's practice-area table (criminal-law median) is in a part of the PDF not captured in the cached text.

## Key Question 3 — Public-defender procurement: price points and approval thresholds

### Takeaway
Since Sept 1, 2025 a Texas county's competitive-bidding threshold is $100,000 (SB 1173 raised Local Gov't Code §262.023(a) from $50,000); county-level policies set lower internal approval tiers (e.g., Navarro: $25,000–$49,999 needs commissioners court; Bexar is moving department-head authority to $100,000), so a PD office license priced under roughly $25,000–$50,000/yr can typically be bought on a purchase order or sole-source memo; observed PD AI/software contracts cluster at $5,000–$50,000/yr (Cumberland Co. PA JusticeText $17,300/yr; DuPage Co. IL JusticeText ≤$50,000/yr sole-source; Warren Co. NY LegalServer ≤$48,900), and TIDC Improvement Grants require a county cash match (multi-year 80/60/40/20 reimbursement; $5,000 minimum; routine tech upgrades ineligible).

### Inferences
- For Texas PD offices, an annual office license under $25,000 is the most frictionless: below most counties' internal commissioners-court tier and far below the $100,000 bidding threshold; $25,000–$100,000 generally needs a commissioners-court agenda item and written quotes; above $100,000 triggers formal procurement (or a cooperative/sole-source justification).
- The observed PD AI contract band ($17,300–$50,000/yr for JusticeText at county scale; ~$1,300–$1,500/attorney/yr for research tools) suggests pricing a 5–10-lawyer appellate division at roughly $10,000–$25,000/yr, or per-record pricing that totals similarly at 100–200 records/yr.
- TIDC's match structure means a county must put cash in from year one; a vendor "pilot credit" that is documented as in-kind or discount lowers the county's match exposure and fits TIDC's "process improvement" test if framed around appellate backlog or writ capacity (see prior note).

## Key Question 4 — Reimbursability of a per-matter software fee for appointed counsel (Texas Art. 26.05(d); federal CJA)

### Takeaway
Texas Art. 26.05(d) makes "reasonable and necessary expenses, including expenses for investigation and for mental health and other experts" reimbursable, with prior approval via a sealed ex parte request (26.052(f)–(g)) or after the fact if "reasonably necessary and reasonably incurred" (26.052(h)); county schedules convert that into closed lists (El Paso: travel, transcripts, exhibits, up to 3/6 hours of legal research, and "other expenses… only if prior court approval has been obtained") and expressly treat "equipment" as non-reimbursable overhead, so a per-record review fee is most plausibly vouchered as a prior-approved "other expense" tied to the record, not as a subscription. Federally, CJA counsel can incur up to $1,000 of services without prior authorization (since Jan 1, 2024), computer-assisted legal research is reimbursable up to what the equivalent manual hours would have cost, and software "not typically found in a law office" needs a CJA 21 provider authorization; the 2025 non-capital appeal maximum is $9,700 ($9,800 in 2026) at $175/$177 per hour.

The El Paso "equipment… may not be claimed" clause is the main obstacle to a subscription being vouchered; a per-record deliverable priced like a transcript or investigator invoice avoids it.
- The 2025 Harris schedule has no presumptive maxima (hourly only), so there the sale is "fewer hours at $125" rather than reimbursement; in capped counties (Williamson $7,500; Collin flat $3,500/$2,000; Midland $4,000 per the prior note) a flat per-record fee that is reimbursed as an expense is worth more because it sits outside the fee cap.
- Federal: a per-record review priced under $1,000 can be obtained without prior authorization as an "other service" (§ 310.20.30) and claimed on the CJA 21; above that, or for a subscription, counsel needs prior authorization and a CJA 21 provider record, and should document the manual-hours equivalent the way CALR claims are documented. The appeal maximum ($9,700 ≈ 55 hours at $175) is a meaningful cap that makes hour-saving tools attractive even when unreimbursed.

## Key Question 5 — Value anchors: hourly rates, record-review hours, cost of the reporter's record, private review prices

### Takeaway
A Texas appellate record costs roughly $6 per page for the original plus copies (Tarrant County's TRAP 20.2 indigent rate, Dec 2025; federal ordinary transcripts $4.40/page), so a 1,000-page record is ≈$6,000 of county money before any lawyer reads it; established benchmarks allow about 1 hour per 50 record pages for review (California appellate projects), i.e., ~20 hours ≈ $2,500–$3,100 at Texas appointed rates ($125–$155/hr) or ≈ $7,400 at the $368 private median; appointed caps ($2,250–$7,500 per appeal in sampled counties) and the federal $9,700 appeal maximum bound what the hours are worth.

### Cited Findings
- Reporter's record pricing: Tex. Gov't Code § 52.047 lets the official reporter set the transcript fee, requires delivery within 120 days of application plus payment or indigency, lets the judge "determine a reasonable fee" on objection, and caps additional copies at one-third of the original per-page cost; the reporter may add charges for postage, exhibits, indexing and binding — [Gov't Code § 52.047 (FindLaw)](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/government-code/gov-t-sect-52-047/); [onecle text](https://law.onecle.com/texas/government/52.047.html); AG Opinion GA-0164 confirms transcript fees are reporter income, not salary supplements — [GA-0164](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/opinions/greg-abbott/ga-0164)
- Tarrant County order under TRAP 20.2 sets the indigent-criminal-appeal reporter's-record rate at $6.00 per page for the original and two copies, $2.00 per page for additional copies, $10 for CD/flash-drive conversion, effective Dec 15, 2025 (official local rule) — [Tarrant County TRAP 20.2 rate order](https://topics.txcourts.gov/LocalRulesPublic/PreviewAttachment/2737); TRAP 20.2 makes the county pay the reporter for an indigent appellant's record "in the amount the trial court sets" (rule text in the cached Feb 6, 2026 TRAP) — [Texas Rules of Appellate Procedure (Feb 2026)](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1461565/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf); a Collin County prosecutors' forum post says the State is billed "at the rate charged to indigent defendants in that county" (anecdote) — [TDCAA forum](https://tdcaa.infopop.net/eve/forums/a/tpc/f/157098965/m/3297069416)

### Inferences
- Worked anchors for a 1,000-page record: county pays ≈$6,000 for the record itself; a lawyer at the CCAP norm spends ≈20 hours reading it (≈$2,500 at $125/hr Harris; ≈$3,100 at $155/hr Travis/Williamson; ≈$3,500 at $175 CJA; ≈$7,360 at the $368 private median). - Buyer trust requirements that shape presentation: Texas lawyers' top AI concerns are ethics/misuse (69%), client-info privacy (65%), fictitious citations (64%) and confidentiality/privilege (64%), while cost is 10% (2026 State Bar survey, Q2); AffiniPay: trust in an existing vendor is the #1 purchase reason (43%) (Q2).

### Inferences
- The market-standard presentation for a lawyer-facing tier is: monthly price, ~17–20% off on annual prepay, a no-card trial, and a bar-partner discount of ~10–15%; for a per-record product the equivalents are a free or heavily discounted first record, volume packs (e.g., 5- or 10-record bundles) and a TCDLA/HCCLA member rate.
- A "no findings, no fee" guarantee has no precedent in legal AI and interacts badly with the ethics framing (it implies the tool, not the lawyer, judges the merits); an accuracy/citation guarantee ("every finding carries a record cite or your fee is refunded") is closer to the Orbital Witness precedent and directly answers the 64% "fictitious cases" concern.
- For PD offices, the observable playbook is a time-boxed pilot with a few attorneys → a one-year contract under the county's internal approval tier ($17k–$50k) → sole-source renewal; pricing the pilot so that the first-year contract stays below the county's commissioners-court tier shortens the cycle.
