## Key Question 1: TIDC Appellate Addendum (Dec 2016) and 2015 Guidelines — recommended appeals per attorney, hours per appeal, record-length assumptions

### Takeaway
The December 2016 Appellate Addendum recommends a full-time appellate caseload of no more than 40 / 30 / 20 / 14 appeals per year for reporter's records of <100 / 100–500 / 500–1,500 / >1,500 pages (34.8 / 46.4 / 69.1 / 100.2 Delphi hours per case against 1,389 case-hours per year), which weights to 31.2 appeals per year; it classifies by record length, not by an explicit pages-per-hour reading rate. The January 2015 trial-level guidelines explicitly excluded appeals.

### Cited Findings
- Document: "Appellate Addendum: Guidelines for Indigent Defense Caseloads, A Report to the Texas Indigent Defense Commission, Pursuant to House Bill 1318," Public Policy Research Institute (Texas A&M), December 2016; authors Dottie Carmichael, Steve Wood, Heather Caspers, George Naufal — [TIDC Appellate Addendum PDF](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- Final recommended full-time-equivalent annual caseloads (Exec. Summary p. ix; Fig. 5-2 p. 16; p. 17): "Less than 100 Pages: 40 cases; 100-500 Pages: 30 cases; 500-1500 Pages: 20 cases; More than 1500 Pages: 14 cases." Weighted by observed case mix: "an overall estimate of 31.2 appellate cases per year." — [Addendum](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- Table 5-1 (p. 13) Delphi-recommended hours per case: <100 pages 34.8 hours; 100–500 pages 46.4 hours; 500–1,500 pages 69.1 hours; 1,500+ pages 100.2 hours; "Time Available: 1,389 case-related hours in the work year." Formula: "(2,087 Hours/Work-Year – 698 Hours of Non-Case Time) / (# Hours/Case) = Annual Full-Time Caseload." The 2,087-hour year is the federal OPM divisor; the 698 non-case hours came from 5 of 12 panelists (range 1,351–1,439 available hours). — [Addendum p. 13](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- Table 5-2 (p. 15) record-length distribution in the timekeeping data: <100 pages 36% of cases; 100–500 pages 41%; 500–1,500 pages 22%; 1,500+ pages 1%; weighted caseload 14.4 + 12.3 + 4.4 + 0.1 = 31.2. — [Addendum p. 15](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- Current-practice hours actually spent (Fig. 3-1, p. 9), Stage 1-2 (post-conviction through Court of Appeals mandate): 17.2 hrs (<100 pp), 31.4 hrs (100–500), 49.5 hrs (500–1,500), 97.3 hrs (>1,500); Stage 3 (CCA/PDR): 13.3, 10.5, 16.3, 9.6 hrs respectively. Text: "A reporter's record with less than 100 pages requires about 17 hours to reach an appellate court mandate, while a case with more than 1500 pages requires almost 100 hours." — [Addendum p. 9](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- Delphi vs. current (Fig. 4-1, p. 10): Stage 1-2 Delphi hours 32.4 / 43.1 / 66.8 / 98.6; Stage 3 Delphi hours 22.8 / 26.3 / 29.6 / 30.0 (vs 15.2 baseline shown). Delphi recommended "the largest proportional increase (88 percent) in time spent on the simplest cases" in the Court of Appeals and "an extraordinary 213 percent increase in time for cases with more than 1500 pages in Stage 3"; smallest Stage 3 increase 71% for <100 pages. — [Addendum p. 10](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- Delphi hours per task, Stage 2 Court of Appeals (Fig. 4-2b, p. 12), by record bucket (<100 / 100–500 / 500–1,500 / >1,500): Record Review 3 / 9 / 17 / 40 hours; Appellate Briefs 12 / 16 / 30 / 35 hours; Client Communication 2 / 2 / 2 / 3; Initiation of Appeal 1 each; Appellate Motions 1 each; Oral Argument 2 / 2 / 4 / 5; Case-Specific Office Support 2 / 2 / 3 / 4. Stage 3 (Fig. 4-2c): Client Communication 2; PDR 4; Briefs after PDR granted 8 / 10 / 12 / 12; Office Support 3; Oral Argument 7 / 8 / 9 / 9. Narrative: "Delphi members concluded the review of records exceeding 1500 pages in length should be the single most time consuming activity for appellate counsel." — [Addendum pp. 11–12](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- Stage 1 (motion for new trial) Delphi hours (Fig. 4-2a): MNT investigation 4–7 hrs; MNT litigation 2–4 hrs; investigator time 0–2 hrs; "Delphi experts recommended two to three hours of case time be allocated to external investigators during Stage 1." — [Addendum pp. 11, 14](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- Record length as the complexity metric (p. 6): "the length of the trial court reporter's record is one of the few consistently available indicators of difficulty and attorney effort available for appellate cases... The record length is widely used as a caseload metric among appellate defender offices" (citing Illinois OSAD, Washington Defender Association, Michigan SADO). The report does not state a pages-per-hour reading rate; it only buckets by record length. — [Addendum p. 6](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- Data: timekeeping from Dallas and Harris County PDOs for "857 appellate cases (607 from Dallas County and 250 from Harris County)" with mandate dates January 2012–February 16, 2016 and a known reporter's-record page length. — [Addendum p. viii, p. 4](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- Delphi panel: 12 appellate practitioners (10 solo/firm, 2 chief appellate public defenders), averaging 22.9 years in practice; three rounds Nov 2015–Jan 14, 2016; advised by Norman Lefstein and Stephen Hanlon. — [Addendum pp. 4, iv](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- Market concentration: "during FY 2015, only 55 Texas attorneys handled 10 or more court-appointed appellate cases" (citing TIDC FY2015 Statewide Indigent Defense Caseload Report); only six private attorneys could be recruited for timekeeping. — [Addendum pp. 3–4](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- Funnel: of 1,429,714 felony and Class A/B misdemeanor cases disposed in FY2013–14, "only 11,917 cases reached the second phase: Proceedings in the Court of Appeals"; "About a quarter of cases (27 percent, n=2,512) advanced to the third stage: Proceedings in the Court of Criminal Appeals." — [Addendum pp. 5–6](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- Oral argument (fn. 21, p. 10): Delphi members said oral argument should occur in 33–40% of COA cases; "the actual rate of oral arguments at this stage is only four percent"; for the CCA, recommended ~74% vs actual 11%. Actual rates were used in the caseload math. — [Addendum p. 10](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- Comparison to current practice (Fig. 5-1, p. 14): current full-time caseloads were 67 / 41 / 26 / 14 cases vs Delphi 40 / 30 / 20 / 14 — reductions of 40%, 27%, 23%, 0%. NAC 1973 standard is 25 appeals per year; Delphi "allow for Texas appellate attorneys to accept an additional 6.2 cases each year than are advised by the NAC standard." — [Addendum pp. 14–16](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- Mixed-caseload illustration (p. 17): an attorney with 30 appeals (<100 pp) = 50% effort, 11 appeals (100–500 pp) = 25%, 15 appeals (500–1,500 pp) = 50% "would exceed full-time caseload guidelines by 25 percent." — [Addendum p. 17](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)
- 2015 main report: "Guidelines for Indigent Defense Caseloads," PPRI, January 22, 2015. "Investigation was limited in focus to adult criminal trial-level cases. Other types of cases such as juvenile cases and appeals were excluded from analysis because of time constraints." It contains no appellate case weight; it cites the NAC "25 appeals" standard. — [TIDC 2015 Guidelines PDF](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/kzfd5tup/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-01222015.pdf) (note: the older URL /media/31818/150122_weightedcl_final.pdf now returns 404)

### Inferences
- Implied record-reading throughput (estimate, derived from Table 5-1 and Fig. 4-2b): Delphi allots 17 hours of record review for a 500–1,500-page record (~30–90 pages/hour) and 40 hours for >1,500 pages; total Delphi hours per appeal rise roughly 65 hours between the smallest and largest bucket while record length rises by >1,400 pages, i.e., on the order of 20–25 additional pages of record per additional hour of total case time.
- At the Harris County district-court appellate rate of $125/hour (Q2), a Delphi-compliant <100-page appeal (34.8 hrs) is about $4,350 and a 100–500-page appeal (46.4 hrs) about $5,800; a full-time 31.2-case year at the weighted mean (~45 hours/case) is roughly 1,389 hours × $125 ≈ $174,000 gross before overhead (estimate).
- 99% of appointed appeals in the timekeeping sample had records under 1,500 pages, and 77% under 500 pages; tools that speed record review matter most in the 100–1,500-page band where review is 9–17 Delphi hours.

### Gaps
- The Addendum gives no explicit pages-per-hour assumption and no distribution of transcript lengths finer than the four buckets; Appendix D/E (detailed Delphi results) were not reviewed line-by-line.
- TIDC FY2015 Statewide Indigent Defense Caseload Report (tidc.tamu.edu/public.net/Reports/AttorneyCaseLoad.aspx) returned HTTP 503 during this session; not read.

## Key Question 2: County indigent defense plans and fee schedules — appeal, Anders, PDR, writ lines, caps, hourly rates, expense/technology language

### Takeaway
Appointed appellate pay in the ten counties ranges from $50–$125/hour (Tarrant) and $60–$85/hour out-of-court (Bexar) to $155/hour (Travis felony; Williamson felony capped at $7,500), with flat alternatives of $2,000–$3,500 per appeal (Collin, Dallas misdemeanor) and $1,500 (Montgomery misdemeanor); Harris district courts pay $125/hour (non-capital), $150 (capital/11.071) with no presumptive maximum since the 2/27/2023 schedule. Every plan tracks Art. 26.05(d) "reasonable and necessary expenses" language, but none of the plans or schedules mentions software, legal-research subscriptions, or AI tools as reimbursable; Bexar and El Paso expressly fold "computer research"/equipment into non-reimbursable overhead.

## Key Question 3: HB 1445 (89th Leg.) and Art. 26.05 — what changed; Art. 26.05(d) expense text

### Takeaway
HB 1445 (Acts 2025, 89th Leg., R.S., ch. 515, §1, effective September 1, 2025) amended only Art. 26.05(c) to let a managed-assigned-counsel program director's "designee" receive and approve/reduce fee vouchers; the 60-day appeal to the regional presiding judge and the 45-day commissioners-court payment deadline remain, and Art. 26.05(d) still limits mandatory expense reimbursement to "reasonable and necessary expenses, including expenses for investigation and for mental health and other experts."

### Cited Findings
- Source document: TIDC, "Texas Fair Defense Laws 2025–2027" (final-2025-fair-defense-laws.pdf), Art. 26.05 at pp. 46–48. — [TIDC Fair Defense Laws 2025](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/2ilf2z0x/final-2025-fair-defense-laws.pdf)
- Art. 26.05(a) (as compiled): counsel "other than an attorney with a public defender's office or an attorney employed by the office of capital and forensic writs, appointed to represent a defendant in a criminal proceeding, including a habeas corpus hearing, shall be paid a reasonable attorney's fee for performing the following services, based on the time and labor required, the complexity of the case, and the experience and ability of the appointed counsel: (1) time spent in court...; (2) reasonable and necessary time spent out of court on the case, supported by any documentation that the court requires; (3) preparation of an appellate brief and preparation and presentation of oral argument to a court of appeals or the Court of Criminal Appeals; and (4) preparation of a motion for rehearing." — [Fair Defense Laws 2025, p. 46](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/2ilf2z0x/final-2025-fair-defense-laws.pdf)
- Art. 26.05(b): payments "in accordance with a schedule of fees adopted by formal action of the judges of the county courts, statutory county courts, and district courts trying criminal cases in each county," with a copy to the commissioners court. — [p. 46](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/2ilf2z0x/final-2025-fair-defense-laws.pdf)
- Art. 26.05(c) (post-HB 1445 text): "Each fee schedule adopted shall state reasonable fixed rates or minimum and maximum hourly rates, taking into consideration reasonable and necessary overhead costs and the availability of qualified attorneys willing to accept the stated rates, and shall provide a form for the appointed counsel to itemize the types of services performed. No payment shall be made under this article until the form for itemizing the services performed is submitted to the judge presiding over the proceedings or, if the county operates a managed assigned counsel program under Article 26.047, to the director of the program or the director's designee, and until the judge or the director or director's designee, as applicable, approves the payment. If the judge or the director or director's designee disapproves the requested amount of payment, [they] shall make written findings stating the amount of payment that [they] approve[] and each reason for approving an amount different from the requested amount. An attorney whose request for payment is disapproved or is not otherwise acted on by the 60th day after the date the request for payment is submitted may appeal the disapproval or failure to act by filing a motion with the presiding judge of the administrative judicial region... Not later than the 45th day after the date an application for payment of a fee is submitted under this article, the commissioners court shall pay to the appointed counsel the amount that is approved by the presiding judge of the administrative judicial region and that is in accordance with the fee schedule for that county." — [p. 47](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/2ilf2z0x/final-2025-fair-defense-laws.pdf)
- TIDC "2025 Legislative Note: Subsection (c), amended by Acts 2025, 89th Leg., R.S., Ch. 515 (H.B. 1445), Sec. 1, eff. September 1, 2025, allows the director's designee to approve or reduce attorney fee vouchers." — [p. 48](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/2ilf2z0x/final-2025-fair-defense-laws.pdf)
- Art. 26.05(d): "A counsel in a non-capital case, other than an attorney with a public defender's office, appointed to represent a defendant under this code shall be reimbursed for reasonable and necessary expenses, including expenses for investigation and for mental health and other experts. Expenses incurred with prior court approval shall be reimbursed in the same manner provided for capital cases by Articles 26.052(f) and (g), and expenses incurred without prior court approval shall be reimbursed in the manner provided for capital cases by Article 26.052(h)." Art. 26.05(h): investigation/expert reimbursement may be paid directly to a licensed investigator or expert. — [p. 47](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/2ilf2z0x/final-2025-fair-defense-laws.pdf)
- Art. 26.052(h) (capital, incorporated by reference): "Counsel may incur expenses without prior approval of the court. On presentation of a claim for reimbursement, the court shall order reimbursement of counsel for the expenses, if the expenses are reasonably necessary and reasonably incurred." — [Fair Defense Laws 2025, p. 50](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/2ilf2z0x/final-2025-fair-defense-laws.pdf)
- TIDC commentary on fee schedules: "court's determination of a reasonable attorney's fee for appointed counsel is limited by the fixed or minimum and maximum hourly rates adopted by formal action of the judges within a county. In re State ex rel. Wice v. Fifth Judicial Dist. Court of Appeals, 581 S.W.3d 189, 192 (Tex. Crim. App. 2018)." — [p. 48](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/2ilf2z0x/final-2025-fair-defense-laws.pdf)
- Search snippets, not fetched (capitol.texas.gov blocked): the Senate committee analysis says the bill addresses "a substantial administrative burden for the directors" of MAC programs in large counties by allowing "the director [to] establish a designee"; House passed April 29, 2025 (143–1–1), Senate May 25, 2025 (31–0); "The amendment applies only to expenses incurred by an attorney on or after the effective date of this Act." — [HB 1445 enrolled text (blocked for this session)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/HB01445F.htm); [HB 1445 analysis (blocked)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/analysis/html/HB01445E.htm)

### Inferences
- HB 1445 changed who signs the voucher in MAC counties (Harris misdemeanor, Bexar, Travis/CAPDS, Collin MHMC), not rates or the payment clock; the "45 days from application" payment duty and the 60-day appeal remain the economic timing constraints for appointed appellate counsel.

## Key Question 4: Article 11.071 §2A compensation — rates, $25,000 cap, who sets rates; RPDO and OCFW budgets

### Takeaway
Art. 11.071 sets no hourly rate: the convicting court "shall reasonably compensate" appointed (non-OCFW) writ counsel, the state reimburses the county up to $25,000 per application for fees plus investigative expenses, and anything above that is the county's obligation; the regional presiding judges keep the appointment lists (Gov't Code §78.056) but counties' own schedules set the rate (e.g., Harris $150/hr; Bexar $165/$225). RPDO reported 16 attorney positions with 8 vacant in late 2025; OCFW's appropriation documents were unreachable.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 11.071 §2(e)/(f) (compiled): where OCFW does not or cannot accept, "the convicting court shall appoint counsel from a list of competent counsel maintained by the presiding judges of the administrative judicial regions under Section 78.056, Government Code. The convicting court shall reasonably compensate as provided by Section 2A an attorney appointed under this section, other than an attorney employed by the office of capital and forensic writs... An attorney appointed under this section who is employed by the office of capital and forensic writs shall be compensated in accordance with Subchapter B, Chapter 78, Government Code." — [TIDC Fair Defense Laws 2025, pp. 12–13](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/2ilf2z0x/final-2025-fair-defense-laws.pdf)
- Art. 11.071 §2A(a): "The state shall reimburse a county for compensation of counsel under Section 2, other than for compensation of counsel employed by the office of capital and forensic writs, and for payment of expenses under Section 3, regardless of whether counsel is employed by the office of capital and forensic writs. The total amount of reimbursement to which a county is entitled under this section for an application under this article may not exceed $25,000. Compensation and expenses in excess of the $25,000 reimbursement provided by the state are the obligation of the county." §2A(b): the convicting court certifies the amount to the comptroller, who issues a warrant "not to exceed $25,000." §2A(c): the cap "does not prohibit a county from compensating counsel and reimbursing expenses in an amount that is in excess." §2A(d): same $25,000 limit for CCA appointments under prior law. — [pp. 13](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/2ilf2z0x/final-2025-fair-defense-laws.pdf)
- Art. 11.071 §3: counsel "shall investigate expeditiously, before and after the appellate record is filed"; not later than 30 days before filing, counsel "may file with the convicting court an ex parte, verified, and confidential request for prepayment of expenses, including expert fees"; §3(d) "Counsel may incur expenses for habeas corpus investigation, including expenses for experts, without prior approval... the convicting court shall order reimbursement of counsel for expenses, if the expenses are reasonably necessary and reasonably incurred." §4(a): application due 180 days after appointment or 45 days after the State's brief on direct appeal, whichever is later. — [pp. 13–14](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/2ilf2z0x/final-2025-fair-defense-laws.pdf)
- Art. 11.071 §2(d): after denial, appointed counsel must within 15 days "move for the appointment of counsel in federal habeas review under 18 U.S.C. Section 3599." — [p. 13](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/2ilf2z0x/final-2025-fair-defense-laws.pdf)

Eight of those are vacant at this time. ## Key Question 5: State Bar of Texas Performance Guidelines for Non-Capital Criminal Defense Representation — appellate duties

### Takeaway
The 2011 Guidelines (Texas Bar Journal, Vol. 74, No. 7, July 2011, pp. 634–637 for Guidelines 8.7–9.8) make appellate counsel's named duties: immediately confirm deadlines with the trial court and contact trial counsel; review the clerk's and reporter's records for completeness and object to errors; "fully review the appellate record for all reviewable errors, prepare a well researched and drafted appellate brief"; consider reply briefs/rehearing; and, after an adverse decision, advise the client "in writing by certified mail" of the PDR right (and later the certiorari right), explaining that there is no right to appointed counsel for a PDR but counsel may be appointed on request. The text does not use the word "Anders" and does not cite Ex parte Wilson by name.

### Cited Findings
- Source: Texas Bar Journal reprint PDF (6 pages, "www.texasbar.com/tbj Vol. 74, No. 7 • Texas Bar Journal 635–637," July 2011), linked by TIDC as the official text. — [Performance Guidelines (Texas Bar Journal reprint)](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Texas_Bar_Journal&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=14703)
- Guideline 9.1 (Duties of Defense Counsel in Post-Trial Proceedings): "counsel must continue representation of the client until counsel has been formally granted permission to withdraw... Counsel shall continue to represent the client until appeals are exhausted, including in motion for new trial proceedings"; before withdrawing, notify the court of an indigency affidavit and "assist the client in filing a written notice of appeal and in requesting prompt appointment of post-trial counsel." — [Guidelines p. 635](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Texas_Bar_Journal&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=14703)
- Guideline 9.2: post-trial counsel must "be familiar with the Rules of Appellate Procedure and any local rules of the courts of appeal." Guideline 9.3 (Motion for New Trial): lists timing, effect on notice of appeal, grounds, sworn-affidavit requirement, TRAP 21.6 presentment, overruling by operation of law, written findings if granted; grounds to consider include denial of counsel, defective charge, jury misconduct, suppression of evidence, continuance denial, sufficiency, interest of justice. — [pp. 635–636](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Texas_Bar_Journal&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=14703)
- Guideline 9.4 (Protecting the Right to Appeal): inform client of right to appeal judgment and sentence; if trial counsel will not handle the appeal, withdraw only after assisting with notice of appeal, MNT, and "assisting the client in requesting prompt appointment of appellate counsel"; cooperate with appellate counsel including on bail pending appeal. — [p. 636](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Texas_Bar_Journal&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=14703)
- Guideline 9.5 (Direct Appeal), verbatim duties: "A. ...familiar with the procedures applicable to an appeal, including the rules specifying the time period for filing an appeal and the requirements for submission of the clerk's and reporter's records. B. ...immediately consult with the trial court to ascertain relevant information concerning the perfection of the appeal and relevant filing deadlines... C. ...inform the client of any opportunity that may exist to be released on bail pending the disposition of the appeal... D. Counsel should immediately contact trial counsel to obtain background information on the client, information on the nature of the issues presented, and to determine whether filing a motion for new trial... is necessary... E. Retained counsel should... immediately inform the court... by filing the appropriate designation of counsel... and all counsel, both retained and appointed, must submit the proper designations of the clerk's and reporter's records... F. Counsel must review the clerk's and reporter's records to determine whether they are true, correct and complete in all respects. If errors or omissions are found, objections to the record must be immediately filed... G. Counsel should fully review the appellate record for all reviewable errors, prepare a well researched and drafted appellate brief, file the brief in a timely manner and in accordance with all other requirements in the Rules of Appellate Procedure and any local rules, and notify the court of counsel's desire to present oral argument in the case, when appropriate. H. Counsel should consider preparing and filing a reply brief or a motion for rehearing if, under the circumstances, such is needed or required..." — [p. 636](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Texas_Bar_Journal&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=14703)
- Guideline 9.6 (Right to File a PDR): "In the event that the intermediate appellate court's decision is unfavorable to the client, counsel must advise the client in writing by certified mail of the client's right to file a petition for discretionary review and the action that must be taken to properly file such a petition," explaining that (A) CCA review is discretionary; "(B) If the client is indigent, the client does not have the right to appointed counsel for the purpose of filing a petition for discretionary review but that, upon request, counsel may be appointed for this purpose; and (C) If the client is indigent and if the petition for discretionary review is granted, the client does have the right to court-appointed counsel for further proceedings on the merits." — [p. 636](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Texas_Bar_Journal&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=14703)
- Guideline 9.7 (PDR): familiarity with time limits, page limits and expansion for good cause; consider the TRAP reasons for review (conflict among courts of appeals, important unsettled question, conflict with CCA/SCOTUS, statute held unconstitutional or misconstrued, justices disagreed, departure from usual course); "fully review the appellate opinion for all reviewable errors, prepare a well researched and drafted petition"; brief on the merits if granted; reply brief; motion for rehearing; TRAP 79.2 rehearing after summary refusal only on "substantial intervening circumstances." Guideline 9.8 (Certiorari): advise in writing by certified mail of the certiorari right; no right to appointed counsel for the petition. — [pp. 636–637](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Texas_Bar_Journal&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=14703)
- The 2016 TIDC Appellate Addendum cites these as "STATE BAR OF TEX., PERFORMANCE GUIDELINES FOR NON-CAPITAL CRIMINAL DEFENSE REPRESENTATION (2011)" at the same texasbar.com URL. — [Addendum fn. 4](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)

### Inferences
- The guidelines' appellate workflow (record-integrity check → full record review for "all reviewable errors" → brief → reply/rehearing → certified-mail PDR letter) maps directly onto the Delphi task categories (Initiation, Record Review, Appellate Briefs, Oral Argument, PDR) used in the 2016 caseload study.
- The certified-mail PDR-advice duty in 9.6 is the practical embodiment of the Ex parte Wilson line of cases, but the guideline text itself does not cite the case.

### Gaps
- No 2021 "updated" edition of the Performance Guidelines was located on texasbar.com in this pass; the only text read is the July 2011 Texas Bar Journal reprint. Whether a 2021 revision changed Guidelines 9.x is unverified.
