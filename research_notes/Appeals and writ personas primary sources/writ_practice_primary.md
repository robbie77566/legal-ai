## Key Question 1: OCFW capacity — headcount, caseload, 11.071/11.073 counts, attrition, budget

### Takeaway
As of FY2025 (year ending Aug. 31, 2025) OCFW reports 14 attorneys, 10 mitigation specialists, 6 paralegals and 2 accountants, represents "over 70%" of death-sentenced persons in initial Article 11.071 proceedings, and represents 8 forensic-writ (11.073-type) clients out of "over 70" Forensic Science Commission referrals since 2020 — up from 12 attorneys/7 mitigation specialists/3 paralegals in FY2022–FY2024. OCFW's own reports, LARs, attrition figures and budget on ocfw.texas.gov were unreachable; the only budget figures found are secondary.

## Key Question 2: CCA practice — the "white card," remand vs. denial without written order, Elizondo, Robbins, out-of-time appeal/PDR

### Takeaway
The CCA's own FY2025 table shows that of 2,341 non-capital 11.07 dispositions, 981 (42%) were denials "without written order" (470 on the Court's own review, 511 on trial-court findings), 183 (8%) were remands for a hearing or affidavits, 411 (18%) were §4 dismissals and 270 (12%) were non-compliance dismissals; only 103 (4%) were filed and set. The "white card" is the practitioner's name for the CCA's card-form notice of a summary disposition — above all the "non-compliant" dismissal under TRAP 73.2 — rather than a term used in any CCA document found.

### Cited Findings
**CCA process and dispositions (practice description)**
- "When the writ application is received at the Court of Criminal Appeals, it is assigned a cause number and randomly assigned to one of seven staff lawyers who work exclusively on extraordinary writs. The staff lawyer writes a memorandum summarizing the claims, evidence, and arguments ... and making a recommendation to the Court. The case is randomly assigned to a judge who then becomes responsible for shepherding the case through the Court." In FY2021 the Court received 3,325 new 11.07 applications and 533 other original proceedings, "the lowest number of 11.07 applications since 2001." — [Falkenberg, Article 11.07 Writs of Habeas Corpus (Harris County PD, rev. May 2023), p. 12](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)
- "Writ applications are routinely remanded for findings and resolution when they allege facts, which, if true, could entitle the applicant to relief. Writ applications that do not 'allege facts' are routinely denied without written order." — [Falkenberg, pp. 12–13](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)
- "The Court of Criminal Appeals will vote to deny, dismiss, remand, hold, file and set, or grant a writ application. A dismissal is a decision unrelated to the merits of the claims, while a denial is a final adjudication of the issues raised and will be based either on the trial court's findings and the Court's independent review of the case or on the Court's own review. See Ex parte Grigsby, 137 S.W.3d 673, 674 (Tex. Crim. App. 2004)." Remand orders "[m]ost of the time ... provide[] 90 days for the habeas court to investigate and make findings." Filing and setting "generally means the case is being set for a written opinion." — [Falkenberg, p. 13](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)
- No rehearing: TRAP 79.2(d) bars a motion for rehearing of an order denying or dismissing an 11.07/11.071 application; "if you style your pleading as a motion or suggestion that the Court reconsider on its own motion, the motion will get a ruling on the merits." — [Falkenberg, p. 14](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)
- Non-compliance: "The Court of Criminal Appeals routinely dismisses writ applications that do not conform to these rules as non-compliant. Tex. R. App. P. 73.2 ... Failure to certify to the word count in the memorandum and exceeding the two page per ground limit will cause a writ application to be peremptorily dismissed, as will not using the form altogether." The Court overlooks non-compliance where the trial court recommends an agreed grant (Ex parte Golden, 991 S.W.2d 859, 862 (Tex. Crim. App. 1999)). — [Falkenberg, p. 7](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)
- TRAP 73.2 (text): "The Court of Criminal Appeals may dismiss an application that does not comply with these rules." TRAP 73.6: "The Court may deny relief based upon its own review of the application or may issue such other instructions or orders as may be appropriate." — [Texas Rules of Appellate Procedure (eff. through Feb. 6, 2026), pp. 92–94](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)
- Form instructions: "Failure to follow these instructions may cause your entire application to be dismissed"; "This application form must be verified ... or it may be dismissed for noncompliance." — [CCA Art. 11.07 form and instructions (rev. 2018)](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1442926/1107-form-rev-2018.pdf)

**Ex parte Elizondo (actual innocence)**
- Ex parte Elizondo, 947 S.W.2d 202 (Tex. Crim. App. 1996), held that "the incarceration of an innocent person is as much a violation of the Due Process Clause as is the execution of such a person," so bare actual-innocence claims "are cognizable by this Court in a postconviction habeas corpus proceeding whether the punishment assessed is death or confinement" (id. at 205). The standard: "the petitioner must show by clear and convincing evidence that no reasonable juror would have convicted him in light of the new evidence" (id. at 209); the court "must necessarily weigh such exculpatory evidence against the evidence adduced at trial" (id. at 206). — [Falkenberg, pp. 27–28](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf); [Mallet concurrence, WR-90,980-01/02 (July 1, 2020), p. 2](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1448324/wr-90980-0102-mallet-concur_br-july_1_2020.pdf) ("an applicant must prove by clear and convincing evidence that no reasonable juror would have convicted him based on the newly discovered evidence")
- Related: a guilty plea does not bar an Elizondo claim (Ex parte Tuley, 109 S.W.3d 388 (Tex. Crim. App. 2002)); evidence that "merely 'muddies the waters'" (multiple recantations) is insufficient (Ex parte Harleston, 431 S.W.3d 67, 89 (Tex. Crim. App. 2014)); innocence claims are "fact and conduct-centric" (Ex parte Fournier, 473 S.W.3d 789 (Tex. Crim. App. 2015)). — [Falkenberg, pp. 28–29](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)

**Ex parte Robbins (Article 11.073)**
- Robbins I: Ex parte Robbins, 360 S.W.3d 446 (Tex. Crim. App. 2011), cert. denied (May 14, 2012) — majority denied relief because Robbins "failed to prove that the new evidence unquestionably establishes his innocence" and had no "due process right to have a jury hear [Dr.] Moore's re-evaluation," over Judge Cochran's concurrence and Judge Alcala's false-testimony dissent. — [Udashen, Legal Issues and Standards in Texas Innocence Cases (slides hosted on txcourts.gov)](https://txcourts.gov/media/1203204/Udashe_IPOT-PowerPoint-on-Legal-Issues1-and-Standards-in-Texas-Innocence-Cases-2.pdf)
- Robbins II (2014): Ex parte Robbins, 478 S.W.3d 678 (Tex. Crim. App. 2014) — "The Court has held that a medical examiner who no longer stood by a prior opinion and testimony about cause of death constituted 'scientific knowledge' under the statute," and Article 11.073 "provides a new legal basis for false evidence claims that were rejected prior to its enactment 'in the small number of cases where the applicant can show by the preponderance of the evidence that he or she would not have been convicted if the newly available scientific evidence had been presented at trial.'" (478 S.W.3d at 690). The 2015 amendment to 11.073(d) ("a testifying expert's scientific knowledge") "codified the holding." — [Falkenberg, p. 27 & n.22](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf); [Udashen slides](https://txcourts.gov/media/1203204/Udashe_IPOT-PowerPoint-on-Legal-Issues1-and-Standards-in-Texas-Innocence-Cases-2.pdf) ("Robbins case reconsidered under Art. 11.073 and relief granted. Majority held Medical Examiner's reconsideration of her opinion was new scientific evidence that contradicted scientific evidence relied upon by the state at trial.")
- **[snippet]** Robbins III (Jan. 27, 2016, on rehearing; reported at 560 S.W.3d 130): the Court again granted relief, with three concurrences; the case had been set for oral argument June 3, 2015 (WR-73,484-02, Montgomery County). The 2016 opinion itself could not be retrieved. — [CCA submission schedule 6-3-2015](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/978887/6-3-2015-submission-list.pdf); [Texas Tech Law Review case update (blocked)](https://texastechlawreview.org/court-of-criminal-appeals-update-ex-parte-robbins)
- Statutory text as quoted in practice materials: 11.073 applies to "relevant scientific evidence that: (1) was not available to be offered by a convicted person at the convicted person's trial; or (2) contradicts scientific evidence relied on by the state at trial"; relief requires that "had the scientific evidence been presented at trial, on the preponderance of the evidence the person would not have been convicted"; and 11.073(d) requires the court to "consider whether the field of scientific knowledge, a testifying expert's scientific knowledge, or a scientific method on which the scientific evidence is based has changed since" trial or a prior writ. 11.073 does not reach punishment-phase evidence (Ex parte White, 506 S.W.3d 39 (Tex. Crim. App. 2016)). — [Udashen slides](https://txcourts.gov/media/1203204/Udashe_IPOT-PowerPoint-on-Legal-Issues1-and-Standards-in-Texas-Innocence-Cases-2.pdf); [Falkenberg, pp. 27–28](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)

**Out-of-time appeal and out-of-time PDR**
- Out-of-time PDR: "Appellate counsel has a duty to timely inform an appellant of the appellate decision and of the right to pursue discretionary review pro se. Ex parte Crow, 180 S.W.3d 135 (Tex. Crim. App. 2005); Ex parte Wilson, 956 S.W.2d 25 (Tex. Crim. App. 1997). Informing the appellant the conviction was affirmed on appeal without informing of the right to pursue a pro-se petition for discretionary review (PDR) is insufficient. Ex parte Florentino, 206 S.W.3d 124 (Tex. Crim. App. 2006). This duty also extends to lawyers who filed an Anders brief. Ex parte Owens, 206 S.W.3d 670 (Tex. Crim. App. 2006)." Where counsel was not ineffective but a "breakdown in the system" cost the PDR, "due process requires that the appellant be permitted to exercise his statutory right to file a PDR. Ex parte Riley, 193 S.W.3d 900 (Tex. Crim. App. 2006). This holding is routinely applied to out-of-time appeal situations as well." — [Falkenberg, p. 24](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)
- TRAP 48.4 requires appellate counsel, within five days of the opinion, to send the client the opinion and notice of the right to file a pro se PDR by certified mail, return receipt requested, and certify compliance to the court of appeals; "The Rule has been in place for well over 15 years, but the Court still sees valid out-of-time PDR claims based on counsel errors on a weekly basis. The Court has not yet said that failure to comply with Rule 48.4 is prima facie evidence of attorney error." — [Falkenberg, p. 25](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf); rule text at [TRAP 48.4](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)
- Out-of-time appeal: counsel must perfect appeal on request; "[t]hat retained counsel did not intend to handle the resultant appeal does not justify his failing to assist his allegedly indigent client in giving notice of appeal" (Ex parte Axel, 757 S.W.2d 369, 374 (Tex. Crim. App. 1988)); Garza v. Idaho, 139 S. Ct. 738 (2019) presumes prejudice when counsel ignores a request to appeal despite a waiver, but Ex parte Castillo, 664 S.W.3d 833 (Tex. Crim. App. 2022) found no prejudice where the certification showed no right to appeal at all. — [Falkenberg, pp. 23–24](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)
- Form of relief (read directly): in Ex parte Thompson, WR-91,220-01 (Tex. Crim. App. June 17, 2020) (per curiam, not published), "the trial court has found that counsel failed to timely file a notice of appeal through no fault of either counsel or Applicant. Relief is granted. Ex parte Riley ... Within ten days from the date of this Court's mandate, the trial court shall determine whether Applicant is indigent ... All deadlines shall be calculated as if Applicant was sentenced on the date of this Court's mandate. Should Applicant decide to appeal, he must file a written notice of appeal in the trial court within thirty days from the date of this Court's mandate." — [Ex parte Thompson](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1447603/wr-91-220-01-thompson-opinion-061720.pdf)
- Section 4 interaction: a writ seeking only an out-of-time appeal or PDR does not "challenge the conviction," so it does not trigger the §4 subsequent-writ bar (Ex parte McPherson, 32 S.W.3d 860, 861 (Tex. Crim. App. 2000)); when an out-of-time appeal is granted, other claims in the writ are dismissed under Ex parte Torres, 943 S.W.2d 469 (Tex. Crim. App. 1997). — [Falkenberg, pp. 15–16](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)
- Volume: the CCA granted out-of-time appeals 23/24/21 times and out-of-time PDRs 22/16/27 times in FY2023/FY2024/FY2025. — [CCA Activity Reports FY23–FY25](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1462062/cca-annual-reports-fy25.pdf)

### Inferences
- Denials without written order (on the Court's own review plus on trial-court findings) have run at 41–46% of all 11.07 dispositions in FY2023–FY2025, versus remands at 8–11% and grants (all types, filed-and-set) at 2–4%. Remand share fell from 11% (FY2020–21) to 8% (FY2025).
- "White card" appears to be practitioner shorthand for the CCA's card-form notice of a summary disposition (the Court's dismissal/denial orders are not opinions and are not published), and the UT CLE title ties it specifically to the non-compliance dismissal — which affected 213–299 applications a year in FY2020–FY2025. The exact form of the notice was not confirmed in any CCA-published document.
- For an out-of-time PDR, counsel must plead and prove (usually by applicant affidavit and the appellate record/48.4 certification) that appellate counsel did not timely tell the client of the affirmance *and* of the right to file a pro se PDR; for an out-of-time appeal, that the client timely wanted to appeal and counsel (or the system) failed to perfect it.

## Key Question 3: AEDPA for Texas convictions — finality, statutory tolling, §4-barred writs, equitable tolling

### Takeaway
The Fifth Circuit's rules, as confirmed by the U.S. Supreme Court in Gonzalez v. Thaler, are: no PDR → final 30 days after the court of appeals' judgment (Roberts v. Cockrell); PDR refused → final when the 90-day certiorari period ends or when cert is denied (Roberts; Erickson); the state mandate date is irrelevant. A state application filed before the conviction is final (before the mandate) is not "properly filed" and tolls nothing (Larry v. Dretke); a subsequent writ dismissed under §4 is still "properly filed" and tolls (Villegas v. Johnson); the 90-day cert window after a state-habeas denial does not toll (Ott v. Johnson). Equitable tolling follows Holland's two-part test but is reserved for "rare and exceptional circumstances." All of this section is snippet-sourced because every federal case-law host was blocked.

### Cited Findings
- Finality with no PDR: "A petitioner may seek review in the CCA by filing a PDR within thirty days after the intermediate court renders judgment. TEX. R. APP. P. 68.1, 68.2(a). If he elects not to file a PDR, his conviction becomes final under AEDPA at the end of the thirty-day period—that is, 'when the time for seeking further direct review expired.' Roberts v. Cockrell, 319 F.3d 690, 694-95 (5th Cir. 2003)." Roberts held "the issuance of a mandate by a state court is irrelevant to determining when a judgment becomes 'final' for the purposes of § 2244(d)(1)(A)." **[snippet]** — [Fifth Circuit opinions quoting Roberts (blocked for fetch)](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/pub/16/16-20651-CV0.pdf); [10-20095 (unpub.)](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/unpub/10/10-20095.0.wpd.pdf)
- TRAP 68.2(a) (read directly): "The petition must be filed within 30 days after either the day the court of appeals' judgment was rendered or the day the last timely motion for rehearing or timely motion for en banc reconsideration was overruled by the court of appeals." — [TRAP (Feb. 6, 2026), p. 88](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)
- Supreme Court confirmation: Gonzalez v. Thaler, 565 U.S. 134 (2012) — for a state prisoner "who does not seek review in that state's highest court, the judgment becomes 'final' ... on the date that the time for seeking such review expires," "not when a mandate is issued"; the Court rejected the argument that the petition was timely because it was filed within a year of when the time for seeking certiorari expired. Facts: Texas court of appeals affirmed July 12, 2006; PDR deadline Aug. 11, 2006 passed; federal petition Jan. 24, 2008 was untimely. **[snippet]** — [Gonzalez v. Thaler (Justia, blocked)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/us/565/10-895); [Fifth Circuit opinion in same case](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/pub/08/08-10871-CV0.wpd.pdf)
- Finality with PDR, no cert: "Because 'direct review includes a petition for certiorari to the Supreme Court[,] ... the conclusion of direct review is when the Supreme Court either rejects the petition for certiorari or rules on its merits'"; "That Erickson did not actually file a petition for certiorari is insignificant. See Roberts, 319 F.3d at 694." Erickson argued his conviction became final "90 days after the TCCA refused his PDR on April 16, 2014," i.e., July 16, 2014. **[snippet]** — [Fifth Circuit 2018 published opinion (blocked)](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/pub/16/16-20651-CV0.pdf)
- Statutory tolling, "properly filed": Larry v. Dretke, 361 F.3d 890 (5th Cir. 2004) — "to determine whether an application is properly filed, we look to procedural rules governing the court's jurisdiction to consider the application, not rules governing whether it can consider any particular claim" (at 893); because under Ex parte Johnson, 12 S.W.3d 472 (Tex. Crim. App. 2000) the CCA lacks jurisdiction over an 11.07 application until the conviction is final (mandate issued), an application filed before the mandate "was not properly filed" and did not toll (at 894–95); the federal petition was time-barred. **[snippet]** — [Fifth Circuit cases applying Larry (blocked)](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/unpub/06/06-10130.0.wpd.pdf); [Casemine commentary (blocked)](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/proper-filing-of-state-habeas-applications-under-aedpa:-insights-from-larry-iii-v.-dretke/view)
- The state-side rule Larry relies on, read directly: "the Court held that it does not have jurisdiction ... until the conviction under attack becomes final. Ex parte Johnson, 12 S.W.3d 472, 473 (Tex. Crim. App. 2000). ... If the case is pending on direct appeal or discretionary review, the conviction is not final and will not be final until the appellate mandate issues." — [Falkenberg, p. 4](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)
- §4-dismissed subsequent writ: Villegas v. Johnson, 184 F.3d 467 (5th Cir. 1999) — "a post-conviction application dismissed as successive or an abuse of the writ under art. 11.07 § 4, which was filed in compliance with the state's procedural requirements, is 'properly filed' under section 2244(d)(2)"; "properly filed" means the application "conforms with a state's applicable procedural filing requirements," consistent with Artuz v. Bennett, 531 U.S. 4 (2000). **[snippet]** — [Fifth Circuit opinions citing Villegas (blocked)](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/pub/98/98-10298.CV0.wpd.pdf); [Casemine commentary](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/feliz-talaz-villegas-v.-johnson%3A-aedpa-tolling-and-successive-state-habeas-petitions/view)
- No tolling for the cert window after state habeas: Ott v. Johnson, 192 F.3d 510 (5th Cir. 1999) rejected the argument "that the ninety days in which a state habeas petitioner may petition the United States Supreme Court for a writ of certiorari, even if no petition for certiorari is filed, should toll the limitations period"; the limitations period there expired Sept. 19, 1997, one day after the CCA denied the state application. **[snippet]** — [Ott v. Johnson (blocked)](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/pub/98/98-41211.CV0.wpd.pdf)
- Equitable tolling: Fifth Circuit applies Holland v. Florida's test — "(1) that he has been pursuing his rights diligently, and (2) that some extraordinary circumstance stood in his way and prevented timely filing" — but "equitable tolling is warranted in only 'rare and exceptional circumstances.' Davis v. Johnson, 158 F.3d 806, 811 (5th Cir. 1998)"; petitioners "need not pursue their rights with 'maximum feasible diligence'" but must show "reasonable diligence"; refusal to toll is reviewed for abuse of discretion. Jackson v. Davis, 933 F.3d 408 (5th Cir. 2019) reversed a time-bar where the prisoner never received notice of the CCA's denial and kept inquiring; Jones v. Lumpkin (5th Cir. 2022) affirmed denial where delays were self-caused. **[snippet]** — [5th Cir. 18-10526 (blocked)](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/pub/18/18-10526-CV0.pdf); [19-10079](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/19/19-10079-CV0.pdf); [Casemine on Jackson v. Davis](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/expanding-equitable-tolling-in-federal-habeas-corpus:-jackson-v.-davis/view)
- Practice warning on "shell writs": some jurisdictions require "a 'shell writ' containing only bare allegations before appointing counsel," and applicants file early placeholders "to avoid missing AEDPA deadlines for federal court"; the CCA "no longer grants motions to dismiss writ applications without question. Ex parte Speckman, 537 S.W.3d 49 (Tex. Crim. App. 2017)." — [Falkenberg, p. 11](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)

### Inferences
- Combining the state and federal rules: the AEDPA year starts (no PDR) on day 31 after the court-of-appeals judgment, but the 11.07 application cannot be properly filed until the court-of-appeals mandate issues (typically later), so the first weeks of the federal year run untolled; an 11.07 filed before the mandate wastes the filing and tolls nothing (Larry).
- A §4 dismissal costs the applicant the merits but not AEDPA tolling (Villegas), so a procedurally compliant subsequent writ still pauses the federal clock while pending.

### Gaps
- No Fifth Circuit or Supreme Court opinion was read in full (ca5.uscourts.gov, justia, courtlistener, scholar, findlaw and supremecourt.gov all blocked); pin cites come from later opinions quoting the cases. The CCA's position on whether an 11.07 "pending" period includes the time between the trial court and the CCA (always yes in practice) was not separately sourced.

## Key Question 4: Ethics — Opinion 705 (generative AI) and the former client's right to the trial file (Opinions 657/570/627)

### Takeaway
Opinion 705 (Feb. 2025), as summarized by the State Bar's own blog and reprinted in the April 2025 Texas Bar Journal at p. 310, imposes four duties on Texas lawyers using generative AI: competence (Rule 1.01, understand the tool and verify output), confidentiality (Rule 1.05, vet tools before inputting client information and train staff), verification/supervision (unverified output risks candor and fairness violations), and billing (no billing for AI-saved time; direct AI costs passable only by prior agreement). For the trial file, Opinion 657 (May 2016) says the file belongs to the former criminal client, the lawyer bears the cost of any copy the lawyer keeps, but Art. 39.14(a) discovery (other than the client's own statement) may not be handed over and 39.14(f)-protected information must be redacted. The opinions' own text was unreachable.

### Cited Findings
**Opinion 705**
- "In February 2025, the Professional Ethics Committee for the State Bar of Texas issued Opinion 705, addressing the ethical implications surrounding the use of generative artificial intelligence (AI) by Texas attorneys. This opinion was requested by the Taskforce for Responsible AI in the Law (TRAIL), an initiative started by State Bar Immediate Past President Cindy Tisdale." — [Texas Bar Blog, "Ethics opinion offers principles for lawyers' ethical use of AI" (Apr. 2025, Joshua Weaver)](https://blog.texasbar.com/2025/04/articles/ethics/ethics-opinion-offers-principles-for-lawyers-ethical-use-of-ai/)
- Obligation #1 (competence): "Central to the attorney's obligations with respect to AI is the duty of competence, as outlined in Rule 1.01 ... attorneys cannot blindly rely on generative AI outputs but must critically assess and verify the accuracy of generated content." — [Texas Bar Blog](https://blog.texasbar.com/2025/04/articles/ethics/ethics-opinion-offers-principles-for-lawyers-ethical-use-of-ai/)
- Obligation #2 (confidentiality): "The opinion warns of the risks associated with inputting sensitive client details into AI systems, particularly those that may inadvertently share information with third parties. To mitigate these risks, Opinion 705 recommends thorough vetting of generative AI tools for confidentiality safeguards and suggests training staff to ensure compliance with confidentiality rules." — [Texas Bar Blog](https://blog.texasbar.com/2025/04/articles/ethics/ethics-opinion-offers-principles-for-lawyers-ethical-use-of-ai/)
- Obligation #3 (verification/supervision): "Lawyers must independently verify any information generated by AI ... Opinion 705 states that using AI-generated content without proper verification could expose attorneys to potential violations of rules related to fairness, honesty, and candor to the court, emphasizing the importance of responsible supervision." — [Texas Bar Blog](https://blog.texasbar.com/2025/04/articles/ethics/ethics-opinion-offers-principles-for-lawyers-ethical-use-of-ai/)

- For a writ lawyer, the practical consequence of Opinion 657 is that trial counsel's file will arrive with the State's 39.14 discovery stripped or redacted; the writ lawyer must obtain that discovery anew from the prosecutor (subject to the Morton Act's offense-date limit, Question 8) rather than from predecessor counsel.
- Opinion 705's confidentiality duty is tool-vetting, not a consent requirement; nothing in the State Bar's own summary mentions client consent.

## Key Question 6: Transcripts for indigent 11.07 applicants — Art. 11.07 §3(d), TRAP 20.2, case law, court-reporter rates

### Takeaway
TRAP 20.2 gives an indigent *appellant* a free appellate record only "[w]ithin the time for perfecting the appeal"; there is no parallel free-record right for an 11.07 applicant, and Texas and Fifth Circuit cases deny a free transcript for collateral attack absent a non-frivolous claim and specific need (Escobar v. State; Smith v. Beto). Art. 11.07 §3(d) lets the habeas court order forensic testing, affidavits and hearings and requires hearing transcripts within 15 days. Gov't Code §52.047 sets no statewide per-page rate (fee is set by the judge if disputed; extra copies capped at one-third of the original rate); the one published local indigent rate found is Tarrant County's $6.00/page (original plus two copies) effective Dec. 15, 2025.

### Cited Findings
- TRAP 20.2 (read directly): "Within the time for perfecting the appeal, an appellant who is unable to pay for the appellate record may, by motion and affidavit, ask the trial court to have the appellate record furnished without charge. If after hearing the motion the court finds that the appellant cannot pay or give security for the appellate record, the court must order the reporter to transcribe the proceedings. When the court certifies that the appellate record has been furnished to the appellant, the reporter must be paid from the general funds of the county in which the offense was committed, in the amount set by the trial court." — [TRAP (Feb. 6, 2026), p. 25](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)
- Art. 11.07 §3(d) as quoted in practice materials: to resolve designated issues "the court may order affidavits, depositions, interrogatories, additional forensic testing, and hearings, as well as using personal recollection"; "it shall be the duty of the reporter who is designated to transcribe a hearing ... to prepare a transcript within 15 days of its conclusion," and "immediately transmit the transcript to the clerk of the convicting court." "Writ records regularly reach the Court without transcripts from the hearing or any indication that a live hearing occurred." — [Falkenberg, p. 10](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)
- Counsel for indigent applicants: "When the trial court concludes that the interests of justice require representation, an eligible indigent defendant is entitled to have the trial court appoint an attorney ... Tex. Code Crim. Proc. arts. 1.051(d)(3), 26.04(c). The Court of Criminal Appeals typically orders appointment of counsel to indigent applicants when it remands a case specifically for a live hearing"; Art. 11.074 requires appointment when the State represents that the applicant "is not guilty, is guilty of only a lesser offense, or was convicted or sentenced under a law that has been found unconstitutional." No constitutional right to habeas counsel (Pennsylvania v. Finley; Ex parte Mines, 26 S.W.3d 910, 913 (Tex. Crim. App. 2000)). Filing fees are prohibited (Art. 11.051). — [Falkenberg, pp. 6, 9–10](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)
- **[snippet]** Free record for habeas: "an indigent criminal defendant is not entitled – either as a matter of equal protection or of due process – to obtain a free record in order to assist in preparation of a petition for writ of habeas corpus absent a showing that the habeas corpus action is not frivolous and there is a specific need for the trial records which are sought. Escobar v. State, 880 S.W.2d 782, 783 [(Tex. App.—Houston [1st Dist.] 1993)]" (quoted in a 2001 Fourteenth Court of Appeals opinion dismissing a mandamus against a district clerk). — [14th COA opinion, Mar. 15, 2001 (blocked for fetch)](https://txcourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/14thCOA/Case/Opinions/031501/010232f.PDF)
- **[snippet]** Smith v. Beto (5th Cir. 1973): Texas petitioner "was not entitled to a free copy of his state trial transcript for use in his post conviction state habeas corpus proceeding"; Fifth Circuit also holds an indigent "is not entitled to a free transcript if he had access to the record on direct appeal." — [5th Cir. 92-2582 (unpub., blocked)](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/unpub/92/92-2582.0.wpd.pdf)
- **[snippet]** A 1973 Texas AG opinion observed that Article 11.07 "fails to specifically provide payment to the court reporter in the case of an indigent petitioner." — [Tex. Att'y Gen. Op. H-69 (1973) (blocked)](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/opinion-files/opinion/1973/H-0069.pdf)
- **[snippet]** Gov't Code §52.047: the requesting person "is entitled to the original and one copy of the transcript"; additional copies "for a fee per page that does not exceed one-third of the original cost per page"; "If an objection is made to the amount of the transcript fee, the judge shall determine a reasonable fee, taking into consideration the difficulty and technicality of the material"; the reporter must furnish the transcript "not later than the 120th day after the application is received and the fee is paid or indigency is established"; reporters may charge separately for postage, exhibit reproduction and indexing. — [Gov't Code §52.047 (FindLaw, blocked)](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/government-code/gov-t-sect-52-047/); [onecle (blocked)](https://law.onecle.com/texas/government/52.047.html)
- **[snippet]** Tarrant County order "effective December 15, 2025," setting "page rate compensation for reporter's records prepared in indigent cases by the official court reporter of each of the district courts trying criminal cases" under TRAP 20.2: $6.00 per page for the original and two copies, $2.00 per additional copy, $10 per digital media conversion. — [Tarrant County local rule attachment (topics.txcourts.gov, blocked)](https://topics.txcourts.gov/LocalRulesPublic/PreviewAttachment/2737)
- **[snippet]** Comparison: federal courts in Texas charge $4.40/page for an ordinary original transcript (N.D. Tex. effective Oct. 1, 2024; E.D. Tex. FY2025). — [N.D. Tex. transcript rates (blocked)](https://www.txnd.uscourts.gov/transcript-rates-all-parties)

### Inferences
- At $6.00/page, a 2,000-page reporter's record costs about $12,000 and a 5,000-page record about $30,000 if purchased new; the practical route for an indigent 11.07 applicant is the appellate record already prepared (free under TRAP 20.2 on direct appeal) obtained from appellate counsel or the clerk, not a new transcription.
- Because TRAP 20.2 is keyed to "the time for perfecting the appeal," an applicant who never appealed (e.g., after a guilty plea) has no rule-based path to a free transcript for the writ; the cost is borne privately or by an innocence organization unless the habeas court orders a transcript under §3(d).

### Gaps
- Art. 11.07 §3(d)'s cost-shifting sentence on forensic testing ("the applicant shall pay the cost of the testing if the applicant retains counsel") and Gov't Code §52.047's current text were not read (statutes site returned only its application shell). No CCA opinion on free records for 11.07 applicants was found; Escobar is an intermediate-court decision quoted in an unpublished opinion. No statewide survey of county transcript rates was located.

## Key Question 8: Michael Morton Act retroactivity and the prosecutor's file after conviction (Public Information Act)

### Takeaway
Courts of appeals hold Art. 39.14 as amended by the Michael Morton Act applies only to offenses committed on or after Jan. 1, 2014 (Garza v. State, 453 S.W.3d 548 (Tex. App.—San Antonio 2014)); no CCA holding extending it to post-conviction proceedings for older offenses was found. Under the Public Information Act, Holmes v. Morales, 924 S.W.2d 920 (Tex. 1996) let prosecutors withhold closed files categorically under §552.108; the Legislature then narrowed §552.108(a)(2) so that, per AG rulings, it reaches only investigations that "concluded in a final result other than a conviction or deferred adjudication," meaning a convicted case's file is not covered by that exception (though other exceptions may apply), and "basic information" about the arrest and offense is always releasable. Statute and opinion texts were unreachable.

### Cited Findings
- **[snippet]** "appellate courts uniformly have held that the Act applies to all offenses committed after January 1, 2014," citing Garza v. State, 453 S.W.3d 548 (Tex. App.—San Antonio 2014) (Act inapplicable to a 2009 offense), and Gonzales v. State, 2015 WL 4273261, at *3 (Tex. App.—San Antonio July 15, 2015, no pet.) (2011 offense tried in 2014; Act "governs litigation of offenses occurring on or after January 1, 2014"). — [UT CLE criminal discovery paper (blocked)](https://utcle.org/ecourses/OC7249/get-asset-file/asset_id/41140)
- **[snippet]** TYLA summary: "the law only applied to crimes committed after the effective date. It is not retroactive," though many offices applied it to older cases voluntarily; "debate continues over disclosure from older prosecutions in post-conviction writ and testing contexts, where prosecutors keep significant discretion." — [TYLA Michael Morton Act page (blocked)](https://justiceforall.tyla.org/act.html)
- Trial counsel cannot pass 39.14 discovery to the client (see Question 4, Opinion 657), so post-conviction access to the State's file runs through the prosecutor or the PIA. — [Opinion 657 (blocked)](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/opinions/opinion-657)
- **[snippet]** Holmes v. Morales, 924 S.W.2d 920 (Tex. 1996): "section 552.108 of the Texas Government Code (The Open Records Act) excepts Holmes' 'closed' files from disclosure"; the closed files "included cases that Holmes never prosecuted or prosecuted to a conclusion, including post-conviction appellate review"; the court "rejected the attorney general's argument that prosecutors might withhold such records only when disclosure would 'unduly interfere with law enforcement or crime prevention.'" — [Holmes v. Morales (vLex, blocked)](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/holmes-v-morales-no-885536189); [HB 1002 (75R) bill analysis (blocked)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/75R/analysis/html/HB01002H.htm)
- **[snippet]** Current AG application: "a governmental body claiming section 552.108(a)(2) must demonstrate the requested information relates to a criminal investigation that has concluded in a final result other than a conviction or deferred adjudication"; §552.108 "does not except from disclosure basic information about an arrested person, an arrest, or a crime" (§552.108(c)); and "a habeas corpus proceeding is a civil proceeding," so pending habeas review does not itself make a file an active prosecution under (a)(1). — [Texas AG open-records letter rulings (blocked)](https://www2.texasattorneygeneral.gov/opinions/openrecords/51paxton/orl/2016/pdf/or201624582.pdf); [OR2015-03230](https://www2.texasattorneygeneral.gov/opinions/openrecords/51paxton/orl/2015/pdf/or201503230.pdf)
- Habeas-side alternative to the PIA: the habeas court "may order affidavits, depositions, interrogatories, additional forensic testing, and hearings" once issues are designated (Art. 11.07 §3(d)), and Brady claims "are appropriately raised in post-conviction habeas corpus because they implicate a constitutional right and require suppressed evidence, which was necessarily unavailable at the time of trial." — [Falkenberg, pp. 10, 25](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)
- Crime-lab records: the DPS "Texas Crime Lab Records (CLR) Connect portal," created by the 88th Legislature after OCFW litigation, "will facilitate the disclosure of relevant evidence simultaneously to the defense and the prosecution" and "is expected to go live during August 2026." — [FY25 Support Agencies report, p. 70](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1461688/fy-25-annual-report-of-judicial-support.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a pre-2014 offense the writ lawyer has no statutory discovery right against the prosecutor; the available tools are (a) the office's voluntary open-file/conviction-integrity policy, (b) a PIA request, which after the post-Holmes amendment cannot be refused under §552.108(a)(2) merely because the file is "closed" when the case ended in conviction (other exceptions — work product, §552.108(a)(1) if a related prosecution is pending, informant/victim confidentiality — may still apply), and (c) discovery orders from the habeas court after an order designating issues.

### Gaps
- No CCA or court-of-appeals opinion addressing Morton Act access in a post-conviction (11.07/Chapter 64) posture was found. The text of §552.108 as amended, the AG's Public Information Handbook, Holmes itself and the 1997 amending bill could not be read (capitol.texas.gov, texasattorneygeneral.gov and vLex blocked).
