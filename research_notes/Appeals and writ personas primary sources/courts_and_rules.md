## KQ2. The CCA Article 11.07 application form and instructions

### Takeaway
The form posted on the CCA's forms page is still the "Revised 2018" version (Appendix E to TRAP 73); it requires every applicant and petitioner (including attorneys) to use the complete form, limits each ground's factual summary to the two pages provided, caps a separate memorandum at 15,000 words/50 pages, and requires verification by unsworn declaration or notarized oath. No 2024–2026 revision of the form or of TRAP 73 was found on the CCA's Miscellaneous Docket pages.

### Cited Findings
- The CCA "Forms" page lists exactly two forms: "Writ of Habeas Corpus Application Art. 11.07 (PDF) 2018" and "Certification of Defendant's Right of Appeal (rev. 2018)". — [CCA Practice Before the Court — Forms](https://txcourts.gov/cca/practice-before-the-court/forms/)
- Form header: "APPENDIX E — COURT OF CRIMINAL APPEALS OF TEXAS — APPLICATION FOR A WRIT OF HABEAS CORPUS SEEKING RELIEF FROM FINAL FELONY CONVICTION UNDER CODE OF CRIMINAL PROCEDURE, ARTICLE 11.07"; every page footer reads "Article 11.07 Writ Application Form ... Revised 2018". — [11.07 form (PDF)](https://txcourts.gov/media/1442926/1107-form-rev-2018.pdf)
- Instruction 1: "All applicants and petitioners, including attorneys, must use the complete application form"; the form "is not for death-penalty cases, probated sentences which have not been revoked, misdemeanors, or pretrial habeas applications under Article 11.08." Instruction 2: "Failure to follow these instructions may cause your entire application to be dismissed." Instruction 4: file "the entire application form, including those sections that do not apply to you"; missing/renumbered pages may cause dismissal as non-compliant. Instruction 5: separate application per case number. — same PDF
- Instruction 6: "Each ground shall begin on a new page, and the recitation of the facts supporting the ground shall be no longer than the two pages provided for the ground in the form." Instruction 7: "Do not attach any additional pages for any ground"; more than five grounds → copy pages 14–15. Instruction 8: "Legal citations and arguments may be made in a separate memorandum that complies with Texas Rule of Appellate Procedure 73 and certifies that the document does not exceed 15,000 words if computer-generated or 50 pages if not." Instruction 12: "Warning: If the application form does not include all of the grounds for relief, additional grounds brought at a later date may be procedurally barred. See TEX. CODE CRIM. PROC. Art. 11.07 § 4." — same PDF
- Fields (items 1–18): name, DOB, place of confinement, warden, TDCJ-CID and SID numbers; (1) what the application concerns (conviction / sentence / time credit / parole / mandatory supervision / out-of-time appeal or PDR); (2) court number and county; (3) trial case number; (4) trial judge; (5) trial counsel; (6) judgment date; (7) offense and sentence; (8) counts; (9) plea (guilty-open, guilty-plea bargain, not guilty, nolo); (10) kind of trial; (11) testified?; (12) sentence discharged?; (13) direct appeal (court, case no., counsel, decision/date); (14) PDR (case no., decision/date); (15) prior 11.07 applications (writ no., decision/date, and "why the current grounds were not presented and could not have been presented in your previous application"); (16) other pending petitions; (17) time-credit claim exhaustion through TDCJ's time credit resolution system; (18) grounds — "A factual summary that merely references an attached memorandum or another ground for relief will not constitute a sufficient summary of the facts." ### Takeaway

### Cited Findings — current text (all from the [TRAP compilation dated 02.06.2026](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf), linked from [Rules & Standards](https://txcourts.gov/rules-forms/rules-standards/))
- Rule 9.4(i)(1): everything counts "except the following: caption, identity of parties and counsel, statement regarding oral argument, table of contents, index of authorities, statement of the case, statement of issues presented, statement of jurisdiction, statement of procedural history, signature, proof of service, certification, certificate of compliance, and appendix."
- Rule 9.4(i)(2) limits: (A) death-penalty direct-appeal brief/response and 11.071 subsequent application — 37,500 words / 125 pages; (B) "A brief and response in an appellate court (other than a brief under subparagraph (A)) and a petition and response in an original proceeding in the court of appeals: 15,000 words if computer-generated, and 50 pages if not. In a civil case in the court of appeals, the aggregate of all briefs filed by a party must not exceed 27,000 words"; (C) reply brief and reply in COA original proceeding — 7,500 words / 25 pages; (D) Supreme Court petition for review/response and SC original-proceeding petition/response — 6,500 words / 20 pages "inclusive of the introduction"; (E) CCA original-proceeding petition/response (non-death), PDR in the CCA, and motion for rehearing/response in an appellate court — 4,500 words / 15 pages; (F) reply to SC petition response — 3,250 / 10; (G) SC motions — 2,500 / 10; (H) reply to CCA original-proceeding response and reply to PDR — 2,400 words / 8 pages; (I) death-penalty CCA original proceeding — 9,000 / 30; (J) reply in death-penalty CCA original proceeding — 4,800 / 16. (The redline is in [Misc. Docket 25-9104, pp. 3–5](https://txcourts.gov/media/1461814/259104.pdf).)
- Rule 9.4(i)(3) Certificate of Compliance: "A computer-generated document that is subject to a word limit under this rule must include a certificate by counsel or an unrepresented party stating the number of words in the document. The person certifying may rely on the word count of the computer program used to prepare the document." (4) "A court may, on motion, permit a document that exceeds the prescribed limit."
- Rule 26.2 Criminal Cases: "(a) By the Defendant. The notice of appeal must be filed: (1) within 30 days after the day sentence is imposed or suspended in open court, or after the day the trial court enters an appealable order; or (2) within 90 days after the day sentence is imposed or suspended in open court if the defendant timely files a motion for new trial. (b) By the State. The notice of appeal must be filed within 20 days after the day the trial court enters the order, ruling, or sentence to be appealed."
- Rule 26.3 Extension of Time: "The appellate court may extend the time to file the notice of appeal if, within 15 days after the deadline for filing the notice of appeal, the party: (a) files in the trial court the notice of appeal; and (b) files in the appellate court a motion complying with Rule 10.5(b)."
- Rule 31.8 Expedited Appeal by the State of an Order Granting Bail (new; effective Sept. 1, 2025; final Feb. 6, 2026): applies to State appeals of bail "in an amount considered insufficient by the prosecuting attorney" for defendants charged with listed Penal Code offenses (murder, capital murder, certain aggravated assaults, aggravated kidnapping, aggravated robbery, aggravated sexual assault, indecency with a child, trafficking, continuous trafficking) or charged with a felony while on felony bail; State's notice "must comply with Code of Criminal Procedure Article 44.01 and expressly state that the appeal is an expedited appeal under Articles 44.01(a)(7), (f-1), and (f-2)" and be filed with the district clerk within 10 days of the bail order; State must file a bookmarked appendix (bail order, reporter's record of bail hearing, everything presented to the magistrate); magistrate must ensure the reporter's record "is prepared within 5 days of the State's requesting the record and arranging for payment"; briefs "need not comply with Rule 38 and may be in the form of a motion or letter... must not exceed 3,000 words"; "No extensions of time will be granted"; State's brief within 5 days of notice, defendant's within 5 days after; court of appeals "must conduct a de novo review... and issue an order within 20 days after the date the appeal is filed"; may affirm, modify, or reject and remand; opinion not required. Comment: "New Rule 31.8 is added to implement the amendments to Article 44.01 of the Code of Criminal Procedure."
- Rule 35.2 Criminal Cases: record due "(a) if a motion for new trial is not filed, within 60 days after the date the sentence is imposed or suspended in open court or the order appealed from is signed; (b) if a timely motion for new trial is filed and denied, within 120 days after the date the sentence is imposed or suspended in open court; or (c) if a motion for new trial is granted, within 60 days after the order granting the motion is signed."
- Rule 38.1(i): "Argument. The brief must contain a clear and concise argument for the contentions made, with appropriate citations to authorities and to the record." (Rule 38.1(h) Summary of the Argument; (j) Prayer; (k) Appendix in Civil Cases.)
- Rule 44.2 Reversible Error in Criminal Cases: "(a) Constitutional Error. If the appellate record in a criminal case reveals constitutional error that is subject to harmless error review, the court of appeals must reverse a judgment of conviction or punishment unless the court determines beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the conviction or punishment. (b) Other Errors. Any other error, defect, irregularity, or variance that does not affect substantial rights must be disregarded. (c) Presumptions..." (venue, jury impaneled and sworn, arraignment, plea, charge certified).
- Rule 49.1: "A motion for rehearing may be filed within 15 days after the court of appeals' judgment or order is rendered. The motion must clearly state the issues relied on for the rehearing." Rule 49.2: no response required unless requested; "The motion will not be granted unless a response has been filed or requested by the court."
- Rule 68.2: "(a) First Petition. The petition must be filed within 30 days after either the day the court of appeals' judgment was rendered or the day the last timely motion for rehearing or timely motion for en banc reconsideration was overruled by the court of appeals. (b) Subsequent Petition... within 10 days after the timely filing of another party's petition. (c) Extension of Time. The Court of Criminal Appeals may extend the time to file a petition for discretionary review if a party files a motion complying with Rule 10.5(b) no later than 15 days after the last day for filing the petition..."
- Rule 68.4 Contents of Petition: "must be as brief as possible... addressed to the 'Court of Criminal Appeals of Texas'"; (a) identity of judge, parties, and counsel; (b) table of contents; (c) index of authorities; (d) statement regarding oral argument; (e) statement of the case ("should seldom exceed half a page"); (f) statement of procedural history (opinion date, rehearing motion date, date overruled); (g) grounds for review, "separately numbered," with record page references if petitioner has the record, or questions presented; (h) argument "amplifying the reasons for granting review. See Rule 66.3"; (i) prayer; (j) appendix containing the COA opinion. Rule 68.9: reply within 15 days after the petition is filed in the CCA.
