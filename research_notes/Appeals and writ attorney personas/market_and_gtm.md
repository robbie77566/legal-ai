# Market size, buying behaviour and GTM channels for a record-review / issue-spotting product sold to criminal appellate and post-conviction (writ) attorneys (Texas emphasis)

Research date: 2026-10-18. Texas figures are labelled TX; national figures US. Where a number is unavailable the best proxy is given and labelled PROXY. Note: txcourts.gov, tcdla.com, tbls.org and legalethicstexas.com could not be fetched directly from this environment (proxy blocked), so figures from those sites come from search-index extracts of the named documents; URLs are given so the writer can verify.

## Key Question 1 — Market size: how many of these lawyers exist (TX and US) and how many "matters per year" exist

### Takeaway
The addressable Texas practitioner pool is small and concentrated: ~130 TBLS-certified criminal appellate specialists sit inside a ~3,900-member TCDLA and a ~700-member HCCLA; the "matter" pool is larger and steadier — roughly 850 PDRs and ~2,100–2,450 Article 11.07 writ applications reach the Court of Criminal Appeals every year, on top of several thousand direct criminal appeals filed in the 14 criminal-jurisdiction courts of appeals (statewide criminal COA count not located; see Gaps/PROXY). Nationally, NACDL (~10,000+ direct members, 90 affiliates up to 40,000 attorneys) and NAPD (~30,000 members) bound the defender universe.

### Inferences
- Core TX "specialist" segment ≈ 130 certified criminal appellate lawyers (plus a larger uncertified pool on county appellate wheels and in PD appellate divisions). PROXY for total TX criminal-appellate-active lawyers: several hundred (certified 130 + PD appellate staff in Harris/Dallas/Bexar/Travis/Tarrant + wheel attorneys), not thousands. TCDLA's 3,864 is the realistic upper bound of reachable criminal-defense lawyers in TX, most of whom are trial lawyers who do occasional appeals.
- TX "matters per year" PROXY: ~850 PDRs + ~2,100–2,450 11.07 applications at the CCA, plus direct criminal appeals in 14 COAs. If the 2nd COA's 470 criminal filings (FY2024) is typical of a mid-to-large COA, statewide direct criminal appeals are plausibly in the 4,000–6,000 range — this is an extrapolation, not a sourced figure. A large fraction of 11.07 applications are pro se (OCA notes pro se/original proceedings are 54% of COA filings in FY26), which cuts the lawyer-handled share but expands the family/pro-se-facing demand.
- Every 11.07 application and PDR is an opportunity for a record-review product; IPTX's ~1,000–1,250 requests/year is an additional, largely unmet post-conviction screening demand that a family-facing tier could serve.

### Gaps
- No official TBLS count by specialty was retrievable (tbls.org blocked here); the ~132–134 figure is from undated third-party directories. The report writer should treat "~130" as approximate.
- No statewide FY2024/FY2025 count of criminal appeals filed across all courts of appeals was found; the OCA Annual Statistical Report PDF (ar-statistical-fy24.pdf) contains it but could not be opened.
- Current State Bar Appellate Section and Criminal Justice Section membership counts not found (section reports in Texas Bar Journal do not list them).
- Counts of attorneys on county appellate appointment wheels (Harris/Dallas/Travis/Bexar/Tarrant) were not found; county plan documents list qualifications but not roster sizes.
- No national count of appellate public defender offices or state appellate defender agencies was found beyond NAPD's program counts (24–25 statewide programs; 100–110+ county programs, 2018).

## Key Question 2 — Buying behaviour: how solo/small criminal-defense firms and PD offices evaluate and buy software

### Takeaway
Solos budget little and rarely (41% budget for tech; <$3,000/yr on practice-management software), are cloud-native but AI-cautious (8% of solos have adopted AI widely), and discover tools through bar-association discounts, free trials and CLE. Public defender offices buy through county purchasing with security/CJIS-style review and, in Texas, can use TIDC Improvement Grants (≥$5,000, county match, annual cycle with a spring deadline) for "process improvement" technology — but not for routine tech upgrades.

### Cited Findings
- Clio 2025 Legal Trends for Solo and Small Law Firms: only 8% of solo practitioners and 4% of small firms have adopted AI "widely or universally"; most start with low-barrier tools such as ChatGPT or AI legal research; 79% of solos and 81% of small firms use cloud practice management; over 80% of legal professionals expect AI use to rise in the next year — [Clio press release, 2025](https://www.clio.com/about/press/legal-trends-solo-small-law-firms-2025/); Clio 2024 found 19% of mid-sized and smaller firms using AI — [Canadian Lawyer](https://canadianlawyermag.com/news/general/solos-and-small-firms-lag-with-ai-adoption-clio-report/392441)
- ABA 2024 Legal Technology Survey: 65% of firms budget for technology (same as 2022); 41% of solos vs 90% of 100+-lawyer firms; average annual tech spend across all respondents $13,991 (vs $14,106 in 2022); solos report spending less than $3,000/yr on practice-management software; most common solo hardware spend $1,000–$2,999 (37%) — [ABA 2024 Budgeting & Planning TechReport](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/law_practice/resources/tech-report/2024/2024-budgeting-and-planning-techreport); [ABA 2024 Solo & Small Firm TechReport](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/law_practice/resources/tech-report/2024/2024-solo-and-small-firm-techreport)
- Bar-member-discount channel in practice: Rev's TCDLA partner page offers members 3%–15% off human transcription by plan tier — [Rev x TCDLA](https://www.rev.com/pricing/bar-association/tcdla); Briefpoint markets "try Briefpoint right now – for free" and "one flat price, no per-case fees" — [Briefpoint outside counsel](https://briefpoint.ai/outside-counsel/); [Briefpoint Autodoc](https://briefpoint.ai/autodoc/)
- PD offices pay for JusticeText out of operating budgets; a Kentucky defender said he "wishes his office had more funding to expand the program" and not all Kentucky defenders have access — [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/public-safety/some-kentucky-public-defenders-say-ai-can-help-defendants)
- Virginia Indigent Defense Commission contracted JusticeText for $160,000 to roll out to 125 public defenders (≈$1,280/defender/yr) — [LawNext, Sep 2022](https://www.lawnext.com/2022/09/justicetext-raises-2-2m-seed-round-to-advance-its-mission-of-building-police-accountability.html)
- TIDC Improvement Grants fund "indigent defense process improvement projects, including technology"; routine tech upgrades/replacements are ineligible; minimum request $5,000; county matching funds required; FY2025 cycle ran Oct 2024–Sep 2025 with a May 10, 2024 priority deadline — [TIDC FY25 grant training](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/nubduuur/fy25-improvement-grant-app-training.pdf); FY2027 RFA (Oct 2026–Sep 2027) priority deadline May 8, 2026, applications via TIDC's Fluxx portal; multi-year grants minimum $50,000 — [TIDC FY2027 Improvement Grant RFA](https://www.tidc.texas.gov/media/erbbgpcw/fy2027-improvement-grant-rfa.pdf); [Texas Association of Counties summary](https://www.county.org/Grant-Opportunities/Indigent-Defense-Improvement-Grant-Request)
- Example award: Burnet County received an FY2026 Improvement Grant of $52,791 (TR-26-002) for equipment for its North Hill Country Regional Public Defender Office, Oct 1 2025–Sep 30 2026 — [Burnet County Commissioners Court packet](https://public.destinyhosted.com/burnedocs/2025/ComCt/20251014_4555/566%5F9b%5F%2D%5FFY26%5FBurnet%5FNorth%5FHill%5FCountry%5FRPDO%5FEquipment%2C%5FTARP%5FStatement%5Fof%5FGrant%5FAward%2Epdf)
- Everlaw for Good gives nonprofits, pro bono attorneys and CJA panel attorneys Everlaw "free of charge up to a monthly cap, with heavily discounted rates for larger matters" plus free EverlawAI Assistant credits; its 2024 announcement cites public defenders carrying "five times the workload considered acceptable" — [Everlaw for Good](https://everlaw.com/everlaw-for-good); [BusinessWire, May 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240509238116/en); [Program categories](https://support.everlaw.com/hc/en-us/articles/25296598812955-Everlaw-for-Good-Program-Category-Definitions)
- Thomson Reuters (Oct 2024) offers CoCounsel Core to legal services organizations and legal nonprofits at a subsidized $50 per seat per month — [LawNext](https://www.lawnext.com/2024/10/thomson-reuters-launches-program-to-provide-cocounsel-ai-to-legal-services-and-legal-nonprofits.html)
- Government-side security expectations: county/state RFPs for defender/prosecutor case systems ask for CJIS and NIST alignment, MFA, encryption, cloud preference and (Delaware DOJ) attestation that no data leaves the US or is accessed from outside the US — [Champaign County IL board packet, Nov 2024](https://champaigncountyil.gov/CountyBoard/CB/2024/241121_Meeting/241121AddendumFull.pdf); [Orleans Parish DA RFI](https://orleansda.com/assistance/request-for-information-rfi-case-management-system); [Delaware DOJ CMS RFP](https://bidcondocs.delaware.gov/lgl/lgl_12001-cms_rfp.pdf); vendor Filevine advertises SOC 2 Type II and "full alignment to CJIS Security Policy 5.9.3" for PD case management — [Filevine](https://filevine.com/practice-types/public-defender-case-management/)
- Appointed-counsel payment mechanics (TX): HB 1445 (effective Sept 1, 2025) amends CCP art. 26.05(c) so each county fee schedule must state reasonable fixed rates or min/max hourly rates (considering overhead and whether qualified attorneys will accept them), include an itemization form, and the commissioners court must pay within 45 days of application; payment requires judicial (or MAC director) approval — [HB 1445 enrolled text](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/HB01445F.htm); [HB 1445 bill analysis](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/analysis/pdf/HB01445H.pdf)
- Harris County Auditor conducted an "Indigent Defense Attorney Billing Review, Fiscal Years 2023 through 2025" (indicates voucher scrutiny) — [Harris County Auditor](https://auditor.harriscountytx.gov/portals/auditor/documents/AuditDocs/081426Indigent%20Defense%20Attorney%20Billing%20Review%20Fiscal%20Years%202023%20through%202025.pdf)

### Inferences
- A solo appellate lawyer's whole annual software budget is on the order of a few thousand dollars; a $100–$300/month subscription competes with their practice-management spend, so per-matter pricing that can be passed through or vouchered is likely to lower friction more than a seat subscription.
- PD procurement is slow (annual grant cycle, county purchasing, security questionnaires) but sticky; the JusticeText/Virginia benchmark (~$1,280/attorney/yr) and TR's $50/seat/month LSO price set the expectation band for defender-office pricing.
- TIDC's exclusion of "routine tech upgrades" means a product must be framed as a process-improvement pilot (e.g., reducing appellate backlog or expanding writ capacity) with measurable outcomes to be grant-eligible.

### Gaps
- No Texas-specific survey of criminal-defense solo software budgets was found; ABA and Clio figures are national.
- No Harris County Commissioners Court agenda item disclosing the JusticeText contract amount was found, though Harris County PD is a named JusticeText customer.
- Typical county data-security review timelines for PD software purchases were not found in a citable source.

## Key Question 3 — Influencers and channels that reach Texas criminal appellate / writ lawyers

### Takeaway
TCDLA is the hub (3,864 members; Rusty Duncan draws 850–1,000 attendees with $1,000 booths that sold out by March 2025; it already platforms AI vendors such as Rev in its CLE), supported by HCCLA (700+), the State Bar Appellate Section (free online CLE, Lunch & Learn series), the Texas Appellate Law Podcast/Texas Appellate Counsel Digest, TIDC's grant and training apparatus, and the Innocence Project of Texas; nationally NACDL's 90 affiliates and NAPD's ~30,000 members.

### Cited Findings
- TCDLA: 3,864 members (goal 4,000) — [TCDLA](https://tcdla.com); Rusty Duncan 2025 exhibitor packet anticipated 850–1,000 attendees; booth $1,000 with $500 deposit; booths "sold out by March" 2025; 2026 packet again projects 850–1,000; 2026 is the 39th annual course — [Rusty 2025 Exhibitor Packet](https://www.tcdla.com/TCDLA/cleandevents/docs/Rusty%202025%20Exhibitor%20Packet.pdf); [Rusty 2026 Exhibitor Packet](https://www.tcdla.com/TCDLA/cleandevents/docs/Rusty%202026%20Exhibitor%20Packet.pdf); [TCDLA Marketing Kit 2025](https://www.tcdla.com/TCDLA/cleandevents/docs/TCDLA%20Marketing%20Kit%202025.pdf) ("900+ attendees"); [TCDLA Marketing Kit 2026](https://www.tcdla.com/TCDLA/cleandevents/docs/TCDLA%20Marketing%20Kit%202026.pdf); Voice for the Defense advertising contact listed on TCDLA's Advertise page — [TCDLA Advertising](https://www.tcdla.com/TCDLA/Events/Advertise.aspx)

- NACDL: 90 affiliates totaling up to 40,000 attorneys — [NACDL 2026-27 release](https://www.nacdl.org/newsrelease/News-Release-~-2026-27-Officers-and-Board-Members); NAPD ~30,000 members — [NAPD slides Nov 2025](https://publicdefenders.us/app/uploads/2025/11/Membership-Overview-Slides-2.pdf)

### Inferences
- The cheapest high-density reach is a Rusty Duncan booth (~$1,000 + sponsorship) and a TCDLA member discount/partner page, replicating the Rev model; the appellate-specific slice is best reached through the Appellate Section's free CLE/Lunch & Learn slots and the Texas Appellate Law Podcast/Digest.
- Because TCDLA and HCCLA require member endorsement to join, peer referral is structurally built into these communities; a referral or "endorse a colleague" incentive mirrors their culture.
- For PD offices and counties, TIDC's grant trainings and county commissioners courts are the decision venues; vendor case studies framed as TIDC-fundable "process improvement" will matter more than ads.

### Gaps
- Voice for the Defense ad rates, circulation and TCDLA listserv size are in the Marketing Kit PDFs but the figures were not extractable here.
- No sources found for Dallas CDLA, Austin CDLA (ACDLA), San Antonio CDLA, TCDLEI, or Texas Center for Legal Ethics AI CLE specifics; the Texas Criminal Justice Section (State Bar) membership and programming were not found.
- Podcasts/newsletters specifically followed by Texas *criminal* appellate lawyers beyond the Texas Appellate Law Podcast were not identified.

## Key Question 4 — Comparable products, pricing, adoption and positioning

### Takeaway
No product found markets appellate issue-spotting from trial records to criminal defense lawyers; the closest comparables are evidence/discovery AI for criminal defense (JusticeText: ~$3M raised, 50+ PD agencies incl. Harris County PD; Matey/CrimD: $7.5M seed, Austin; Rev: $0.25/min and a TCDLA discount) and brief-side tools with per-brief precedents (Clearbrief $300/brief or $150/user/mo; TypeLaw $895/brief; Briefpoint $89/attorney/mo). Family-facing post-conviction review is sold by lawyers at $5,000–$15,000 flat and by prison consultants at $2,500–$25,000.

### Cited Findings

**Criminal-defense AI (closest comparables)**

## Key Question 5 — Willingness to pay, hourly rates, per-matter precedents and reimbursability

### Takeaway
Texas private practitioners' 2025 median rate is $368/hr (State Bar) while criminal defense averages ~$221/hr (Clio) and appointed appellate work pays $60–$200/hr with hard caps ($2,500–$7,500 per appeal/writ in sampled counties), so an hour of record review is worth roughly $100–$370 depending on payer; the Fair Defense Act makes "reasonably necessary" investigative/expert expenses reimbursable (with or without prior approval), which is the doctrinal hook for treating a per-matter software fee as a vouchered expense, though no county plan explicitly naming software was found.

### Cited Findings
- State Bar of Texas 2025 Income and Hourly Rate Report: median hourly rate for full-time private practitioners in 2025 was $368, up $43 from 2023, "one of the largest two-year gains since 2007" (practice-area table not retrieved) — [State Bar of Texas, 2025 report](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Demographic_and_Economic_Trends&Template=%2FCM%2FContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=72113); earlier editions: [2019 report](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Demographic_and_Economic_Trends&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=54237); [2011 fact sheet](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Demographic_and_Economic_Trends&Template=%2FCM%2FContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=20499)
- Clio (2025 data, page updated 2026): average Texas lawyer rate $366/hr vs $349 US; Texas criminal lawyer average $221/hr; practice-area range $158–$540 — [Clio Texas rates](https://www.clio.com/resources/legal-trends/compare-lawyer-rates/tx/)
- Appointed appellate/writ compensation (TX counties): Williamson County appeals voucher $155/hr capped at $7,500 per case (Aug 2023) — [Williamson County voucher](https://www.wilcotx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/2185/Hourly-Fee-Appeals-Voucher-Crim-eff-1112023); El Paso County (appointments after Jan 1, 2024) $100/hr in court, $85/hr out of court, appeals to the Eighth COA/CCA from $85/hr — [El Paso fee schedule](https://epcounty.com/courts/documents/councilofjudges/El_Paso_District_and_County_Court_Attorney_Fee_Schedule_11-2023_Uploaded_2024.pdf); Midland County felony appeals and writs $200/hr not to exceed 20 hours or $4,000 — [Midland schedule](https://tidc.tamu.edu/IDPlanDocuments/Midland/Midland%20District%20and%20County%20Court%20Compensation%20of%20Court%20Appointed%20Counsel.pdf); 81st/218th District Courts (Feb 2025) court-of-appeals work $60–$120/hr, maximum $4,500 — [81st/218th fee schedule](https://81-218.txcourts.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2025-02-25-ALL-Counties-Fee-Schedule-v1.pdf) (an older version listed $50/hr capped at $2,500 — [2019 supplement](https://81-218.txcourts.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2019-12-20-fee-supplement-to-indigent-defense-plan-updated.pdf)); Collin County death-penalty $150/hr, capital non-death $100/hr — [Collin County](https://www.collincountytx.gov/Courts/Indigent-Defense/Documents/CurrentFelonyCriminalCaseFeeSchedule.pdf)

### Inferences
- Value of an hour of record review: ~$85–$200 (appointed, capped) vs ~$221 (Clio criminal average) vs $368 (State Bar overall median). - Reimbursability: the FDA/art. 26.05 "reasonably necessary" expense pathway and the prior-approval culture (investigators, experts, transcripts) make a per-matter software fee plausibly voucherable with a motion and order, but this is untested in any plan found; counties with hourly-plus-expense schedules (Williamson, El Paso, Midland) are better test beds than flat-fee counties.
- Price anchors for a professional tier: $89–$150/user/month (Briefpoint/Clearbrief) for subscription; $300–$900 per matter (Clearbrief/TypeLaw) for per-record pricing; $50/seat/month (TR LSO) to ~$1,300/attorney/yr (JusticeText) for PD offices.

## Key Question 6 — Regulatory and trust requirements that gate sales

### Takeaway
Texas Opinion 705 (Feb 2025) and ABA Formal Opinion 512 (Jul 2024) require lawyers to vet AI vendors' confidentiality/data-retention practices (Rule 1.05), verify outputs and bill fairly, with ABA 512 requiring informed client consent before feeding client data into self-learning tools; TRAIGA (HB 149, effective Jan 1, 2026) is narrow (biometrics, manipulation, discrimination, government disclosure; AG enforcement $10k–$200k per violation; NIST AI RMF safe harbor); county PD buyers add CJIS/NIST/US-residency expectations; and Texas Gov't Code § 81.101 plus the Parsons Technology history means any family-facing output must carry clear "not a substitute for an attorney" disclaimers and avoid individualized legal advice.

### Cited Findings
- Texas Professional Ethics Committee Opinion 705 (Feb 2025): identifies four core obligations for generative AI use — competence (Rule 1.01), confidentiality (Rule 1.05), independent verification of AI outputs, and fair billing; warns about entering sensitive client details into systems that may share with third parties and recommends vetting tools and training staff; advisory, not binding — [Opinion 705 (primary)](https://legalethicstexas.com/resources/opinions/opinion-705/); [Akerman summary](https://www.akerman.com/en/firm/newsroom/akerman-lawyers-examine-texas-ethics-opinion-on-responsible-use-of-legal-generative-ai.html); [Promise Legal playbook](https://blog.promise.legal/ai-ethics-texas-law-firms/); Opinion 705 "mirrors" Opinion 680 (cloud) but adds that many GenAI systems retain input data and may train on it — [Chamberlain Hrdlicka](https://chamberlainlaw.com/Data-Privacy-Tracker/embrace-technology-but-stay-vigilant-what-texas-lawyers-need-to-know-about-generative-ai-and-confidentiality); one summary says client consent may be required in some cases — [Ezel.ai summary](https://ezel.ai/ethics-opinions/tx/705-generative-ai-in-the-practice-of-law)
- ABA Formal Opinion 512 (July 29, 2024): because self-learning GAI tools can disclose client information, lawyers must obtain a client's informed consent prior to inputting information relating to the representation into such a tool; generic boilerplate engagement-letter language is insufficient; lawyers must understand tool limits, verify output and consider supervision policies — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/american-bar-association-issues-formal-opinion-use-generative-ai-tools); [Frankfurt Kurnit](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102jwnl/aba-issues-comprehensive-formal-ethics-opinion-on-lawyers-use-of-generative-ai); [Ezel.ai ABA 512](https://ezel.ai/ethics-opinions/aba/512-generative-ai-tools)
- TRAIGA (HB 149) signed June 22, 2025, effective Jan 1, 2026: covers developers and deployers; prohibits social scoring, certain biometric identification, behavioral manipulation, unlawful discrimination and certain explicit content; government agencies must disclose AI interaction; healthcare providers must disclose AI use; no private right of action; AG enforcement with civil penalties $10,000–$200,000 per violation, a notice-and-cure mechanism, a safe harbor for NIST AI RMF compliance and for self-discovered violations, and a regulatory sandbox — [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/texas-enacts-responsible-artificial-intelligence-governance-act); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-us/knowledge/publications/c6c60e0c/the-texas-responsible-ai-governance-act) (notes the AI definition is broad enough to include virtual assistants and recommendation algorithms); [McDonald Hopkins](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/texas-enacts-broad-artificial-intelligence-regulations)
- Texas UPL: Gov't Code § 81.101(a) defines practicing law to include giving advice or performing services requiring legal skill outside court; in Unauthorized Practice of Law Committee v. Parsons Technology (N.D. Tex. 1999) a court enjoined Quicken Family Lawyer software; the Legislature then amended § 81.101 to exclude the design, sale and distribution of software that "clearly and conspicuously states" it is not a substitute for an attorney's advice, and the Fifth Circuit vacated the injunction — [5th Cir. opinion, 179 F.3d 956](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/179/179.F3d.956.99-10388.html); [HB 1507 analysis (1999)](https://lrl.texas.gov/scanned/hroBillAnalyses/76-0/HB1507.PDF); the UPL Committee is appointed by the Supreme Court of Texas to investigate and enforce — [TXPD ethics article](https://txpd.org/ethics-articles/the-unauthorized-practice-of-law-in-texas/)
- Government buyer security expectations: RFPs/RFIs for defender/prosecutor systems require CJIS and NIST alignment, MFA, encryption, and (Delaware) attestation that data never leaves the US and is not accessed from abroad — [Champaign County IL](https://champaigncountyil.gov/CountyBoard/CB/2024/241121_Meeting/241121AddendumFull.pdf); [Orleans Parish DA RFI](https://orleansda.com/assistance/request-for-information-rfi-case-management-system); [Delaware DOJ RFP](https://bidcondocs.delaware.gov/lgl/lgl_12001-cms_rfp.pdf); vendors advertise SOC 2 Type II and CJIS 5.9.3 alignment — [Filevine](https://filevine.com/practice-types/public-defender-case-management/); CJIS cloud background — [American City & County](https://www.americancityandcounty.com/?p=25693)
- Innocence organizations typically require the request to come from the incarcerated person, not family (an intake-design precedent for family-facing products) — [PA Innocence Project](https://www.painnocence.org/request-help)

### Inferences
- A lawyer-facing tier must ship with: a no-training/zero-retention data policy, a DPA, SOC 2 (and CJIS-aligned controls for county buyers), US data residency, and an explicit "verify before filing" workflow — these directly answer Opinion 705 and ABA 512 vetting duties and remove the need for per-client consent under ABA 512's self-learning-tool rule.
- TRAIGA is unlikely to bite a record-review product directly (it targets biometrics, manipulation, discrimination and government/healthcare disclosure), but adopting the NIST AI RMF yields a statutory safe harbor and a credible sales artifact; vendors selling to county PD offices (government deployers) should expect disclosure-of-AI obligations to flow down.
- Family-facing outputs should be framed as educational screening (e.g., "issues a lawyer may wish to review") with conspicuous "not a substitute for an attorney" statements per the amended § 81.101, and should route to licensed counsel; individualized advice ("file an 11.07 on ground X") to a non-lawyer is the UPL risk zone. Outputs labelled "attorney work product" are only meaningful when prepared for/at the direction of a lawyer.
