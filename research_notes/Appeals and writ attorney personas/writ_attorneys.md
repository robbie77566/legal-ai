## Key Question 1: Who does this work in Texas, and in what volume?

### Takeaway
Texas 11.07 habeas is a ~2,100-application-per-year docket at the Court of Criminal Appeals (down from ~5,400 in FY2001 and ~3,400–4,000 in FY2016–2018), handled by a small set of institutional actors (OCFW for capital, innocence organizations, a few public-defender post-conviction attorneys, State Counsel for Offenders) plus private solo/small-firm writ specialists. No source gave a pro se vs. counsel-filed split; the CCA's own disposition data shows a very small number of grants per year against thousands of filings.

### Inferences
- With ~2,100 11.07 filings and 57 grants in FY2025, the grant rate is on the order of 2–3% of filings (estimate; the 57 may include 11.071 and supplemental writs, and the denominator mixes pro se and counsel filings).
- The "dismissed for non-compliance" count (270 in FY2025, ~13% of filings) indicates that form/procedural compliance alone disposes of a substantial share of the docket — a direct product opportunity (form validation).
- Filing volume has fallen roughly 60% since FY2001 (5,410 → ~2,100), while institutional capacity (OCFW, IPTX) is characterized by caseload overload and attrition.
- The number of private writ specialists is unknown; the practitioner websites found (Bennett & Bennett, Broden & Mickelsen, Chris Perri Law, Texas Defense Firm, Blizzard Law Firm) suggest a small boutique market concentrated in Houston, Dallas, and Austin.

## Key Question 2: Economics — right to counsel, fees, who pays for investigation and experts, 11.071 compensation

### Takeaway
Non-capital 11.07/11.072 applicants have no general right to appointed counsel (appointment is discretionary, mandatory only in the narrow Art. 11.074 situation where the State agrees the applicant is innocent or convicted under a void law), so private writ work is retained at flat fees commonly quoted in the $15,000–$45,000 range, typically split into an investigation phase and a litigation phase, with experts, records and transcripts billed on top. Capital 11.071 counsel is appointed, with state reimbursement to counties capped at $25,000 per application.

### Cited Findings

**Right to counsel (non-capital)**
- Legislative analyses (2015): for non-capital habeas, Arts. 11.07 and 11.072 "give judges the discretion to appoint counsel"; "a judge has the discretion to appoint counsel to represent a defendant in a habeas corpus proceeding in a non-capital case" — [HB 1346 House analysis (84R)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/84R/analysis/html/HB01346H.htm)
- SB 662 (2015) added Art. 11.074, making appointment mandatory for indigent applicants "who are not guilty of the crime for which they were convicted or were convicted under a law that is now void," but "only mandatory when the prosecution agrees that the defendant's status warrants the appointment of counsel"; applies to cases imposing a penalty other than death or ordering community supervision — [SB 662 enrolled analysis](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/84R/analysis/html/SB00662F.htm)
- CCA materials say an eligible indigent defendant can ask for appointed counsel when the trial court concludes that "the interests of justice require representation" (this tracks Art. 11.07 §3(d)) — [Falkenberg 11.07 paper, txcourts.gov](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)
- HB 1346 (84R, 2015) proposed appointing counsel for certain non-death habeas applicants paid "at the same rate as attorneys appointed to represent criminal defendants at trial"; passage not confirmed in results — [HB 1346 analysis](https://www.lrl.texas.gov/scanned/hroBillAnalyses/84-0/HB1346.PDF)
- A 2015 bill analysis notes 11.071 "provides court-appointed counsel for indigent death-sentenced defendants who want counsel, and that no such provision exists for non-death cases" — [HB 1346 analysis](https://www.lrl.texas.gov/scanned/hroBillAnalyses/84-0/HB1346.PDF)
- HB 115 (2025), which would have required appointed counsel for indigent 11.073 applicants, passed the House but "did not ultimately make it through the Senate" — [Texas Defender Service](https://www.texasdefender.org/texas-junk-science-law-reform-progress/)

**Private fee ranges (practitioner websites; firm-specific, not authoritative)**
- Bennett & Bennett (Houston): $500 consultation fee; "If you decide to move forward after the consultation, we charge at least $10,000—often more, depending on the circumstances—to investigate the case and determine whether grounds exist for filing a petition for habeas relief. If investigation turns up viable grounds, we charge at least $15,000—often more—to prepare, file, and pursue habeas relief through the Texas courts." — [Bennett & Bennett post-conviction](https://bennettandbennett.com/post-conviction)
- Broden & Mickelsen (Dallas): two-stage model — "we charge a fee to evaluate the merits of any potential habeas claim; only if we find a valid basis ... will we charge an additional fee for litigating the habeas petition" — [Broden & Mickelsen](https://www.brodenmickelsen.com/?p=10409)
- Chris Perri Law (Austin): "the total legal fee for a writ of habeas corpus ranges from $15,000 to $45,000. That said, most writs cost around $30,000," split into an investigation phase (roughly half) and a litigation phase; expert fees "range widely, though most often the fees are around $5,000 to $10,000 total" — [Chris Perri Law](https://www.chrisperrilaw.com/blog-posts/how-much-does-a-writ-of-habeas-corpus-cost-in-texas)
- JustAnswer attorney response: $15,000–$30,000, some attorneys offering partial pro bono hours — [JustAnswer](https://www.justanswer.com/criminal-law/qts15-estimated-attorney-cost-file-writ-habius.html)
- Blizzard Law Firm (selling a DIY online 11.07 course) cites a Google figure of "$20,000 to $50,000 dollars to hire a post-conviction attorney" — marketing source — [Blizzard Law Firm course](https://blizzardlawfirm.com/ultimate-online-guide-writ-of-habeas-corpus-texasfreedom-from-texas-prison-online-course/)
- Texas Defense Firm lists 11.07 cost components: "legal fees, court filing fees, costs of obtaining and reviewing records and transcripts, expert witness fees, and other related expenses," varying "significantly depending on the complexity of the case" — [Texas Defense Firm](https://texasdefensefirm.com/11-07-writ-of-habeas-corpus-and-convictions)

**Who pays for testing/investigation**
- Art. 11.07 §3(d): "The state shall pay the cost of additional forensic testing ordered under this subsection, except that the applicant shall pay the cost of the testing if the applicant retains counsel for purposes of filing an application under this article." — [Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 11.07 (texas.public.law)](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._code_of_crim._proc._article_11.07)

**11.071 (capital) compensation and expenses**
- Art. 11.071 §2A: state reimbursement to a county for appointed counsel on one application is capped at $25,000; the county pays any excess and may pay more than the state reimburses; counsel may incur habeas investigation costs, including expert costs, without advance approval and seek reimbursement ex parte — [Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 11.071 (texas.public.law)](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._code_of_crim._proc._article_11.071)
- 2003 bill text (HB 1734, introduced, not confirmed enacted) describes an ex parte, verified, confidential request for prepayment of expenses filed no later than 30 days before the application, and proposed raising the cap from $25,000 to $50,000 (the $25,000 figure governs in the current text) — [HB 1734 (78R)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/78R/billtext/pdf/HB01734I.pdf)
- SB 1743 (2015) text describes a "reasonable fee based on time and labor required, the complexity of the case, and the experience and ability of the appointed counsel" with the $25,000 state reimbursement cap; enactment of that specific language not confirmed — [SB 1743 enrolled text](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/84R/billtext/html/SB01743F.htm)
- SB 1224 (78R, 2003) addressed 11.071 expense payment procedures (itemized statements, fee dispute committees) but gives no rates — [SB 1224](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/78R/billtext/pdf/SB01224E.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because 11.07 counsel is almost always retained, the private writ attorney's economics are fixed-fee against open-ended record-gathering and investigation hours; a two-phase (viability investigation, then litigation) fee structure is the dominant pattern across three independent firm sites. Software that shortens the screening phase changes the economics of the first (lower-margin, higher-risk) phase.
- The retained-counsel rule in 11.07 §3(d) shifts forensic testing costs to the client, which (inference) makes 11.073 forensic claims disproportionately expensive for privately retained applicants vs. OCFW/innocence-org clients.
- The 11.071 $25,000 state cap is per application and nominally unchanged since at least 2003, so (inference) counties and appointed counsel absorb the difference on multi-year capital writs.

### Gaps
- No current hourly rates or county fee schedules for 11.071 appointed counsel were found (the regional presiding judges' procedures document does not list rates).
- No data on how often trial courts exercise discretionary 11.07 §3(d) appointment, or on Art. 11.074 usage.
- No source on who pays for transcripts for indigent habeas applicants (the Griffin v. Illinois equal-protection issue surfaced in an old Fifth Circuit case but no Texas rule was found).

## Key Question 3: The workflow — intake/screening, record, investigation, drafting, filing, trial-court phase, CCA review, subsequent-writ bar, federal clocks, 11.071 timelines

### Takeaway
The 11.07 workflow is statutorily rigid: file on the CCA form in the convicting court; the State answers within 30 days (older cases: 15 days); the trial court has 20 days after that to designate controverted fact issues or the clerk forwards the record to the CCA immediately; designated issues must be resolved within 180 days of the State's receipt; the CCA then denies/dismisses without written order, remands, or files and sets. Art. 11.07 §4 bars any later application on the same conviction unless a new factual/legal basis or an innocence gateway is shown, which, combined with the one-year AEDPA clock that is tolled only by a "properly filed" state application, forces counsel to front-load every claim into a single, fully investigated application.

### Cited Findings

**Filing and the trial-court phase (Art. 11.07 §3; TRAP 73)**
- Application is filed with the clerk of the convicting court; the prosecutor "must answer not later than the 30th day after the date the copy of the application is received"; "Matters alleged in the application not admitted by the state are deemed denied" (current text, citing a 2023 Senate bill) — [Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 11.07 (texas.public.law)](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._code_of_crim._proc._article_11.07)
- Older versions gave the State 15 days to respond; a 1999 CCA opinion and a 2009 Prison Legal News summary describe the 15-day structure — [CCA opinion No. 73,799](https://txcourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/ccaInformation/opinions/73799.htm); [Prison Legal News (2009)](https://prisonlegalnews.org/news/2009/jun/15/delayed-order-designating-issues-in-texas-habeas-action-has-no-effect)
- Within 20 days of the expiration of the State's answer period, the convicting court must decide whether there are "controverted, previously unresolved facts material to the legality of the applicant's confinement"; "Failure of the court to act within the allowed 20 days shall constitute such a finding" (i.e., no issues); if there are such facts "it shall enter an order within 20 days of the expiration of the time allowed for the state to reply, designating the issues of fact to be resolved"; resolution tools include affidavits, depositions, interrogatories, forensic testing, and hearings — [art. 11.07 (texas.public.law)](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._code_of_crim._proc._article_11.07)
- CCA: "Without the timely entry of an order designating issues, Article 11.07 imposes a duty upon the clerk of the trial court to immediately transmit to this Court the record"; a designation order entered outside the 20-day window was held untimely and found to have "interfered with the clerk's duty to transmit"; the statute does not let the trial court extend these deadlines other than by timely designation — [CCA opinion No. 73,799](https://txcourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/ccaInformation/opinions/73799.htm)
- TRAP 73.5: "within 180 days from the date of receipt of the application by the State, the convicting court shall resolve any issues that the court has timely designated for resolution" — [TRAP 73.5 (state-rules.com)](https://state-rules.com/texas/appellate/73.5)
- TRAP 73.4(b): if the convicting court designates issues, "the clerk shall immediately transmit to the Court of Criminal Appeals a copy of that order and proof of the date the district attorney received the habeas application"; when "any pleadings, objections, motions, affidavits, exhibits, proposed or entered findings of fact and conclusions of law, or other orders are filed or made a part of the record, the district clerk shall immediately send a copy to all parties" — [TRAP 73.4 (state-rules.com)](https://state-rules.com/texas/appellate/74.4)
- TRAP 73.1 and 73.4 were amended effective December 1, 2018 (Misc. Docket 18-9151, joint CCA/Supreme Court order) to update 11.07 filing procedures and the clerk's summary sheet — [Misc. Docket 18-9151](https://www.TxCourts.gov/media/1442661/189151.pdf); [Texas Bar Journal, Oct. 2018](https://lsc-pagepro.mydigitalpublication.com/article/Court+Of+Criminal+Appeals+Of+Texas+Order/3197634/528302/article.html)
- TRAP 73.7 (2019): "The Texas Rules of Evidence apply to a hearing held on a postconviction application for a writ of habeas corpus filed under Code of Criminal Procedure Article 11.07 or 11.071"; TRAP 73.8 (adopted 2019) governs supplemental and amended applications — [Misc. Docket 19-003](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1444076/misc-docket-19-003-order-proposing-amendments-to-trap-737-and-adoption-of-738.pdf); [TRAP 73.8](https://www.state-rules.com/texas/appellate/73.8)
- Prior amendments: Misc. Docket 16-9012 amended TRAP 73.4, 79.2 and TRE 615 effective Jan. 1, 2016 — [Misc. Docket 16-9012](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1311973/169012.pdf)
- state-rules.com shows TRAP 73.1, 73.6 and 73.8 as "Amended January 1, 2026 (current)," but the page commentary still describes the 2019 change; no 2025/2026 CCA order amending Rule 73 was found, so the 2026 label may reflect site updating rather than a substantive amendment — [TRAP 73.1 (state-rules.com)](https://www.state-rules.com/texas/appellate/73.1); [TRAP 73.6](https://state-rules.com/texas/appellate/73.6)

**Subsequent-application bar (Art. 11.07 §4)**
- §4(a) bars merits review of a later application challenging the same conviction unless it shows sufficient specific facts that (1) the current claims could not have been presented previously because the factual or legal basis was unavailable, or (2) "by a preponderance of the evidence, but for a violation of the United States Constitution no rational juror could have found the applicant guilty beyond a reasonable doubt"; §4(c): a factual basis is unavailable if it "was not ascertainable through the exercise of reasonable diligence on or before" the relevant date — [HB 1291 (89R) bill text showing §4](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/HB01291I.htm)
- CCA has interpreted "subsequent applications" under §4 "to include all applications for writs of habeas corpus regarding the same conviction" — [CCA opinion No. 73,064](https://txcourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/ccaInformation/opinions/73064a2.htm)
- CCA granted reconsideration on whether an application was barred as "subsequent" where the applicant learned the facts months after the first application was denied — [CCA opinion No. 73,594](https://txcourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/ccaInformation/opinions/73594.htm)
- Bills to reword §4(a) were introduced in 2019 (HB 3425, also covering scientific-evidence claims), 2021, 2023 (HB 247 engrossed), and 2025 (HB 1291, adding a state-consent option and making the "sufficient specific facts" showing a separate requirement); none confirmed enacted — [HB 3425 (86R)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/86R/billtext/html/HB03425I.htm); [HB 247 (88R)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88R/billtext/pdf/HB00247E.pdf); [HB 1291 (89R)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/HB01291I.htm); [Legiplex HB 1291 tracker](https://app.azure.legiplex.com/tx/legislature/2025/89-r/bills/hb1291)

**11.071 (capital) timelines**
- 2017 bill text shows the then-existing rule: application due 180 days after counsel is appointed or 45 days after the State's original brief on direct appeal, whichever is later; the bill proposed striking the 180-day prong. The convicting court may, "for good cause shown and after notice and an opportunity to be heard by the attorney representing the state," grant one 90-day extension; either party may request a hearing; if good cause is not established the request is denied — [HB 3617 (85R)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/85R/billtext/pdf/HB03617I.pdf); [SB 958 (85R)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/85R/billtext/html/SB00958I.htm). Whether the 2017 change was enacted was not confirmed.
- 1999 (HB 1516) analysis: the State's answer deadline moved to the 120th day after notice of the writ (from the 30th), with extensions not to run past 180 days after notice — [HB 1516 analysis (76R)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/76R/analysis/html/HB01516F.htm)
- Current 11.071 text is posted at [SCOTUSblog copy of art. 11.071 (Mar. 2026)](https://www.scotusblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/TexasCrimCodeArticle11.071.pdf) and [texas.public.law](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._code_of_crim._proc._article_11.071) (not readable in this environment).

**11.072 (community supervision)**
- An 11.072 application "may not be filed ... if the applicant could obtain the requested relief by means of an appeal under Article 44.02 and Rule 25.2"; "Not later than the 60th day after the day on which the state's answer is filed, the trial court shall enter a written order granting or denying the relief sought" — [HB 1713 enrolled (78R, 2003)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/78R/billtext/html/HB01713F.htm)
- TDCAA checklist: State's answer due 30 days from receipt (one 30-day extension for good cause); trial court must grant or deny within 60 days after the State's response, "No dismissal or extension is available"; the ruling is by the trial court and appealable — [TDCAA Art. 11.072 Writs Checklist](https://www.tdcaa.com/wp-content/uploads/Art.-11.072-Writs-Checklist.pdf)
- "In an Article 11.072 proceeding, the trial judge is the sole finder of fact" — [Texas Court of Appeals opinion (FindLaw)](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/tx-court-of-appeals/1726520.html)

**11.09 (misdemeanor)**
- Art. 11.09 applies to persons "confined on a charge of misdemeanor" at every stage from charge through post-conviction; misdemeanor convictions not on unrevoked community supervision are ruled on by the trial court and are generally appealable to the intermediate courts of appeals; the applicant must prove by a preponderance "facts showing a cognizable irregularity and harm"; the trial court "sits as the fact finder and is the sole judge of credibility"; a trial court may refuse to issue a writ frivolous on its face; prosecutors are advised to check whether the misdemeanor was used to enhance (e.g., DWI) — [TDCAA Journal, Post-conviction Art. 11.09 writs (Jacobs)](https://tdcaa.com/journal/post-conviction-art-11-09-writs-of-habeas-corpus)
- CCA held statutory county courts have habeas power and 11.09 does not limit jurisdiction to confined applicants — [CCA opinion](https://txcourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/ccaInformation/opinions/110499a.htm); 2025 CCA decision treats defendants on appeal bonds for fine-only Class C misdemeanors as "restrained" for habeas purposes — [Criminal Legal News, Nov. 2025](https://www.criminallegalnews.org/news/2025/nov/15/texas-court-of-criminal-appeals-announces-defendants-on-appeal-bonds-for-fine-only-class-c-misdemeanors-are-restrained-for-purposes-of-seeking-habeas-relief/)
- A 2023 Thirteenth Court case dismissed 11.09 appeals because the habeas court's denials were not merits-based and had no written findings — [13th COA 2023](https://cases.justia.com/texas/thirteenth-court-of-appeals/2023-13-22-00120-cr.pdf); the CCA granted PDR in Garcia on whether the State can appeal an 11.09 order that functionally grants a new trial — [SPA, Garcia](https://spa.texas.gov/past-pdr-cases/garcia-leonardo-fabio/)

**11.073 (junk science) — 2025 status**
- HB 115 (89R, 2025, Rep. David Cook) would have replaced 11.073's preponderance standard with a "reasonable likelihood" test, required written CCA opinions on denials, required appointed counsel for indigent applicants, allowed CCA consideration of certain noncompliant successive 11.071 writs, and created a new Art. 11.66 document-production writ, applying to applications filed on or after Dec. 1, 2025 — [Texas Policy Research summary](https://www.texaspolicyresearch.com/bills/89th-legislature-hb-115/); House preliminary vote 118–10 — [Texas Tribune, May 14, 2025](https://www.texastribune.org/2025/05/14/texas-junk-science-bill-house-david-cook/); final House vote 124–14; "House Bill 115 did not ultimately make it through the Senate this session" — [Texas Defender Service](https://www.texasdefender.org/texas-junk-science-law-reform-progress/)

**Federal habeas (28 U.S.C. §2254) and the AEDPA clock**
- AEDPA requires filing "within one-year of 'the date on which the judgment became final by the conclusion of direct review or the expiration of the time for seeking such review'"; the period is tolled during a "properly filed application for State post-conviction or other collateral review" — [Fifth Circuit, 98-10298](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/pub/98/98-10298.CV0.wpd.pdf); [Fifth Circuit, 00-41089](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/pub/00/00-41089.cv0.wpd.pdf)
- Larry v. Dretke: a Texas 11.07 application filed before the conviction became final (direct appeal still pending) is not "properly filed" and does not toll the federal clock — [Casemine commentary on Larry v. Dretke](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/proper-filing-of-state-habeas-applications-under-aedpa:-insights-from-larry-iii-v.-dretke/view); [Fifth Circuit, 02-21010](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/pub/02/02-21010-CV0.wpd.pdf)
- A state application dismissed for abuse of the writ (§4) was treated as not "properly filed," so no tolling — [Fifth Circuit (unpub.), 09-20093](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/unpub/09/09-20093.0.wpd.pdf)
- Fifth Circuit affirmed a finding that an ambiguously worded CCA denial order meant the first state application was not "properly filed" — [Fifth Circuit, 99-20398](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/pub/99/99-20398.cv0.wpd.pdf)

### Inferences
- Combining §4 (one bite), the 30-day/20-day/180-day trial-court clock, and the requirement that the state writ be filed only after finality (Larry) yields the standard counsel strategy: wait for mandate, then file one exhaustive application; counsel must track (a) the direct-appeal finality date, (b) the AEDPA expiry, (c) the state filing date, and (d) the state disposition date, because untolled days before and after the state writ are what remain for the federal petition. (Interaction mechanics such as the 30-day PDR window and 90-day certiorari window are well-known but were not retrieved from a citable source in this run — see Gaps.)
- Because trial-court inaction within the 20-day window is deemed a "no controverted facts" finding and triggers immediate transmittal, habeas counsel (inference) must be ready to prompt the court for a designation order and submit proposed findings early; the TDCAA side prepares the State's answer and proposed findings within 30 days.
- 11.072 and 11.09 are decided by the trial court and appealed to the court of appeals (not the CCA), so (inference) the drafting product for those vehicles is a trial-court pleading plus an appellate brief, not the CCA form.

### Gaps
- The full current text of Art. 11.07 §3 (including the exact 2023 amendment bill number that moved the State's answer to 30 days), §5 (transmittal), and §3(d) (counsel appointment) could not be read; the 30-day figure rests on a search extract of texas.public.law.
- The current 11.071 §4 filing deadline (whether the 180-day-after-appointment prong survives) and current findings deadlines were not confirmed.
- No citable source retrieved for the Texas-specific finality rules (30-day PDR period; 90-day certiorari period) or for Fifth Circuit PDR-tolling cases (e.g., Roberts v. Cockrell); planned query was cut off by the search budget.
- No source on the CCA's internal "white card" practice (the term did not surface in results); only the "denied without written order" disposition language was found.
- Ex parte Elizondo (actual innocence standard), Ex parte Robbins (11.073), and the out-of-time PDR line of cases were not retrieved (search budget exhausted).

## Key Question 4: Inputs and outputs — records to gather, documents produced, format rules

### Takeaway
The core output is the CCA's mandatory 11.07 application form (every ground stated on the form, two pages per ground, verified by notary oath or inmate declaration) plus an optional memorandum capped at 15,000 words / 50 pages; omissions or renumbering can get the whole application dismissed. Inputs are the trial and appellate record, trial counsel's file and affidavit, the prosecutor's file (Michael Morton Act access is not retroactive to pre-2014 offenses), and expert/forensic material; the trial court's findings are built from affidavits and the parties' proposed findings.

### Cited Findings

**The 11.07 application form and TRAP 73.1**
- TRAP 73.1: an 11.07 application "must be on the form prescribed by the Court of Criminal Appeals"; the form must list every ground; "Each ground for relief and supporting facts raised on the form shall not exceed the two pages provided for each ground in the form"; "legal citations and arguments may be made in a separate memorandum" — [TRAP 73.1 (state-rules.com)](https://www.state-rules.com/texas/appellate/73.1); [TRAP 73 (courtrules.net)](https://www.courtrules.net/texas/texas-appellate-procedure/rule-73)
- Form instructions (2018 version): "Each ground shall begin on a new page, and the recitation of the facts supporting the ground shall be no longer than the two pages provided"; "Any ground not raised on the form will not be considered"; memorandum "does not exceed 15,000 words if computer-generated or 50 pages if not"; "All applicants and petitioners, including attorneys, must use the complete application form"; "Failure to follow these instructions may cause your entire application to be dismissed"; verification by "signing either the Oath Before Notary Public or the Inmate's Declaration, which are at the end of this form on pages 11 and 12"; "You may be prosecuted and convicted for aggravated perjury if you make any false statement of a material fact in this application" — [11.07 Writ 2018 form (CIRA/district clerk host)](https://newtools.cira.state.tx.us/upload/page/7062/11.07%20Writ%202018form.pdf); [Harris County District Clerk 11.07 form](https://www.hcdistrictclerk.com/Common/Forms/pdf/1107_Writ%20Form.pdf); [Bexar County copy](https://elections.bexar.org/DocumentCenter/View/20646/-1107-Writ-of-Habeas-Corpus-Seeking-Relief-From-Final-Felony-Conviction); [Johnson County copy](https://johnsoncountytx.org/home/showpublisheddocument/708/635762253280130000)
- CCA filing checklist (rev. eff. 7/17/2015): memorandum "shall not exceed 15,000 words or 50 pages"; a computer-generated memorandum over 15,000 words (or non-computer over 50 pages) is non-compliant "unless the trial court grants leave to exceed the limit" — [CCA checklist for filings](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/208125/checklist-for-filings-in-cca-revised-eff-07172015.pdf)
- Multiple form versions circulate (2007, 2014, 2018) with slightly different instructions — [CCA order re: form](https://www.TxCourts.gov/media/1442665/189153.pdf)
- A completed counsel-filed 11.07 application example (Powell) is publicly posted — [DocumentCloud: Powell Habeas](https://assets.documentcloud.org/documents/3222327/Powell-Habeas.pdf)

**Trial-court record and findings**
- Filings that become part of the habeas record and are served on all parties by the district clerk: "pleadings, objections, motions, affidavits, exhibits, proposed or entered findings of fact and conclusions of law, or other orders" — [TRAP 73.4 (state-rules.com)](https://state-rules.com/texas/appellate/74.4)
- Trial counsel's affidavit is central to IAC resolution: in a Fifth Circuit case, "On the basis of Jaynes' affidavit, the state judge who had presided at Cooper's trial found that Jaynes' performance was not deficient and that, even if it had been, Cooper could not demonstrate prejudice" — [Fifth Circuit, 05-41747](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/pub/05/05-41747-CV0.wpd.pdf)
- 11.071 (1997 analysis): parties must file proposed findings by a set date — [SB 1728 analysis (75R)](https://lrl.texas.gov/scanned/srcBillAnalyses/75-0/SB1728RPT.PDF)
- The UT CLE Falkenberg 2020 paper covers "timelines, evidentiary hearings, and proposed findings of fact" from the defense side — [UT CLE 2020](https://utcle.org/ecourses/get-asset-file/asset_id/51785/code/OC8676)

**Prosecutor's file (Michael Morton Act, art. 39.14)**
- Effective Jan. 1, 2014, the Act "amended article 39.14 ... to require that prosecutors disclose all information in a prosecutor's file except the prosecutor's work product and other information (such as information about victims and children) that is made confidential by law" — [Texas Appleseed Michael Morton Act report (2015)](https://texasappleseed.org/sites/default/files/2023-05/tds-2015-nr-michaelmortonactreport-final.pdf)
- "The Michael Morton Act applies to litigation of offenses that occurred on or after January 1, 2014" (not retroactive) — [versustexas.com](https://versustexas.com/?p=58436); there is "a continuing debate about the disclosure of prosecution files in older prosecutions for post-conviction writs or testing, but prosecutors still retain significant discretion"; some offices "have even applied it retroactively, even though it is not mandatory" — [Texas Appleseed report](https://texasappleseed.org/sites/default/files/2023-05/tds-2015-nr-michaelmortonactreport-final.pdf)
- Ethics Opinion 646 (2014): prosecutors cannot condition open-file access on defense counsel waiving client rights; the Act "requires an 'open file' policy by all Texas prosecutors without requiring defendants or their lawyers to agree to any restrictions" — [Texas Ethics Opinion 646](https://legalethicstexas.com/resources/opinions/opinion-646/)

**Forensic testing**
- 11.07 §3(d) allows court-ordered additional forensic testing as a fact-resolution tool, with the cost allocation described in Key Question 2 — [art. 11.07 (texas.public.law)](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._code_of_crim._proc._article_11.07)

### Inferences
- The document set a counsel-filed 11.07 package comprises, per the rules above: (1) the complete CCA form with each ground on its own two-page block, (2) a memorandum ≤15,000 words, (3) exhibits/affidavits (trial counsel, witnesses, experts), (4) verification (notary or inmate declaration), and later (5) proposed findings of fact and conclusions of law, (6) objections to the State's proposed findings, and (7) any supplemental/amended application under TRAP 73.8. Items 5–7 are inferred from the rule text listing those filings, not from a practice guide read in full.
- The two-page-per-ground limit plus the "any ground not raised on the form will not be considered" rule means (inference) the form's ground statements, not the memorandum, are the legally operative document — a drafting tool must treat the form fields as primary and enforce the page budget.
- For pre-2014 offenses (the majority of long-serving TDCJ applicants), prosecutor-file access depends on office policy, so (inference) records acquisition is office-by-office and not scriptable.

### Gaps
- Could not read the current CCA form itself (page count, field list, the clerk's summary sheet added in 2018) or confirm whether a 2024–2026 form revision exists.
- No source retrieved on obtaining trial counsel's file (Texas ethics rules on client files), TDCJ records, DPS/lab files, or appellate-record access/cost for habeas; planned queries were cut off.
- No example of a trial court's order designating issues, proposed findings template, or State's answer template was retrieved.

## Key Question 5: Pain points described by practitioners and institutions

### Takeaway
The documented pain points are institutional overload (OCFW caseloads ~2x national standards with high attrition; IPTX intake backlogs measured in months; a county PD unit that quit 11.07 appointments for lack of capacity), the high up-front cost of investigation and records borne by clients, dependence on trial counsel's affidavit with the trial judge as sole fact-finder, the §4 one-bite bar, non-compliance dismissals, and the AEDPA trap for applications filed too early or dismissed as abusive.

### Cited Findings
- OCFW: 8.5 co-counseled cases per attorney vs. 4–6 national standard; 27%/38% attrition; "Caseloads substantially higher than applicable professional standards"; non-competitive salaries — [LBB Agency 215 (86R)](https://www.lbb.texas.gov/Documents/HAC_Summary_Recs/86R/Agency_215.pdf); 27 departures since 2010 equal to 168% of staff size — [OCFW LAR](https://ocfw.texas.gov/_files/ugd/638415_b49e5480ca054235980c0f609580a734.pdf)
- IPTX: "due to the high volume of requests it receives, it can take up to several months for you to receive a reply" — [IPTX](https://innocencetexas.org/submit-a-case/); 776 new requests in Jun–Dec 2025 against 106 cases under active investigation — [TIDC report](https://www.tidc.texas.gov/media/dyhfbkld/iptx-tt-and-aandm-activity-report-june-2025-to-december-2025.pdf)
- Dallas County PD "stopped accepting [11.07] appointments since January 1, 2008 because of the large increase in appointments on direct appeals" — [Dallas PD report](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20091005160427/http:/www.courts.state.tx.us/tfid/pdf/Dallas%20PD%20Report-%20FINAL.pdf)
- Cost burden: investigation phase alone quoted at "at least $10,000—often more" before any filing — [Bennett & Bennett](https://bennettandbennett.com/post-conviction); costs include "obtaining and reviewing records and transcripts, expert witness fees" — [Texas Defense Firm](https://texasdefensefirm.com/11-07-writ-of-habeas-corpus-and-convictions); experts typically $5,000–$10,000 — [Chris Perri Law](https://www.chrisperrilaw.com/blog-posts/how-much-does-a-writ-of-habeas-corpus-cost-in-texas); retained-counsel applicants pay for court-ordered forensic testing — [art. 11.07 §3(d)](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._code_of_crim._proc._article_11.07)
- Fact-finding deference: trial counsel's affidavit resolved an IAC claim against the applicant — [Fifth Circuit, 05-41747](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/pub/05/05-41747-CV0.wpd.pdf); the trial judge is "the sole finder of fact" in 11.072 and found the applicant's complaints about counsel "not credible" — [Texas COA (FindLaw)](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/tx-court-of-appeals/1726520.html); in 11.09 the trial court "is the sole judge of credibility and demeanor" — [TDCAA Journal](https://tdcaa.com/journal/post-conviction-art-11-09-writs-of-habeas-corpus)
- Form non-compliance: 270 habeas applications "dismissed for non-compliance" in FY2025 — [CCA FY25 report](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1462062/cca-annual-reports-fy25.pdf); "Failure to follow these instructions may cause your entire application to be dismissed" — [11.07 form 2018](https://newtools.cira.state.tx.us/upload/page/7062/11.07%20Writ%202018form.pdf)
- One-bite bar: §4 applies to "all applications for writs of habeas corpus regarding the same conviction" — [CCA No. 73,064](https://txcourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/ccaInformation/opinions/73064a2.htm); legislative attempts to loosen §4 in 2019, 2021, 2023 and 2025 not confirmed enacted — [HB 1291 (89R)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/HB01291I.htm)
- AEDPA traps: premature state filing is not "properly filed" (no tolling) — [Casemine, Larry v. Dretke](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/proper-filing-of-state-habeas-applications-under-aedpa:-insights-from-larry-iii-v.-dretke/view); abuse-of-writ dismissal defeats tolling — [Fifth Circuit 09-20093](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/unpub/09/09-20093.0.wpd.pdf)
- Strict trial-court deadlines cannot be extended except by timely designation order — [CCA No. 73,799](https://txcourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/ccaInformation/opinions/73799.htm)
- Prosecutor-file access for pre-2014 offenses is discretionary — [Texas Appleseed](https://texasappleseed.org/sites/default/files/2023-05/tds-2015-nr-michaelmortonactreport-final.pdf)
- Junk-science reform (lower standard, appointed counsel, written CCA opinions) failed in the Senate in 2025, leaving the preponderance standard and no counsel right in place — [Texas Defender Service](https://www.texasdefender.org/texas-junk-science-law-reform-progress/); practitioner commentary argues "Texas' Junk Science Law Needs Overhaul" — [John T. Floyd Law](https://johntfloyd.com/texas-junk-science-law-needs-overhaul/)

### Inferences
- The combination of (a) no appointed counsel, (b) $10k+ to investigate before knowing if a claim exists, and (c) a one-bite bar means the screening decision carries the highest stakes and is the least supported step — consistent with the product-opportunity framing of a viability screen.
- The high share of non-compliance dismissals suggests pro se and some counsel filings fail at the form level; automated form validation against TRAP 73.1 (two pages per ground, all pages present, verification, word count) would address a measurable slice of the docket.

### Gaps
- No practitioner narrative sources (TCDLA Voice articles, bar journal pieces, blog posts) were retrieved describing uncooperative trial counsel, lost or destroyed records, court-reporter transcript costs, or TDCJ client-contact difficulties; the planned searches for these were cut off by the search budget. These remain unsourced.
- No data on how often trial courts hold live evidentiary hearings vs. decide on affidavits, or on CCA remand rates.

## Key Question 6: How they do the work today — tools, habits, time per matter

### Takeaway
Very little citable material surfaced on tooling. What exists shows a paper/PDF-form-centric process (the CCA form is distributed as a static PDF by district clerks; filings and findings are served by the district clerk), a CLE/practice-paper knowledge base (UT CLE, CCA-hosted Falkenberg paper, TDCAA checklists on the prosecution side), and at least one commercial DIY online course aimed at families.
