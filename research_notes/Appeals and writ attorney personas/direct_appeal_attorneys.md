## Key Question 1: Who does criminal direct-appeal work in Texas (practice settings, counts, caseloads)?

### Takeaway
The work is split among (a) a small cadre of roughly 130–200 TBLS Criminal Appellate Law board-certified lawyers (most in solo/small practice, some in PD offices and DA offices), (b) public defender appellate divisions in the large urban counties (Harris, Dallas, Bexar; capital direct appeals in the Dallas PD capital division), (c) court-appointed private counsel paid under county fee schedules, and (d) TDCJ's Independent Office of Inmate Counsel (formerly State Counsel for Offenders) for in-prison offenses. Caseload benchmarks used in Texas planning documents are roughly 25–31 appeals per attorney per year.

### Cited Findings
- TBLS Criminal Appellate Law certification: the Supreme Court of Texas approved standards for criminal appellate certification in 2011; certification is "available for Texas attorneys who have handled a sufficient number of post-conviction writs and appeals in criminal cases." — [TDCAA Journal, "New board certification in criminal appellate law" (Mar–Apr 2011)](https://www.tdcaa.com/journal/new-board-certification-in-criminal-appellate-law)
- Approximate headcount of Criminal Appellate Law certificate holders: one directory says "approximately 134 attorneys have earned their status as board certified in criminal appellate law"; another lists 132. Neither page is dated. — [Lawyer Legion, Board Certified Criminal Appellate Law Attorneys in Texas](https://www.lawyerlegion.com/certifications/texas/criminal-appellate-law); [texaslegal.guide TBLS specialties](https://texaslegal.guide/specialties/)
- For comparison, the same directory lists 763 attorneys certified in Criminal Law (trial). — [texaslegal.guide](https://texaslegal.guide/specialties/)
- A 2021 firm post says its attorney "is one of just 87 lawyers in Texas who are board certified in both criminal law and criminal appellate law." — [Blizzard Law Firm (2021)](https://blizzardlawfirm.com/2021/11/what-is-a-criminal-law-board-certified-attorney/)
- Attorney-profile boilerplate claims the credential is "shared by fewer than 200 attorneys out of over 60,000 in Texas" (undated, self-promotional, repeated verbatim across profiles). — [Cornell LII lawyer directory, Addison TX appellate lawyers](https://lawyers.law.cornell.edu/lawyers/appeals-appellate/texas/addison?page=6)
- Prosecutors also hold the credential: a Tarrant County ADA earned Criminal Appellate Law certification in 2024; the TBLS credential overall is held by "less than 7% of attorneys in the state." — [Hoodline (Jan 2024)](https://hoodline.com/2024/01/tarrant-county-ada-tori-ford-oblon-earns-elite-criminal-appellate-law-certification-in-texas/)
- TBLS certifies in 27 specialty areas; roughly 7,000 board-certified lawyers out of 70,000+ licensed Texas lawyers. — [texaslegal.guide](https://texaslegal.guide/specialties/); [Broden Mickelsen, Texas Board Certification](https://www.brodenmickelsen.com/?p=10400)
- TCDLA membership: TCDLA's own pages give "3200 members" (older About page) and "3800 members" (Past Presidents & History page); neither is dated. — [TCDLA About](https://tcdla.com/TCDLA/About_TCDLA/Criminal); [TCDLA Past Presidents & History](https://www.tcdla.com/TCDLA/Past_Presidents/Past_Presidents___History.aspx)
- State Bar of Texas Appellate Section: any State Bar member may join by paying dues; no published headcount found. — [Appellate Section Bylaws (Sept 2012)](https://www.tex-app.org/Content/AppellateBylaws_September2012.pdf)
- **Harris County Public Defender (HCPD) Appellate Division**: at launch (2011) it had "a division chief, ten assistant public defenders, and support staff" and was projected to handle "roughly 350 appeals per year." — [Harris County PDO brochure (Dec 2011), via Texas CJE](https://texascje.org/system/files/publications/Harris%20Co%20PDO%20Brochure%20%28Dec%202011%29.pdf)
- The 2013 CSG Justice Center evaluation found the HCPD Appellate Division "was staffed for 275 cases, but had 206 cases assigned in FY 2012," and that its reversal rate was about 5% vs. roughly 3% for criminal appeals generally in the 1st and 14th Courts of Appeals (ratio of cases reversed to cases heard). — [CSG Justice Center, Evaluation of the Harris County Public Defender (2013)](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5b4fe5fb8f5130e4eec43cec/t/5fa2d4f922736d2f034e9add/1604506891704/HCPDO+2013+report.pdf)
- HCPD self-reports that it "was much more successful in reversing or reforming cases in the Texas appellate courts than other lawyers." — [HCPDO Progress Summary](https://hcpdo.org/documents/5/PDO-Progress-Summary.pdf)
- A Harris County budget document cites a TIDC weighted-caseload standard of 31 appeals per attorney per year, treating one appeal as roughly 4.13 felonies; an HCPD overview from the same era cites a national standard of 25 appeals per attorney per year. — [Harris County Budget Book, Public Defender (Day 1, Jan 11)](https://budget.harriscountytx.gov/doc/Budget/budgetbook/Day-1-Jan-11/9-Public-Defender.pdf)
- Harris County scale (all indigent defense, not just appeals): in 2025, more than 88,000 indigent defendants were appointed counsel, approximately 17% of whom got public (PDO) attorneys; the rest went to private assigned counsel. — [Community Impact (Mar 24, 2026)](https://communityimpact.com/houston/spring-klein/government/2026/03/24/harris-county-looks-to-increase-capacity-of-public-defenders-assigned-counsel-in-criminal-courts/)
- Public defenders' share of Harris County cases "has stalled around 15 percent even though the number of cases they handled more than doubled between 2019 and 2023." — [Houston Landing](https://www.houstonlanding.org/the-harris-county-public-defenders-office-is-expanding-but-is-it-growing-fast-enough/)
- **Dallas County PD**: has five divisions (Criminal, Mental Health, Appellate, DNA, Capital). The Capital Murder Division (est. 2013) handles capital cases "through all phases of trial and direct appeal" with "a team of three trial attorneys, an appellate attorney, two investigators, and a legal assistant"; the office represents clients "in the intermediate courts of appeal, the Court of Criminal Appeals in Austin, and the U.S. Supreme Court." — [Dallas County PD, Capital](https://www.dallascounty.org/government/public-defender/our-services/capital.php); [Law Offices of Robert Gregg blog (secondary)](https://gregglawdallas.com/how-do-dallas-countys-5-public-defender-divisions-criminal-mental-health-appellate-dna-capital-impact-your-defense-strategy/)

- **Bexar County PD**: Appellate Division is one of four departments; it "handles the appeal of cases from all courts in Bexar County," with appointments from misdemeanor, criminal district, and juvenile courts. — [Bexar County Public Defender's Office](https://www.bexar.org/1041/Public-Defenders-Office)
- **Regional Public Defender for Capital Cases (Lubbock)**: trial-level only — "represents only indigent defendants charged with a capital case where the death penalty is sought at the trial level"; admin and trial office in Lubbock, satellites in Austin, Dallas, Pearland, San Angelo, San Antonio. (So it is NOT a direct-appeal persona.) — [RPDO About Us](https://rpdo.org/about-us/); [RPDO Offices & Staff](https://rpdo.org/offices-staff/)
- **State Counsel for Offenders → Independent Office of Inmate Counsel (IOIC)**: TDCJ's site says "the State Counsel for Offenders (SCFO) name has changed to the Independent Office of Inmate Counsel (IOIC)." Its Appellate Section: "If a client represented by the Criminal Defense Section is convicted of a crime, attorneys in the Appellate Section will pursue an appeal"; it "will prepare briefs and may argue the case before the appropriate appellate courts," and also handles appeals from sexually-violent-predator civil commitment cases. — [TDCJ IOIC Appellate Section](https://tdcj.texas.gov/tbcj/ioic/appellate_section.html); [TDCJ IOIC index](https://tdcj.texas.gov/tbcj/scfo/index.html)
- A 2017 State Bar committee review said SCFO was organized into four sections: Criminal Defense, Civil Defense, Appellate, and Legal Services. — [State Bar of Texas Legal Services to the Poor in Criminal Matters Committee review of SCFO (2017), via Prison Legal News](https://www.prisonlegalnews.org/news/publications/state-bar-texas-legal-services-poor-criminal-matters-committee-review-operations-state-counsel-offenders-2017/)
- TIDC publishes a "Public Defender Primer" and a "Managed Indigent Defense Systems" chart (last updated Oct 2024) listing all Texas PD and managed assigned counsel offices; El Paso County PD was established in 1987. — [TIDC Public Defender Primer](https://www.tidc.texas.gov/media/txbl01p2/public-defender-primer.pdf); [TIDC Managed Indigent Defense Systems chart (Oct 2024)](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/ipnozjdc/managed-indigent-defense-systems-chart.pdf)
- Volume of the appellate market (demand side): Second Court of Appeals (Fort Worth) FY2024: 470 new criminal cases filed/added, 395 criminal cases disposed. — [2nd COA FY2024 docket statistics](https://test.txcourts.gov/courts/appellate-courts/second-court-of-appeals-fort-worth-texas/about-the-court/docket-statistics/fy-2024/)
- Eighth Court of Appeals (El Paso) gets about 45 criminal filings a year, the fewest of any Texas court of appeals (secondary, unverified). — [Bennett & Bennett](https://bennettandbennett.com/?p=15108)
- Court of Criminal Appeals FY2024: 876 PDRs filed from the courts of appeals; 47 granted, 707 refused, 10 struck, 3 untimely, 2 dismissed; granted share of disposed PDRs 5.9%. (The same report shows a 955 total-petition figure and a 54-granted figure in another table; these do not reconcile cleanly—check the PDF.) FY2025 report lists 134 granted PDRs in its main table. CCA FY2024 total matters 7,431, including about 2,345 habeas applications. — [CCA Annual Report FY24](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1459678/cca-annual-reports-fy24.pdf); [CCA Annual Report FY25](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1462062/cca-annual-reports-fy25.pdf)
- Older context: in FY2015 the CCA granted discretionary review "less than 8 percent of the time" and mandatory matters were 80% of its caseload. — [OCA 2015 Annual Statistical Report](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1308021/2015-AR-Statistical-PRINT.pdf)

### Inferences
- Combining the HCPD figures (275-case staffing for ~11 attorneys ≈ 25/attorney) with the TIDC 31-appeals weighted standard, a reasonable planning assumption is 20–30 appeals per full-time PD appellate attorney per year (estimate).
- Private appointed appellate counsel in Harris County handle the large majority of indigent appeals (PDO is ~15–17% of all indigent appointments, though its appellate share may differ).
- The board-certified population (~130–200) is small enough that a product aimed only at specialists has a tiny TAM; the broader addressable group is the appointed-counsel lists in each county plus PD appellate divisions.

## Key Question 2: Economics (appointed fee schedules, TIDC data, private retainers, record costs)

### Takeaway
Appointed appellate pay in Texas is set county-by-county and ranges from flat fees of ~$2,000 (Travis, Collin "other appeal") to hourly rates with caps ($50–$125/hr capped at $1,800 in Tarrant; $60–$120/hr capped at $4,500 in the 81st/218th districts; up to $6,500 non-capital / $15,000 capital for "Appeals and PDRs" in Bexar), with Harris County paying hourly with no presumptive maximums since a Feb. 2023 schedule change. Private retainer ranges for criminal appeals specifically were not found in any reliable source.

### Cited Findings
**Harris County**
- Harris County District Courts fee schedule effective Feb. 27, 2023: appointed attorneys "are paid on an hourly basis and have no presumptive maximums. Hours are billed in six-minute increments rounded to the nearest such increment." Visible hourly rates: Capital 1st chair $225, Capital 2nd chair $200, First-degree $175. The schedule includes a post-conviction "Appeal" category, but the appeal rate was not visible in the snippet. — [Harris District Court Attorney Fee Schedule (TIDC-hosted)](https://tidc.tamu.edu/IDPlanDocuments/Harris/Harris%20District%20Court%20Attorney%20Fee%20Schedule.pdf)
- An older, undated Justex-hosted Harris County district-court schedule has a post-conviction section "Appeal, Preparation of Motion for New Trial" listing $40/hour with a $600/case total (garbled snippet; date unclear—do not rely on as current). — [Justex: Attorney Fee Schedule, Harris County District Courts Trying Criminal Cases](https://www.justex.net/JustexDocuments/0/FDAMS/Appointed%20Attorney%20Fee%20Schedule.pdf)
- The 2023 change let attorneys "bill not only for the time they spend arguing the case before a judge but for their work on cases outside of the courthouse," and costs "have ballooned" since. — [Houston Chronicle, "Court-appointed lawyers earn millions in Harris County as costs soar"](https://www.houstonchronicle.com/politics/houston/article/court-appointed-attorney-cost-harris-county-22401060.php)
- Harris County Auditor review (FY2023–FY2025): court-appointed attorney fees $78.3M (FY2023), $92.4M (FY2024), $112.0M (FY2025); private appointed attorneys are paid per the district-court indigent defense plan fee schedule (Appendices B and C). — [Harris County Auditor, Indigent Defense Attorney Billing Review FY2023–2025](https://auditor.harriscountytx.gov/portals/auditor/documents/AuditDocs/081426Indigent%20Defense%20Attorney%20Billing%20Review%20Fiscal%20Years%202023%20through%202025.pdf)
- Harris County family-code appointed-counsel claim form (not criminal) lists a flat "APPEALS $3,000.00" line — useful only as a county benchmark. — [Harris County District Clerk Form 40-2](https://www.hcdistrictclerk.com/common/Forms/pdf/0040-2_ClaimAgainstHCforAttFeesUnderFamCode.pdf)

**Travis County**
- Travis County Felony Fee Guidelines (effective May 1, 2025; rates apply to assignments on/after April 1, 2024): hourly Felony A (1st degree / punishment >20 yrs) $155; Felony B (2nd/3rd degree) $125; Felony C (state jail) $85. Flat fees include: Appeal (1st, 2nd, 3rd degree and state jail felonies) $2,000; "Anders brief or motion to revoke/adjudicate" $1,000; oral argument on appeal $200. Itemized statements must show date, service, and time to the tenth of an hour; the Capital Area Private Defender Service (CAPDS) determines whether actions were reasonable and necessary. — [Travis County Felony Fee Schedule 05-01-25](https://www.traviscountytx.gov/images/courts/Docs/2025_Felony_Fee_Schedule_05-01-25.pdf); [TIDC-hosted Travis fee schedule](https://tidc.tamu.edu/IDPlanDocuments/Travis/Travis%20District%20and%20County%20Court%20Attorney%20Fee%20Schedule.pdf)

**Bexar County**
- Bexar County felony voucher form (05-01-2023) has an "Appeals and PDRs" category with not-to-exceed amounts: Capital $15,000; Other felonies $6,500; hourly rates apply within the cap; disputes may go to a peer review committee. The 2025 voucher guidelines say "a copy of your brief must be attached to your voucher for payment on an appeal." Misdemeanor "Appeal to the 4th Court of Appeals" is a $750 flat fee (2023 order). — [Bexar County Felony Voucher Form 05-01-2023](https://www.bexar.org/DocumentCenter/View/38126/Felony-Voucher-Form-05-01-2023); [Bexar Fee Schedule for Vouchers 2025](https://elections.bexar.org/DocumentCenter/View/21761/Fee-Schedule-for-Vouchers-2025-PDF); [Bexar Special Order 2023-SO-11](https://www.bexar.org/DocumentCenter/View/39431/Special-Order-2023-SO-11-10-9-23)

**Tarrant County**
- Tarrant County fee schedule (appointments on/after 10/15/25): appeals at $50–$125 per hour, not to exceed $1,800. Counsel appointed before March 20, 2024 are paid under the schedule in effect at appointment. — [Tarrant County Court Attorney Fee Schedule (TIDC-hosted)](https://tidc.tamu.edu/IDPlanDocuments/Tarrant/Tarrant%20County%20Court%20Attorney%20Fee%20Schedule.pdf)
- Tarrant County reporter's-record rate order effective Dec. 15, 2025 (under TRAP 20.2 context): $6.00 per page for original and two copies; $2.00 per additional copy. — [Tarrant County local rules attachment via topics.txcourts.gov](https://topics.txcourts.gov/LocalRulesPublic/PreviewAttachment/2737)

**Collin County and other comparators**
- Collin County felony schedule: "Appeal from trial: $3,500" and "Other appeal: $2,000," with discretionary adjustments capped per case. — [Collin County Felony Criminal Case Fee Schedule](https://www.collincountytx.gov/Courts/Indigent-Defense/Documents/CurrentFelonyCriminalCaseFeeSchedule.pdf)
- 81st/218th Judicial Districts (Atascosa, Frio, Karnes, La Salle, Wilson): 2025 schedule sets appeal to the Court of Appeals at $60–$120 per hour, maximum $4,500; the 2019 supplement had $50/hour capped at $2,500 for felony/juvenile appeals. — [81st/218th 2025 All-Counties Fee Schedule](https://81-218.txcourts.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025-02-25-ALL-Counties-Fee-Schedule-v1.pdf); [81st/218th 2019 fee supplement](https://81-218.txcourts.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2019-12-20-fee-supplement-to-indigent-defense-plan-updated.pdf)
- Tom Green County: for appeals, "The Court will determine the appropriate hourly rate based upon the complexity of the case and the experience and ability of the appointed attorney." — [Tom Green County attorney fee schedule](https://www.votetomgreencounty.gov/upload/page/0082/docs/AttyFeeSch.pdf)
- An unidentified Texas local rule sets capital appeals at $100/hour, not to exceed $15,000. — [topics.txcourts.gov local rules attachment 926](https://topics.txcourts.gov/LocalRulesPublic/PreviewAttachment/926)
- 38th District (Uvalde/Real): appeals are "paid on a fixed-fee basis"; if the trial judge disapproves a requested fee, written findings are required and the lawyer may appeal by motion to the Presiding Judge of the Administrative Judicial Region. — [topics.txcourts.gov local rules attachment 926](https://topics.txcourts.gov/LocalRulesPublic/PreviewAttachment/926)
- Statewide fee-dispute mechanism: under CCP art. 26.05 the presiding judge of the administrative judicial region reviews disapproved fees; the attorney's motion must attach the disapproval order and the county fee schedule and explain the departure. — [TxCourts "Rules Concerning Appeal of Attorney's Fee"](https://www.TxCourts.gov/media/1437064/rules-concerning-appeal-of-attorneys-fee.pdf); [Rules for Appeal of Trial Court's Disapproval of Requested Court Appointed Counsel Fees](https://www.TxCourts.gov/media/614232/Rules_for_Appeal_of_Trial_Courts_Disapproval_of_Requested_Court_Appointed_Counsel_Fees.pdf)
- AG opinion: so long as a court complies with the minimum fee schedule it has discretion over what is a "reasonable fee" for appointed counsel. — [Tex. Att'y Gen. Op. JM-537 (1986)](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/opinion-files/opinion/1986/jm0537.pdf)
- Fee-schedule index: TIDC maintains a statewide index of every county's attorney fee schedule PDFs. — [TIDC Fee Schedules index](https://tidc.tamu.edu/public.net/Reports/FeeDocuments.aspx)

**TIDC data**
- TIDC collects Indigent Defense Expenditure Reports (IDER) from all 254 counties under Gov't Code §79.036(e); the public data site has a County Indigent Defense Expenditures section and statewide expenditure report by fiscal year. — [TIDC Submit Data Reports](https://www.tidc.texas.gov/oversight/submit-data-reports); [Indigent Defense Data for Texas](https://tidc.tamu.edu/public.net/Reports/StateFinancialReport.aspx)
- TIDC conducted a Harris County managed assigned counsel (MAC) study recommending a unified MAC. — [TIDC Harris County MAC Study](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/puobdlhz/harris-county-mac-study.pdf)

**Record (transcript) costs**
- Indigent appellants: under TRAP 20.2, "within the time for perfecting the appeal, an appellant who is unable to pay for the appellate record may, by motion and affidavit, ask the trial court to have the appellate record furnished without charge." The indigence test is whether a preponderance shows the party could not pay "if he really wanted to and made a good faith effort to do so"; the appellant bears the burden at a contest hearing. — [FindLaw, Texas Court of Appeals opinion](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/tx-court-of-appeals/1353648.html); [Broden & Mickelsen sample Motion for Free Appellate Transcript](https://www.brodenmickelsen.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Motion-for-Free-Appellate-Transcript.pdf)
- The court reporter "is responsible for preparing, certifying, and timely filing the reporter's record" once the appellant has taken required steps including payment; one court ordered an appellant to pay or arrange payment within 15 days or face dismissal (2003 case). — [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/tx-court-of-appeals/1353648.html)
- Per-page rates are set locally (e.g., Tarrant $6.00/page original + 2 copies, effective Dec. 15, 2025). A secondary source says reporters typically charge $4–$7 per page depending on turnaround (general estimate, not authoritative). — [Tarrant order](https://topics.txcourts.gov/LocalRulesPublic/PreviewAttachment/2737); [LegalClarity (secondary)](https://legalclarity.org/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-deadlines-and-briefs/)
- AG opinion KP-0163 (2017) addresses who bears the cost of an extra copy of the criminal reporter's record. — [Tex. Att'y Gen. Op. KP-0163](https://ezel.ai/ag-opinions/tx/kp-0163)

**Private market**
- No source found that prices a Texas criminal *appeal* specifically. Trial-level benchmarks: hourly fees "may be in the $250–$350 range"; retainers of "at least a couple thousand dollars" at $250/hr; one firm says to "anticipate spending at least several thousand dollars in legal fees." — [Joseph Ruiz Law, criminal defense attorney cost in Texas](https://www.josephruizlaw.com/blog/criminal-defense-attorney-cost-in-texas/); [Bryan Fagan (Oct 2024)](https://www.bryanfagan.com/2024/10/how-much-does-it-cost-to-hire-a-criminal-defense-attorney/); [TAD Law, financial costs of fighting criminal charges](https://tadlaw.com/blog/financial-costs-of-fighting-criminal-charges/)

### Inferences
- At a $2,000 flat fee (Travis) and a conservative 40–60 hours per felony appeal (record review plus brief), effective rates are ~$33–$50/hour — far below the $125–$175/hour trial rates in the same counties. This is the single strongest economic fact for a productivity tool: appointed appellate counsel are paid flat or capped and so directly capture any time savings (estimate; hours assumption is mine, not sourced).
- Bexar's "attach your brief to the voucher" rule and Travis's tenth-of-an-hour itemization mean the appellate lawyer's billing artifact is the brief itself plus a time ledger; a tool that auto-generates the itemized time statement from work performed would fit existing voucher workflows.
- Harris County's uncapped hourly model (since 2023) is the outlier; the auditor's FY23–25 billing review signals scrutiny of hours, so time-tracking defensibility matters there.

### Gaps
- Harris County's current appellate hourly rate (post-Feb. 2023) could not be read from the schedule snippet.
- Dallas County district-court appellate fee: the TIDC index lists a "Dallas District Court Attorney Fee Schedule" PDF, but it could not be opened.
- Appellate-specific line items in TIDC's statewide expenditure data: not confirmed to exist as a separate category.
- Private retainer ranges for criminal appeals and PDRs in Texas: no reliable source found; the report writer should mark this as unknown or seek practitioner interviews.
- Reporter's-record per-page rates for Harris, Dallas, Travis, Bexar: not found (only Tarrant).

## Key Question 3: The workflow and its clock (TRAP deadlines, Anders, PDR, rehearing, what "reviewing the record" involves)

### Takeaway
The clock runs from sentencing: MNT within 30 days (overruled by operation of law at 75 days), notice of appeal within 30 days (90 if MNT), record due 60 days (120 with MNT), appellant's brief 30 days after the later-filed record (extensions routinely granted), then PDR 30 days after the court of appeals judgment (or rehearing denial). Record review is an audit for preserved error under TRAP 33.1 followed by harm analysis under TRAP 44.2.

### Cited Findings
**Motion for new trial (TRAP 21)**
- Rule 21.4: "The defendant may file a motion for new trial before, but no later than 30 days after, the date when the trial court imposes or suspends sentence in open court." Amended motions may be filed without leave within that same 30 days, before the court overrules any preceding motion. — [Williams v. State, PD-0870-18 (Tex. Crim. App. June 24, 2020)](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1447952/pd-0870-18-williams-opinion-06242020.pdf); [Ruledex TRAP 26](https://ruledex.com/?p=23704)
- Rule 21.8 (secondary source): the motion must be presented within 10 days of filing; the hearing must occur within 75 days of sentencing; "if the court does not rule within 75 days of the imposition of the sentence, the motion for new trial is overruled by operation of law"; the defendant must request an evidentiary hearing in the motion. — [Dallas Criminal Defense Lawyer Blog, Texas Criminal Appeals Deadlines](https://dallascriminaldefenselawyerblog.com/texas-criminal-appeals-deadlin); [Bennett & Bennett, "First 30 Days After a Criminal Conviction"](https://bennettandbennett.com/?p=15023)

**Notice of appeal (TRAP 26.2)**
- Notice of appeal is due "(1) within 30 days after the day sentence is imposed or suspended in open court, or after the day the trial court enters an appealable order; or (2) within 90 days after the day sentence is imposed or suspended in open court if the defendant timely files a motion for new trial." The 90 days run from sentencing, not from the MNT ruling. — [Williams v. State (Tex. Crim. App. 2020)](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1447952/pd-0870-18-williams-opinion-06242020.pdf); [state-rules.com TRAP 26.2](https://state-rules.com/texas/appellate/26.2)
- TRAP 26.3 allows a 15-day extension window for the notice of appeal if the notice and a motion for extension are both filed within 15 days after the deadline. [rule text from training knowledge; verify at TRAP PDF](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457526/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

**Record (TRAP 35)**
- TRAP 35.2 (text marked as amended Jan. 1, 2026): in a criminal case the record is due within 60 days after sentence is imposed/suspended or the appealed order is signed; within 120 days after sentencing if a timely MNT is filed and denied; 60 days after the order granting an MNT. — [Ruledex TRAP 35](https://ruledex.com/texas/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure/section-two-appeals-from-trial-court-judgments-and-orders/rule-35-time-to-file-record-responsibility-for-filing-record/); [TxCourts appellate timetable for reporters](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/208077/appellatetimetable-reporters.pdf); [TxCourts trial clerks and reporters appellate timetable (2018 rev.)](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1442843/trial-clerks-and-reporters-appellate-timetable-2018-revised.pdf)
- TRAP 35.3(c): the appellate court may extend the record deadline on request of the clerk or reporter; each extension capped at 30 days (10 in accelerated appeals); one court's guidance says it generally grants no more than two extensions for good cause. — [Ruledex TRAP 35](https://ruledex.com/?p=23714); [TxCourts reporter's request for extension form](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/200988/reportersrequestforextension.pdf)

**Briefs (TRAP 38)**
- TRAP 38.6(a): appellant's brief due within 30 days (20 in accelerated appeals) after the later of the clerk's record or reporter's record filing date; appellee's brief 30 days after appellant's brief; reply brief within 20 days after appellee's brief. A 2026 Eighth Court order applied this ("brief is due 30 days after the reporter's record is filed," citing 38.6(a)(2)). — [state-rules.com TRAP 38.6](https://www.state-rules.com/texas/appellate/38.6); [Tucker v. State (Tex. App.—El Paso 2026) via Leagle](https://www.leagle.com/decision/intxco20260728589)
- TRAP 38.6(d): a motion to extend brief time may be filed before or after the due date and must comply with Rule 10.5(b). — [state-rules.com TRAP 38.6](https://www.state-rules.com/texas/appellate/38.6)
- Practitioner commentary (TDCAA forum, informal): the 30-day brief deadline "has become loose in practice" because courts accept most explanations for more time in criminal cases; the 11th Court of Appeals reportedly will not accept "counsel is busy" alone. — [TDCAA Community forum, "Busy Attorneys Must Prioritize"](https://tdcaa.infopop.net/eve/forums/a/tpc/f/6050918821/m/7577028516)

**Preservation and harm**
- TRAP 33.1 requires the record to show a timely request, objection, or motion stating the grounds with sufficient specificity, and that the trial court ruled or refused to rule; "an issue not raised at trial generally cannot be raised on appeal absent fundamental-error doctrine," so appellate counsel should "start by auditing the record for preserved error." — [Pullan & Young, "Texas Criminal Appeals: strategic thinking on error preservation"](https://www.pullanyoung.com/blog/texas-criminal-appeals-strategic-thinking-on-error-preservation); [TDCAA-hosted TRAP 2019](https://www.tdcaa.com/wp-content/uploads/TDCAA-TRAP-2019.pdf); [Ordiway PLLC, "The Power of Preservation"](https://ordiway.com/the-power-of-preservation-protecting-your-rights-for-appeal-even-before-the-verdict/)
- "Reversible error is not automatic": courts weigh the standard of review, preservation, and harm. — [DeGuerin & Dickson, "What Is a Criminal Appeal in Texas?"](https://www.deguerin.com/what-is-a-criminal-appeal-in-texas-a-step-by-step-guide/)
- TRAP 44.2(a): constitutional error requires reversal unless the court determines beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the conviction or punishment; 44.2(b): other errors "that do not affect substantial rights must be disregarded." [rule text from training knowledge; verify at TRAP PDF](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457526/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

**Anders procedure**
- Kelly v. State, 436 S.W.3d 313 (Tex. Crim. App. 2014): once appointed counsel files a motion to withdraw with an Anders brief, counsel "has a duty ... to assist the appellant in filing a motion in the court of appeals for access to the appellate record"; the court of appeals must ensure the appellant has record access before ruling on the Anders brief and withdrawal so the appellant can decide whether to file a pro se response. The court of appeals in Kelly erred by granting withdrawal and declaring the appeal frivolous without verifying record access. — [Kelly v. State (vLex)](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/kelly-v-state-no-891562739); [Casemine commentary on Kelly](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/ensuring-appellant-access-to-appellate-records-in-anders-appeals:-insights-from-syl-v.-kelly/view)
- Post-Kelly checklist courts apply: counsel (1) notified the appellant of the motion and Anders brief and provided copies; (2) informed the appellant of the right to review the record and file a pro se response (citing Kelly at 319–20); (3) provided "a form motion for pro se access to the appellate record that only requires appellant's signature and date." Courts note when no pro se response is filed. — [Tex. App.—Eastland opinions via FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/crt-app-tex-eas/1885232.html); [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/crt-app-tex-eas/1871290.html)
- The Anders brief must show counsel's withdrawal rests on "a conscientious and thorough review of the law and facts"; Kelly relied on In re Schulman, 252 S.W.3d 403 (Tex. Crim. App. 2008). — [Kelly v. State (vLex)](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/kelly-v-state-no-891562739)
- Travis County pays a $1,000 flat fee for an Anders brief vs. $2,000 for a merits appeal. — [Travis County Felony Fee Schedule 05-01-25](https://www.traviscountytx.gov/images/courts/Docs/2025_Felony_Fee_Schedule_05-01-25.pdf)

**PDR, rehearing, mandate**
- The CCA's filing checklist lists "Length of Petition – Rule 9.4(i)(2)(D) 4,500 words or 15 pages." — [CCA Checklist for Filings (rev. eff. 7/17/2015)](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/208125/checklist-for-filings-in-cca-revised-eff-07172015.pdf)
- TRAP 68.4 contents (as retrievable): (a) identity of judge, parties, and counsel (trial and appellate, with addresses), addressed to the "Court of Criminal Appeals of Texas"; (b) table of contents with page references indicating the subject of each ground; (c) alphabetical index of authorities; grounds for review "stated briefly, without argument," separately numbered, with record page cites where petitioner has record access (questions presented may be used instead); argument section must be "direct, concise," with authorities, amplifying the Rule 66.3 reasons for review; prayer for relief. (One source marked "amended Jan. 1, 2026" showed only (a)–(c) in snippet; verify current lettering.) — [Ruledex TRAP 68](https://ruledex.com/texas/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure/section-five-proceedings-in-the-court-of-criminal-appeals/rule-68-discretionary-review-with-petition/); [state-rules.com TRAP 68.4](https://state-rules.com/texas/appellate/68.4); [courtrules.net TRAP 68](https://www.courtrules.net/texas/texas-appellate-procedure/rule-68)
- TRAP 68.2: PDR must be filed within 30 days after the court of appeals' final ruling on all timely motions for rehearing or en banc reconsideration, or after the judgment if none; 68.2(c) permits extension by motion filed within 15 days after the deadline. TRAP 49.1: motion for rehearing in the court of appeals due within 15 days after judgment. TRAP 18.1: mandate issues after the time for PDR expires (or after the CCA's final disposition). [rule text from training knowledge; verify at TRAP PDF](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457526/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)
- The State Prosecuting Attorney has published practitioner papers on PDRs (UT CLE 2015; Advanced Criminal Law Course 2013). — [SPA PDR paper (UT CLE 2015)](https://www.spa.texas.gov/media/w2epllda/pdr-paper-ut-cle-2015.docx); [SPA 2013 Advanced Course paper](https://www.spa.texas.gov/media/nhfb3ch2/2013advancedcoursepaper.pdf)
- Grant rates: FY2024 CCA granted 47 of 876 court-of-appeals PDRs (5.9% of disposed). — [CCA Annual Report FY24](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1459678/cca-annual-reports-fy24.pdf)
- Secondary: the CCA grants PDRs from the Eighth Court at about 2.8% (unverified). — [Bennett & Bennett](https://bennettandbennett.com/?p=15108)

**Post-affirmance duty (the PDR-notice trap)**
- Ex parte Wilson, 956 S.W.2d 25 (Tex. Crim. App. 1997): "for counsel to render effective assistance on appeal, he, or she, must notify the client the case has been affirmed, and that the client can pursue discretionary review on his own." "If appellate counsel's action or inaction denies a defendant his opportunity to prepare and file a petition for discretionary review, that defendant has been denied his sixth amendment right to effective assistance of counsel." Remedy: habeas relief granting an out-of-time PDR, with the applicant required to file within 30 days after the CCA's mandate issues. Relief has been granted where counsel notified the client of affirmance but "did not advise him that he could file a pro se petition for discretionary review," and where counsel "was not notified of the affirmance until after the mandate had issued." — [CCA unpublished opinions 74649, 74745, 74816, 74546, 74569 (txcourts.gov archives)](https://txcourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/ccaInformation/archives/Opinions-Non%20Published/74649.htm); [FindLaw CCA opinion](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/tx-court-of-appeals/92096.html)

**Timeline**
- One firm's step-by-step guide: the appellate lawyer "reviews the record for potentially reversible error and determines which issues can properly be presented"; appeals "are won on the record that already exists." — [DeGuerin & Dickson](https://www.deguerin.com/what-is-a-criminal-appeal-in-texas-a-step-by-step-guide/); [Bennett & Bennett, Texas Criminal Appeals Lawyer](https://bennettandbennett.com/texas-criminal-appeal-lawyer)

### Inferences
- The hard, non-extendable deadlines (notice of appeal; MNT filing; PDR 30-day + 15-day grace) are few; the soft, routinely-extended deadline is the brief. A product's calendaring should distinguish jurisdictional from discretionary deadlines and chain them from the sentencing date and the record-filed date.
- Because MNT and notice-of-appeal windows overlap in the first 30 days after sentencing and are often handled by trial counsel before appellate counsel is appointed, the appellate lawyer frequently inherits a case at day 30–90 with the record not yet filed; the real "work window" opens when the reporter's record lands (day 60–120+ with extensions).
- The Wilson/Kelly line of cases creates two mandatory client-communication artifacts (the post-affirmance PDR-rights letter; the Anders notice packet with form motion for record access) that are checklist candidates for software.

### Gaps
- Exact current text of TRAP 21.8, 26.3, 44.2, 49.1, 68.2, 18.1 could not be read this session (rules PDF blocked); items flagged above are from training knowledge.
- No empirical data found on median time from notice of appeal to court-of-appeals judgment in Texas criminal cases, or on extension frequency (the 2013 HCPD evaluation has a table on extensions filed Feb 2011–Aug 2012 but figures were not retrievable).
- No data found on the share of Texas criminal appeals disposed via Anders briefs.

## Key Question 4: Inputs — how they receive the record, and typical volume

### Takeaway
Attorneys of record download the clerk's record and reporter's record as PDFs from the TAMES Attorney Portal (attorneyportal.txcourts.gov) at every court of appeals and the CCA; sealed records and non-PDF exhibits (audio/video) are excluded and must be viewed at the court or obtained separately. Reporters' records follow the Uniform Format Manual, with text-searchable PDF exhibits.

### Cited Findings
- TAMES Attorney Portal: attorneys "register with their bar member number and a known TAMES email address in order to retrieve Appellate records for cases in which they are listed as an attorney of record"; records are downloadable "only as PDF files at first." — [12th COA, Welcome to the Attorney Portal](https://txcourts.gov/12thcoa/news/welcome-to-the-attorney-portal/); [13th COA](https://txcourts.gov/13thcoa/news/welcome-to-the-attorney-portal); [4th COA](https://txcourts.gov/4thcoa/news/welcome-to-the-attorney-portal)
- Retention: records available for 60 months from filing (Second Court ties it to "until mandate issues"). — [6th COA](https://txcourts.gov/6thcoa/news/welcome-to-the-attorney-portal/); [2nd COA Attorney Portal](https://txcourts.gov/2ndcoa/attorney-portal/)
- Limits: "Sealed records and non-PDF files, such as audio or video exhibits, are not available through the Attorney Portal"; "electronic exhibits must still be viewed at the court" (14th COA); pro se parties cannot use the portal; registrants agree not to post downloaded documents publicly and must protect sensitive information. — [14th COA Attorney Record Portal](https://txcourts.gov/14thcoa/news/attorney-record-portal/); [8th COA](https://txcourts.gov/8thcoa/news/welcome-to-the-attorney-portal/); [7th COA](https://txcourts.gov/7thcoa/news/welcome-to-the-attorney-portal/)
- CCA: "The new Attorney Portal provides secure, online access for appellate attorneys of record in a case to view case documents not available to the public." — [CCA, Welcome to the Attorney Portal](https://txcourts.gov/cca/news/welcome-to-the-attorney-portal)
- The Fifth Court (Dallas) piloted the online records system in 2016. — [Texas Bar Blog (Sept 2016)](https://blog.texasbar.com/2016/09/articles/news/fifth-court-of-appeals-launches-new-online-records-system/)
- Uniform Format Manual for Texas Reporters' Records: approved by Supreme Court Misc. Docket No. 10-9077, effective July 1, 2010; governs official and freelance reporters' records in paper and electronic form; sections cover Page Formatting (page size, margins, character spacing, font, format box), Exhibits, Volumes and Volume Numbering, and Section 8 "Official Reporter's Record in Electronic Format for Appellate Court." Non-compliance is grounds for discipline by the Court Reporters Certification Board. — [TxCourts Uniform Format Manual page](https://www.txcourts.gov/jbcc/court-reporters-certification/statutes-rules-and-resources-for-court-reporters-firms/uniform-format-manual/); [Misc. Docket 10-9077](https://www.txcourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/SupremeCourt/AdministrativeOrders/miscdocket/10/10907700.pdf); [Uniform Format Manual 2010-07-01 PDF](https://www.TxCourts.gov/media/244178/Uniform-Format-Manual-20100701.pdf)
- Exhibit guidelines (2025): documentary exhibits "are to be in text-searchable Portable Document Format (PDF), preferably by converting the document directly into a PDF file, but may be scanned if necessary"; photographs included as images (Manual §§ 8.3, 8.6); the 2025 guidance gives a 10 GB file-size limit for the appellate portal (the 2019 version said 100 MB); oversized exhibits must be split. — [2025 Guidelines for Reporters' Records Exhibits](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1461089/2025-guidelines-for-reporters-records-exhibits.pdf); [2019 Guidelines](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1443938/2019-courts-guidelines-for-reporters-records-exhibits-updated-2019.pdf)
- E-filing: attorneys must file through eFileTexas.gov (except sealed documents); TRAP 9.4(j)(1) requires an electronically filed document to be "in pdf text searchable format"; in criminal appeals, names of complainants and witnesses "should be redacted or replaced with aliases." — [12th COA, Attorneys & Unrepresented Parties Who eFile](https://txcourts.gov/12thcoa/practice-before-the-court/electronic-filing/attorneys-unrepresented-parties-who-efile/)
- InfoTrack and Harris County District Clerk publish eFileTexas how-to FAQs (secondary). — [InfoTrack eFiling in Texas](https://support.infotrack.com/1601498-efiling-in-texas); [Harris County District Clerk eFile FAQ (Oct 2024)](https://www.hcdistrictclerk.com/Common/Civil/EFileFAQ.aspx)

### Inferences
- The default input artifact for a Texas appellate lawyer is a set of per-volume PDFs (reporter's record volumes + clerk's record + exhibit volumes) pulled from TAMES, with audio/video exhibits as a separate, out-of-band problem.
- Because reporters' records are produced from CAT software under a uniform format (fixed page/line layout), page:line citation extraction from the PDF is feasible and is the citation unit briefs use ("RR vol. 3, p. 45").

## Key Question 5: Outputs — brief structure, TRAP 9.4 limits, PDR format, motions, tables

### Takeaway
A Texas appellant's brief is capped at 15,000 words (computer-generated) with a certificate of compliance that may rely on the word-processor count; a PDR and CCA rehearing motion are capped at 4,500 words; preliminary and concluding parts (tables, index of authorities, issues, signature, certificates, appendix) are excluded from the count. The PDR has a rigid prescribed structure under TRAP 68.4.

### Cited Findings
- TRAP 9.4(i): "A brief and response in an appellate court (other than a brief under subparagraph (A) [death-penalty direct appeal to the CCA]) and a petition and response in an original proceeding in the court of appeals: 15,000 words if computer-generated, and 50 pages if not." Petition for discretionary review and response in the CCA (and petition for review in the Supreme Court, and motions for rehearing): "4,500 words if computer-generated, and 15 pages if not." — [Supreme Court of Texas Misc. Docket 12-9129 (2012 amendments)](https://www.TxCourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/SupremeCourt/AdministrativeOrders/miscdocket/12/12912900.pdf); [CCA Checklist for Filings](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/208125/checklist-for-filings-in-cca-revised-eff-07172015.pdf)
- Certificate of compliance: "A computer-generated document must include a certificate by counsel or an unrepresented party stating the number of words in the document. The person certifying may rely on the word count of the computer program used to prepare the document." — [Misc. Docket 12-9129](https://www.TxCourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/SupremeCourt/AdministrativeOrders/miscdocket/12/12912900.pdf)
- Word limits replaced page limits effective Dec. 1, 2012, using a 300-words-per-page conversion; "the preliminary and concluding parts of the brief – tables, jurisdictional statements, issues, signatures, certificates, appendices, and the like – are excluded from the word count." — [Mondaq, "Words, Words, Words"](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/court-procedure/200838/words-words-words); [SCAC transcript June 2012](https://www.txcourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/SupremeCourtAdvisoryCommittee/Meetings/2012/transcripts/sc06232012.pdf)
- Current consolidated TRAP PDF (with amendments through Jan. 1, 2026) is published by OCA. — [Texas Rules of Appellate Procedure (txcourts.gov)](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457526/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf); [TxCourts Rules & Standards](https://www.txcourts.gov/rules-forms/rules-standards/)
- TRAP 38.1 required brief sections (identity of parties and counsel; table of contents; index of authorities; statement of the case; any statement regarding oral argument; issues presented; statement of facts "supported by record references"; summary of the argument; argument "with appropriate citations to authorities and to the record"; prayer; appendix in civil cases). [rule text from training knowledge; verify at TRAP PDF](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457526/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)
- PDR structure per TRAP 68.4: identity of judge/parties/counsel; table of contents; index of authorities; statement regarding oral argument; statement of the case; statement of procedural history; grounds for review (brief, unargued, numbered, with record cites); argument amplifying Rule 66.3 reasons; prayer; appendix (court of appeals opinion). — [Ruledex TRAP 68](https://ruledex.com/texas/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure/section-five-proceedings-in-the-court-of-criminal-appeals/rule-68-discretionary-review-with-petition/); [CCA Checklist](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/208125/checklist-for-filings-in-cca-revised-eff-07172015.pdf)
- Motions for extension of time to file brief: filed before or after the due date under TRAP 38.6(d), must comply with 10.5(b) (state the deadline, the length of extension sought, the facts relied on, and prior extensions). — [state-rules.com TRAP 38.6](https://www.state-rules.com/texas/appellate/38.6); example civil motion for reference — [Docket Alarm, 13th COA motion for extension](https://www.docketalarm.com/cases/Texas_State_13th_Court_of_Appeals/13-23-00272-CV/Novus_Prime_Properties_LLC_v._Space_Exploration_Technologies_Corp/08-01-23-Motion_for_extension_of_time_to_file_brief_filed-01/Motion-01/)
- Trial-level outputs in the appellate lawyer's window: motion for new trial (with request for evidentiary hearing), notice of appeal, motion/affidavit for free record (TRAP 20.2), request for reporter's record. — [Broden & Mickelsen sample motion for free transcript](https://www.brodenmickelsen.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Motion-for-Free-Appellate-Transcript.pdf); [Bennett & Bennett, first 30 days](https://bennettandbennett.com/?p=15023)
- Anders outputs: motion to withdraw; Anders brief demonstrating a conscientious review; client notice letter; form motion for pro se record access. — [Kelly v. State](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/kelly-v-state-no-891562739); [FindLaw Eastland opinions](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/crt-app-tex-eas/1885232.html)
- Post-affirmance output: client letter notifying of affirmance and right to file pro se PDR (Ex parte Wilson). — [CCA opinion 74649](https://txcourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/ccaInformation/archives/Opinions-Non%20Published/74649.htm)
- Billing outputs: Bexar requires the brief attached to the voucher; Travis requires itemized time to the tenth of an hour. — [Bexar Fee Schedule for Vouchers 2025](https://elections.bexar.org/DocumentCenter/View/21761/Fee-Schedule-for-Vouchers-2025-PDF); [Travis Felony Fee Schedule](https://www.traviscountytx.gov/images/courts/Docs/2025_Felony_Fee_Schedule_05-01-25.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because tables of contents/authorities are excluded from the word count but mandatory, and the certificate of compliance is a sworn-ish statement relying on the word processor, automatic generation of TOC/index of authorities and an accurate "countable words" calculation are concrete pain points.
- The statement of facts with record references and the argument with record cites are where record-digest tooling pays off; every factual sentence needs a "(RR vol. X at Y)" or "(CR at Z)" cite.

### Gaps
- No Texas source was retrieved describing the "record digest" / "issue-cite table" artifacts practitioners build (search budget exhausted); this is widely attested in appellate practice generally but unsourced here.
- TRAP 38.1 and 68.4 current lettering (post-Jan. 2026 amendments) not verified.

## Key Question 7: Ethics, UPL, and workload constraints (duty to review entire record, Anders, IAC of appellate counsel, performance guidelines)

### Takeaway
Texas appellate counsel operate under the State Bar's Performance Guidelines for Non-Capital Criminal Defense Representation (adopted 2011, updated Sept. 24, 2021), the Anders/Kelly duties, and the Wilson duty to notify clients of affirmance and pro se PDR rights; failures are routinely remedied by CCA habeas grants of out-of-time appeals/PDRs, which makes these duties the de facto minimum standard of care.

### Cited Findings
- Performance Guidelines: adopted by the State Bar of Texas Board of Directors Jan. 28, 2011; updates adopted Sept. 24, 2021; source committee: Standing Committee on Legal Services to the Poor in Criminal Matters; purposes: "to encourage defense attorneys to perform to a high standard of representation and to promote professionalism," and to "alert counsel to courses of action that may be necessary, advisable, or appropriate." — [Texas Bar Journal, April 2022 reprint](https://lsc-pagepro.mydigitalpublication.com/article/PERFORMANCE+GUIDELINES+FOR+NON-CAPITAL+CRIMINAL+DEFENSE+REPRESENTATION/4237140/741629/article.html)
- The national NLADA Performance Guidelines (2006) expressly exclude post-conviction and appellate duties, so Texas's appellate guideline is a Texas-specific addition. — [NLADA Performance Guidelines (2006)](https://www.nlada.org/defender-standards/performance-guidelines)
- TIDC's Future Indigent Defense Leaders guidelines were derived from the HCPD FACT program guidelines, themselves developed from the State Bar performance guidelines, and address continuing representation through appeal under CCP art. 26.04(j). — [TIDC FIDL Performance Guidelines](https://www.tidc.texas.gov/media/vbvji5ri/fidl_performance_guidelines.pdf)
- Anders/Kelly duties (record access assistance; notice; form motion) — see Key Question 3. — [Kelly v. State](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/kelly-v-state-no-891562739)
- Wilson duty (notify of affirmance + pro se PDR right) and out-of-time PDR remedy — see Key Question 3. — [CCA opinions](https://txcourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/ccaInformation/archives/Opinions-Non%20Published/74745.htm)
- Appointed-fee assessment against defendants requires a hearing and ability-to-pay finding (2016 appellate decision). — [Prison Legal News (June 2017)](https://www.prisonlegalnews.org/news/2017/jun/14/hearing-required-assessment-appointed-attorney-fees-texas/)
- Workload benchmarks: TIDC weighted-caseload standard of 31 appeals per attorney per year (1 appeal ≈ 4.13 felonies); national standard of 25 appeals per attorney per year (as cited in Harris County documents). — [Harris County Budget Book, Public Defender](https://budget.harriscountytx.gov/doc/Budget/budgetbook/Day-1-Jan-11/9-Public-Defender.pdf)
- Comparable out-of-state standards (for benchmarking only): Wisconsin SPD Appellate Division standards direct counsel to "thoroughly review the complete circuit court record, all relevant transcripts." — [Wisconsin SPD Minimum Attorney Performance Standards, Appellate Division (2025)](https://www.wispd.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Minimum-Attorney-Performance-Standards-Appellate-Division.pdf); New York ILS Appellate Standards (2023) — [NY ILS Appellate Standards](https://www.ils.ny.gov/files/Appellate%20Standards%20Final%20100423.pdf); California ADI Guidebook (2024) — [ADI Guidebook](https://adi-sandiego.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/ADI-Guidebook-May-2024-1.pdf); Federal Defender appellate standards — [Chen/DSAG Standards](https://waw.fd.org/sites/waw/files/Chen-PMWG%20DSAG%20Standards%20for%20Federal%20Appellate%20Practice.pdf)

### Inferences
- The "duty to review the entire record" in Texas is enforced less by a single rule than by the Anders standard ("conscientious and thorough review of the law and facts") and the IAC-on-appeal remedy; any tool that summarizes a record must not displace counsel's own reading, or it creates Anders/IAC exposure. A product should position as augmenting review (indexing, flagging objections/rulings) rather than substituting for it.
- Guideline-driven checklists (Anders packet, Wilson letter, record-access motion) are low-risk, high-value automation.
