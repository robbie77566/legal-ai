# Tools Landscape: Criminal Appellate and Post-Conviction (Habeas/Writ) Attorneys, with a Texas Focus (as of October 2026)

Method note: All findings below come from web search results gathered 2026-10-18. Vendor pricing pages (litsoftware.com, clearbrief.com, typelaw.com, everlaw.com, logikcull.com, clio.com, briefpoint.ai, briefcatch.com, thomsonreuters.com help pages, txcourts.gov) could NOT be fetched directly from this environment (DNS/proxy blocks), so prices are labeled "official" only where the search result itself identified the vendor's own page as the source; everything else is "reported" with the reporting source and date where known. Many of the price figures come from competitor comparison blogs (Spellbook, Vaquill, CostBench, Fast.io), which should be treated as low-reliability unless corroborated. The web-search budget for this session ran out before a final round of verification, so several items are left as explicit gaps rather than guessed.

## Key Question 1: Legal research and AI assistants (Westlaw/CoCounsel, Lexis+/Protégé, vLex Vincent, Harvey, Clearbrief, Briefpoint, TypeLaw, BriefCatch, Casetext, Paxton) — what they do with a transcript/record, pricing, solo and public-interest plans

### Takeaway
The big research platforms (Westlaw Precision/CoCounsel, Lexis+ with Protégé, vLex Vincent) all now ingest uploaded transcripts and documents for summarization and Q&A with page/line citations, but they are built around *deposition* and *litigation document* workflows, not a Texas reporter's record or an appellate/habeas record; none of the sources found describe record-cite output in Texas "RR vol:page" or "CR page" form. Pricing is almost universally quote-only; the only published list prices found are TypeLaw ($895/brief, $400/mo self-serve), Thomson Reuters' self-serve configurator for firms ≤10 attorneys (reported ~$155–$640/user/mo depending on tier), and third-party reports clustering CoCounsel bundles at ~$225–$700/seat/mo. No official public-defender or nonprofit price program for Westlaw, Lexis, or CoCounsel surfaced; the closest is bar-association-bundled vLex/Fastcase and ad hoc local panel-attorney discounts.

### Inferences
- Every major research-AI vendor now sells "upload a transcript and ask questions" with page/line citations (CoCounsel Review Documents, Vincent Analyze a Deposition, CaseMap+ AI). None of the sources describe (a) multi-volume Texas reporter's records with volume-paginated cites, (b) clerk's-record vs reporter's-record distinction, (c) issue-spotting for preservation/objection rulings, or (d) output in Texas appellate citation form — so a record-review product's differentiator is the appellate/habeas record structure, not generic transcript Q&A.
- CoCounsel's stated throughput (~1 min per 50 pages per question) implies a 3,000-page reporter's record with 20 questions takes on the order of 20 hours of processing — a real cost for a multi-volume criminal record; this is an inference from the vendor's figure, not a measured result.
- Price floors matter: the credible solo entry points are roughly $155–$270/mo (Westlaw Edge w/ AI-Assisted Research, 3-yr term, reported), ~$69/mo (vLex self-serve, reported), $150/mo (Clearbrief, reported), vs. $400–$640+/mo for CoCounsel bundles and $30k+/yr for Harvey. Appointed-counsel and PD budgets will sit at the low end.
- Clearbrief is the closest existing product to "record-cite verification," but it verifies cites in a draft against uploaded documents; it does not appear to generate a digest of the record or spot issues from the record.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch official Thomson Reuters, LexisNexis, vLex, Clearbrief, Briefpoint, BriefCatch, or Paxton pricing pages; all price figures except TypeLaw's are reported, not official.
- No source found on page/document-size limits for CoCounsel, Protégé, or Vincent uploads (e.g., whether a 5,000-page record can be ingested).

- No official Westlaw/Lexis/CoCounsel public-defender or nonprofit price program was found; no information on whether Casetext's pre-acquisition PD/legal-aid discounts survived the migration.
- Harvey's actual terms for firms under 20 seats are unknown.

## Key Question 2: Transcript and record tools (Acrobat, Lit Software TranscriptPad/TrialPad, CaseMap/TextMap, Everlaw, DISCO, Relativity, Logikcull, Nextpoint, Digital WarRoom, JusticeText, Reduct, Rev/Descript) — can they ingest a Texas reporter's record PDF and what do they produce; pricing

### Takeaway
Transcript-management tools (TranscriptPad, TextMap) and e-discovery platforms (Everlaw, DISCO, Logikcull, Relativity) make transcripts searchable, annotatable, and issue-codable, and most now add AI summaries, but they are built for deposition transcripts and civil document review; they are priced per GB (e-discovery) or per suite subscription (Lit Suite ~$600/yr reported) and do not describe appellate-record-specific features. Audio/video transcription tools (JusticeText, Reduct, Rev, Descript, Vincent multimodal) matter for post-conviction work with body-cam and recorded hearings but produce transcripts, not record digests.

## Key Question 5: Citation and compliance tools (Westlaw/Lexis cite-checkers, BriefCatch, Clearbrief record-cite verification, Word add-ins, TRAP 9.4 word-count helpers)

### Takeaway
Cite-checking against case law is a solved, bundled feature (Westlaw Quick Check, Lexis Brief Analysis, CoCounsel Brief Builder); cite-checking against the *record* is essentially only Clearbrief (color-coded match scoring of a brief's record cites against uploaded transcripts/exhibits, ~$150/user/mo reported) and TypeLaw's human-plus-software service ($400 add-on to hyperlink cites to the record). TRAP 9.4 compliance (15,000 words, certificate of compliance) is handled by word-processor counts; no dedicated Texas word-count tool surfaced.

### Cited Findings
- TRAP 9.4: a brief in an appellate court (other than a death-penalty direct appeal) is capped at 15,000 words if computer-generated or 50 pages if not; civil aggregate 27,000 words / 90 pages; every word counts including headings, footnotes and quotations, excluding caption, tables, statement of issues, signature, certificates and appendix; a computer-generated document subject to a word limit must include a certificate of compliance stating the word count, and the certifier may rely on the word-processing program's count — [TRAP 9.4 text (Syfert)](https://syfert.com/texas/sections/RAP/9.4.html); word limits took effect December 1, 2012, converted at ~300 words/page — [Texas Supreme Court Misc. - Westlaw Quick Check: flags mischaracterized/omitted quotations, recommends authority, warns about cited authority, generates table of authorities; "Contrary Authority" finds uncited cases opposing an opponent's brief — [Thomson Reuters Quick Check](https://legal.thomsonreuters.com/en/products/westlaw/edge/quick-check); [LawNext 2019](https://www.lawnext.com/2019/07/ai-driven-brief-analysis-comes-to-westlaw-but-does-it-differ-from-competitors.html)

## Key Question 7: Adoption data (ABA Legal Technology Survey, Clio Legal Trends, Thomson Reuters, LexisNexis, State Bar of Texas) — solo/small-firm and criminal-defense usage and technology spend

### Takeaway
Measured AI adoption depends heavily on question wording: the ABA's firm-level measure (2024 survey, released 2025) shows ~30% overall and ~18% among solos, while Clio's individual-use measure shows 79% of legal professionals using AI (but only 40% using legal-specific AI, down from 58%), and a State Bar of Texas survey reportedly shows Texas lawyer AI use jumping from 30% (2024) to 62% (2026). No survey found breaks out criminal-defense or appellate lawyers specifically, and no technology-spend figures for that segment surfaced.

### Cited Findings
- ABA 2024 Legal Technology Survey (released 2025; 512 attorney respondents): ~30% report their office uses AI tools; firms with ≤50 attorneys 20% vs 39% for 51+; solos 17.7% rising to 47.8% at 500+ lawyer firms — [ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/web/article/aba-tech-report-finds-that-ai-adoption-is-growing-but-some-are-hesitant); [Lawyers Mutual NC](https://lawyersmutualnc.com/article/aba-technology-survey-shows-lawyers-as-cautious-adopters-of-generative-ai/); [Wisconsin Lawyer PDF](https://www.wisbar.org/NewsPublications/WisconsinLawyer/WisconsinLawyerPDFs/99/01/9.pdf); ABA 2023 AI TechReport — [ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/law_practice/resources/tech-report/2023/2023-artificial-intelligence-ai-techreport/)

### Inferences
- The gap between "any AI use" (79% Clio; 62% Texas) and "legal-specific AI use" (40% Clio) indicates that most solo/small-firm appellate and writ lawyers are using ChatGPT-class tools without legal grounding — the exact population producing the Texas sanctions cases. A purpose-built, verifiable record-review tool at consumer-adjacent pricing addresses that segment.
- Declining legal-specific AI share (58% → 40%) suggests price sensitivity: $400–$640/user/mo bundles are losing to free general tools among small firms.

### Gaps
- No survey found that isolates criminal-defense, appellate, or post-conviction lawyers' AI or research-tool usage.
- No technology-spend dollar figures per lawyer for solo/small criminal-defense firms were found (ABA and Clio reports were not accessed directly).
- LexisNexis survey data on adoption was not located.
- The ABA 2025 Legal Technology Survey (covering 2025) results were not found; all ABA figures are from the 2024 survey.
