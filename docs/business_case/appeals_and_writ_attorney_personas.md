# Primary Sources Confirm the Record-Read Thesis

Read directly rather than through search snippets, the Texas primary documents make the professional-tier case for Snot Nose Legal sharper and in two places cheaper to prove, while taking away one pitch the earlier edition leaned on. The Texas Indigent Defense Commission's own appellate caseload study allots a full-time appointed appellate lawyer **34.8 to 100.2 hours per appeal depending on reporter's-record length, of which 3 to 40 hours is record review**, and caps a full-time caseload at 40 / 30 / 20 / 14 appeals a year for records under 100, 100–500, 500–1,500 and over 1,500 pages ([TIDC Appellate Addendum, Dec 2016](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)); the ten county fee schedules read this pass pay that work at **$50–$155 an hour or $1,250–$3,500 flat**, and two of them (Bexar, El Paso) say in terms that "computer research" and equipment are non-reimbursable overhead ([Bexar District Courts fee order, June 2025](https://tidc.tamu.edu/IDPlanDocuments/Bexar/Bexar%20District%20Court%20Attorney%20Fee%20Schedule.pdf); [El Paso plan](https://tidc.tamu.edu/IDPlan/ViewPlan.aspx?PlanID=518)). The Court of Criminal Appeals' own tables show the writ lawyer's docket at **2,111 non-capital 11.07 applications received in FY2025**, of which 42% were denied without written order, 18% dismissed under the §4 subsequent-writ bar, 12% dismissed for non-compliance with the form rules and only 4.4% filed and set — and of the filed-and-set grants, roughly four in ten were restorations of a lost appeal or PDR rather than merits relief ([CCA Activity Detail FY2025](https://txcourts.gov/media/1462062/cca-annual-reports-fy25.pdf)). The courts of appeals fully reversed **2.6% of criminal dispositions in FY2025** (4.3% of merits dispositions), and the CCA's PDR grant rate ranged from 5.9% to 14.6% across FY2020–FY2025 ([COA Activity Detail FY2025](https://txcourts.gov/media/1461515/coa-activitydetail2025.xlsx); [CCA FY2025](https://txcourts.gov/media/1462062/cca-annual-reports-fy25.pdf)). The State Bar's 2026 survey puts criminal law at **47% AI adoption, the only practice area under 50%**, with citation accuracy the stated gating concern ([SBOT 2026 AI Survey](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Demographic_and_Economic_Trends&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=71798)), and the Supreme Court of Texas's proposed TRAP 9.1(d) would make every signer represent that "record citations are not fabricated" — proposed June 26, 2026, expected effective October 1, 2026, and still without a final order as of October 18 ([Misc. Docket 26-9054](https://txcourts.gov/media/1462936/269054.pdf); [Supreme Court Administrative Orders 2026](https://txcourts.gov/supreme/administrative-orders/2026/)). The plan is unchanged in shape — a $199 Counsel Review on existing rails, then a Record Desk for verification and export, then Chambers tenancy for public defender appellate divisions — and changes in three particulars: the pilot outcome metric becomes TIDC's own record-review hours by record bucket, the "voucher the software" pitch is dropped in favour of pricing the product as overhead a lawyer absorbs inside the hourly fee, and the post-affirmance TRAP 48.4 letter and out-of-time appeal/PDR screen move up the priority list because the CCA's tables show those claims are a large share of what habeas actually wins.

## Executive summary

**The two personas in a sentence each.** The *direct-appeal attorney* — one of **134 lawyers board-certified in criminal appellate law** as of December 31, 2025, a lawyer in a Harris or Dallas public defender appellate division, or one of the few dozen private lawyers who take ten or more appointed appeals a year — inherits a finished case at day 30–120 after sentencing, receives the clerk's and reporter's records as PDFs, and must "fully review the appellate record for all reviewable errors" and brief them, or honestly certify under *Anders* that there are none ([SBOT Attorney Statistical Profile 2025-26](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Demographic_and_Economic_Trends&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=70623); [TIDC Appellate Addendum](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf); [Performance Guidelines 9.5(G)](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Texas_Bar_Journal&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=14703)). The *writ attorney* — a solo or small-firm specialist retained by a family, a Harris County Public Defender post-conviction lawyer, a clinic attorney, or (for capital cases only) one of the Office of Capital and Forensic Writs' 14 attorneys — must decide whether a record and the facts outside it support an Article 11.07 application, then build one exhaustive, form-compliant filing on the CCA's 2018 form, because §4 bars a second, TRAP 79.2(d) bars rehearing of a denial, and the federal one-year clock is tolled only while a "properly filed" state writ is pending ([Falkenberg, *Article 11.07 Writs of Habeas Corpus*, Harris County PD, rev. May 2023](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf); [11.07 form, rev. 2018](https://txcourts.gov/media/1442926/1107-form-rev-2018.pdf); [FY25 Annual Report of the Judicial Support Agencies](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1461688/fy-25-annual-report-of-judicial-support.pdf)).

**The jobs the product should own.** Five, the same five for both personas in different proportions, and now each is anchored to a primary document. First, the preserved-error and issue audit of the entire record — Guideline 9.5(G)'s "fully review the appellate record for all reviewable errors" and the TIDC Delphi panel's "Record Review" task, which it made "the single most time consuming activity for appellate counsel" on long records ([Performance Guidelines](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Texas_Bar_Journal&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=14703); [Addendum p. 12](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)); the engine's `preserved_error` screen and per-finding `preserved`/`harmStandard`/`vehicle` fields already express it ([analysis.service.ts](/home/user/legal-ai/apps/api/src/services/analysis.service.ts); [analysis-fields.ts](/home/user/legal-ai/packages/case-lifecycle/analysis-fields.ts)). Second, the diligence record: volume-by-volume proof of what was read, which both an *Anders* brief and an 11.07 inventory stand on. Third, the writ viability screen and grounds inventory organised the way the 2018 CCA form demands (two pages per ground, item 15's explanation of why any prior writ omitted the ground, item 17's time-credit exhaustion), with the trial-counsel affidavit topics the IAC findings imply ([11.07 form](https://txcourts.gov/media/1442926/1107-form-rev-2018.pdf)). Fourth, the clock, computed from the rule text now read directly — TRAP 26.2, 26.3, 35.2, 49.1, 68.2, 73.4(b)(5), 73.5 — and labelled jurisdictional or discretionary ([TRAP, Feb. 6, 2026 compilation](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)). Fifth, in a later phase, verification of the lawyer's own record citations, which proposed TRAP 9.1(d) would turn from good practice into a signed representation ([Misc. Docket 26-9054](https://txcourts.gov/media/1462936/269054.pdf)). The product should not own research, argument drafting, or filing: vLex Fastcase is free to every Texas attorney through the State Bar, and the roadmap already forbids the research pitch ([texasbar.com, "Free Legal Research"](https://www.texasbar.com/); [professional_tier_expansion.md](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/business_case/professional_tier_expansion.md)).

**The gap in today's tools.** Not one vendor pricing or feature page could be read this pass either — every vendor host was refused at the proxy — so the tools landscape stays secondary and is labelled so throughout. What is primary is the demand side: Texas criminal lawyers are the least AI-adopting segment of the bar (47%), ChatGPT and Copilot dominate what they do use (63% and 44% of AI users), Westlaw/CoCounsel is the most-used legal-specific tool at 30%, and respondents said in open comments they "would use AI much more broadly if citation accuracy were solved" ([SBOT 2026 AI Survey](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Demographic_and_Economic_Trends&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=71798)). Appointed appellate pay runs at roughly **27–42% of the $368 statewide private median hourly rate** ([SBOT 2025 Income and Hourly Rate Report](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Demographic_and_Economic_Trends&Template=%2FCM%2FContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=72113); county schedules below), and no county plan read this pass reimburses software, research subscriptions or AI tools. Reported incumbent prices — CoCounsel bundles in the hundreds of dollars per seat per month, Clearbrief at $150 a month or $300 a brief, TypeLaw at $895 a brief — remain unverified third-party figures, but every one of them is priced for a buyer earning the private median, not the appointed rate.

**The plan's phases.** Phase 0 re-runs the eval gate on prompt set v2 and the production model, adds a direct-appeal reference ledger labelled by a board-certified appellate lawyer, and adopts the TIDC record-length buckets as the product's size taxonomy. Phase 1 ships **Counsel Review at $199 per record** on the existing Stripe Checkout, upload, pipeline and PDF rails: bar-number sign-up, a matter form keyed to vehicle, TAMES-volume recognition at upload, a Part B-first packet carrying the TRAP clock computed from the rule text, an *Anders* diligence appendix phrased as process, 11.07 findings grouped by candidate ground and mapped to the 2018 form's items, an affidavit-topics list, and a TRAP 48.4 post-affirmance checklist. Phase 2 ships the **Record Desk**: the internal QA side-by-side viewer exposed read-only to the ATTORNEY role, a statement-of-facts cite-table export, an 11.07 form-fit export with TRAP 73.1 checks, a TRAP 9.4(i) word-budget helper using the rule's own exclusion list, a per-matter deadline calendar, a verification log drafted to the proposed 9.1(d) representation, and the **Advocate** seat. Phase 3 ships **Chambers** office tenancy for public defender appellate divisions and clinics, with a pilot outcome metric taken from the Addendum (record-review and total hours by record bucket), plus the writ investigation layer. The sequencing and roughly nine engineering-week estimate of the professional-tier document stand, with the preserved-error screen already shipped in prompt set v2 ([professional_tier_expansion.md §5.3, §6.1](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/business_case/professional_tier_expansion.md); [analysis.service.ts](/home/user/legal-ai/apps/api/src/services/analysis.service.ts)).

## Persona 1: the direct-appeal lawyer works a TRAP clock on 35–100 hours a county pays for in part

**Who they are.** Direct-appeal work in Texas is split among a small specialist cadre, urban public defender appellate divisions, private appointed counsel on county lists, and prosecutors doing the mirror-image job. The State Bar counted **134 attorneys board-certified in Criminal Appellate Law** as of December 31, 2025 (133 in-state), up from 125 a decade earlier, against 776 in Criminal Law, down from 831; the Appellate Section had 2,046 members and the Criminal Justice Section 2,963, both smaller than in 2015-16 ([SBOT Attorney Statistical Profile 2025-26](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Demographic_and_Economic_Trends&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=70623)). The appointed segment is more concentrated than any directory count suggests: TIDC's appellate study found that "during FY 2015, only 55 Texas attorneys handled 10 or more court-appointed appellate cases," and could recruit only six private attorneys for timekeeping; its data came from the two Appellate Public Defender Offices in Dallas and Harris County, which supplied 607 and 250 cases with mandates between January 2012 and February 2016 — roughly 150 and 60 a year with known record lengths (estimate from the 4.1-year window) ([Addendum pp. 3–4, viii](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)). Harris County's plan places the Public Defender "on every Court's requested list" ([Harris District Court Plan §10.5.4](https://tidc.tamu.edu/IDPlan/ViewPlan.aspx?PlanID=294)). Current division headcounts stayed behind blocked county hosts: the 2011 Harris brochure's "division chief, ten assistant public defenders" and ~350 projected appeals, the 2013 evaluation's 5% reversal rate, and Dallas County's "five attorneys and one senior legal secretary" are all secondary ([Harris PDO brochure, 2011, not fetched](https://texascje.org/system/files/publications/Harris%20Co%20PDO%20Brochure%20%28Dec%202011%29.pdf); [Dallas County PD Appellate Division, blocked](https://www.dallascounty.org/government/public-defender/appellate.php)). County appointment lists set the entry bar: Dallas requires "two complete state court appellate briefs in criminal cases (excluding Anders briefs)" from the prior five years; Travis's A panel requires two prior felony briefs and says "An Anders brief alone will NOT satisfy the brief submission requirement"; Fort Bend requires three years' licensure and three authored briefs ([Dallas plan](https://tidc.tamu.edu/IDPlan/ViewPlan.aspx?PlanID=293); [Travis plan](https://tidc.tamu.edu/IDPlan/ViewPlan.aspx?PlanID=31); [Fort Bend qualifications addendum](https://tidc.tamu.edu/IDPlanDocuments/Fort%20Bend/Fort%20Bend%20District%20and%20County%20Court%20Attorney%20Fee%20Schedule.pdf)). The professional-tier document's archetype — "an appellate division is typically two to five lawyers carrying every appeal an office of dozens generates" — holds ([professional_tier_expansion.md §3.1](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/business_case/professional_tier_expansion.md)).

**The docket they work.** The courts of appeals received **3,607 new criminal cases in FY2025** (3,586 in FY2024; 3,714 in FY2023; 3,244 in FY2022) and disposed of 4,181, with 3,015 pending at year end and an average of 9.27 months from filing to disposition; the criminal clearance rate recovered from 81% in FY2022 to 96.7% in FY2025 ([COA Activity Detail 2025](https://txcourts.gov/media/1461515/coa-activitydetail2025.xlsx); [COA Activity Detail 2022](https://txcourts.gov/media/1455683/coa-activitydetail2022.xlsx)). Outcomes are lopsided: in FY2025, 2,018 criminal dispositions were plain affirmances (48%), 356 were modified and affirmed, 762 were dismissed (18%), 913 were "otherwise disposed," and **107 were full reversals — 2.6% of all criminal dispositions and 4.3% of merits dispositions** (estimate computed from the workbook; FY2022–FY2024 full-reversal rates were 4.1%, 2.9% and 4.0%). OCA's own FY2024 chart reads Affirmed 50%, Modified 2%, Reversed 4%, Dismissed 21%, Other 23% ([OCA Annual Statistical Report FY2024, p. 76](https://txcourts.gov/media/1461502/ar-statistical-fy24.pdf)). OCA reports no *Anders* counts and no pro se share for the courts of appeals; the opinion-summary workbooks count opinions by type and publication status (the First Court issued 947 opinions in FY2025, 726 published) but carry no *Anders* field ([COA Opinion Summary 2025](https://txcourts.gov/media/1461549/coa-opinion-summary-2025.xlsx)). TIDC's funnel from the previous decade gives the proportion: of 1,429,714 felony and Class A/B misdemeanor dispositions in FY2013–14, "only 11,917 cases reached the second phase: Proceedings in the Court of Appeals," and about 27% (2,512) went on to the CCA stage ([Addendum pp. 5–6](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)); the Addendum's two-year window does not reconcile exactly with OCA's annual filings and the OCA count is the one to use.

**Caseload and hours — the regulator's own numbers.** The December 2016 Appellate Addendum to TIDC's caseload guidelines, prepared by Texas A&M's Public Policy Research Institute from 857 Dallas and Harris PDO appeals and a twelve-member Delphi panel averaging 22.9 years in practice, is the single most useful document in this report and was unread in the earlier edition. Its headline recommendation: a full-time appellate lawyer should carry **no more than 40 appeals a year with records under 100 pages, 30 at 100–500 pages, 20 at 500–1,500 pages, and 14 over 1,500 pages**, which weights to 31.2 a year on the observed mix of 36% / 41% / 22% / 1% ([Addendum pp. ix, 13–17](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)). The hours behind that are the product's economics: Delphi hours per case of **34.8 / 46.4 / 69.1 / 100.2** against 1,389 case-related hours in a 2,087-hour year; current-practice hours actually spent through court-of-appeals mandate of **17.2 / 31.4 / 49.5 / 97.3**, so "a reporter's record with less than 100 pages requires about 17 hours to reach an appellate court mandate, while a case with more than 1500 pages requires almost 100 hours"; and, by task in the court of appeals, Delphi record review of **3 / 9 / 17 / 40 hours** and brief writing of 12 / 16 / 30 / 35 hours, with the panel concluding that "the review of records exceeding 1500 pages in length should be the single most time consuming activity for appellate counsel" ([Addendum pp. 9–13](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)). The study chose record length as its difficulty metric because it "is one of the few consistently available indicators of difficulty and attorney effort available for appellate cases" and is "widely used as a caseload metric among appellate defender offices"; it states no pages-per-hour rate (the implied 30–90 pages an hour for a 500–1,500-page record is an estimate from its tables). It also recorded that oral argument happens in only 4% of court-of-appeals cases against a Delphi ideal of 33–40%, and that current full-time caseloads of 67 / 41 / 26 / 14 exceed its recommendation by 40%, 27%, 23% and 0% ([Addendum pp. 10, 14](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)). The 2015 trial-level guidelines explicitly excluded appeals ([TIDC Guidelines 2015](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/kzfd5tup/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-01222015.pdf)). The out-of-state proxies the earlier edition used — California's one hour per 50 pages, Washington's 36 appeals at 350-page transcripts and 51-hour weeks — are retained only as context and remain secondary ([CAP-LA guidelines, not re-read](https://cap-la.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/2018-rev-claim-GUIDELINES.pdf); [WA OPD decision package, not re-read](https://opd.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-10/000293-2025_1T-AppellateCSI.pdf)).

**The jobs to be done.** The State Bar's Performance Guidelines state them. Appellate counsel must "immediately consult with the trial court to ascertain relevant information concerning the perfection of the appeal and relevant filing deadlines," "immediately contact trial counsel," submit designations of the clerk's and reporter's records, "review the clerk's and reporter's records to determine whether they are true, correct and complete in all respects" and object to errors at once, then "fully review the appellate record for all reviewable errors, prepare a well researched and drafted appellate brief, file the brief in a timely manner," consider a reply brief or rehearing, and after an adverse decision "advise the client in writing by certified mail of the client's right to file a petition for discretionary review," explaining that an indigent client has no right to appointed counsel for the PDR but may request one ([Performance Guidelines 9.5–9.6, Texas Bar Journal July 2011](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Texas_Bar_Journal&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=14703)). The rules put teeth on each. TRAP 38.1(i) requires "a clear and concise argument for the contentions made, with appropriate citations to authorities and to the record"; TRAP 44.2(a) requires reversal for constitutional error "unless the court determines beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the conviction or punishment," while 44.2(b) says other error "that does not affect substantial rights must be disregarded" ([TRAP 38.1, 44.2](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)). TRAP 48.4 requires counsel, within five days of the opinion, to send the client the opinion and notice of the pro se PDR right by certified mail with return receipt and certify compliance; the Harris County PD paper reports that "the Court still sees valid out-of-time PDR claims based on counsel errors on a weekly basis" fifteen-plus years after the rule ([Falkenberg p. 25](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)). TRAP 33.1's preservation requirement and the practitioners' framing of the job as a preserved-error audit are carried from the earlier edition and remain secondary ([Bennett & Bennett, not re-read](https://bennettandbennett.com/texas-criminal-appeal-lawyer); [GMSR, not re-read](https://www.gmsr.com/winning-on-appeal-begins-with-the-record/)). The jobs, then: audit the whole record for preserved error and rank by harm standard; decide early whether this is a merits brief or an *Anders* brief; write a statement of facts and argument in which every factual sentence carries a record cite; manage the clock; and discharge the client-communication duties the CCA enforces by habeas.

**The workflow and its clock, from the rule text.** The notice of appeal is due "within 30 days after the day sentence is imposed or suspended in open court," or 90 days "if the defendant timely files a motion for new trial"; the court of appeals may extend if, within 15 days after the deadline, the party files the notice in the trial court and a Rule 10.5(b) motion in the appellate court ([TRAP 26.2, 26.3](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)). The record is due 60 days after sentencing, 120 if a timely motion for new trial is filed and denied, or 60 after an order granting one ([TRAP 35.2](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)). The appellant's brief is due 30 days after the later of the records (TRAP 38.6 was not transcribed in this pass; the 30-day figure is from the earlier edition's secondary source) ([TRAP 38.6, state-rules.com, not re-read](https://www.state-rules.com/texas/appellate/38.6)). A motion for rehearing "may be filed within 15 days after the court of appeals' judgment or order is rendered" and "will not be granted unless a response has been filed or requested by the court" ([TRAP 49.1–49.2](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)). A PDR "must be filed within 30 days after either the day the court of appeals' judgment was rendered or the day the last timely motion for rehearing or timely motion for en banc reconsideration was overruled," with a 15-day extension window, and must contain separately numbered grounds "with record page references if petitioner has the record," a statement of the case that "should seldom exceed half a page," and the court of appeals' opinion as an appendix ([TRAP 68.2, 68.4](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)). Word limits, re-lettered but not changed on January 1, 2026, are 15,000 words for a brief, 7,500 for a reply, 4,500 for a PDR and 2,400 for a PDR reply, counting everything "except the following: caption, identity of parties and counsel, statement regarding oral argument, table of contents, index of authorities, statement of the case, statement of issues presented, statement of jurisdiction, statement of procedural history, signature, proof of service, certification, certificate of compliance, and appendix," and the certifier "may rely on the word count of the computer program used to prepare the document" ([TRAP 9.4(i)](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf); [Misc. Docket 25-9104](https://txcourts.gov/media/1461814/259104.pdf)). The CCA's grant rate on PDRs filed from the courts of appeals was **8.0% (FY2020), 11.2% (FY2021), 8.8% (FY2022), 6.4% (FY2023), 5.9% (FY2024) and 14.6% (FY2025)**, the last driven by 59 grants from the Third Court and 23 of 32 from Kinney County; Harris produced 113 PDRs and 7 grants, Tarrant 99 and none ([CCA Activity Detail FY2025](https://txcourts.gov/media/1462062/cca-annual-reports-fy25.pdf); [FY2020–FY2024 reports](https://txcourts.gov/media/1459678/cca-annual-reports-fy24.pdf)). Of granted PDRs disposed in FY2024, OCA reports 18% affirmed, 43% reversed and 31% vacated ([OCA FY2024 report p. 71](https://txcourts.gov/media/1461502/ar-statistical-fy24.pdf)). The CCA's operating procedures add that "first motions for extension of time are usually granted," oral argument is 20 minutes a side, and opinions issue Wednesdays at 9:00 a.m. ([CCA Internal Operating Procedures, rev. Jan. 2025](https://txcourts.gov/media/1455241/cca-internal-operating-procedures.pdf)). Record delivery through the TAMES Attorney Portal, the Uniform Format Manual and the 2025 exhibit guidelines are carried from the earlier edition as secondary ([14th COA Attorney Record Portal, not re-read](https://txcourts.gov/14thcoa/news/attorney-record-portal/)), as are the *Kelly v. State* duties when filing an *Anders* brief ([Kelly v. State, vLex, not re-read](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/kelly-v-state-no-891562739)).

The earlier edition's figures for Midland ($200/hour to 20 hours) and the 81st/218th districts ($60–$120/hour to $4,500), Harris's appointed-counsel cost growth from $78.3M to $112.0M, the N.D. Tex. CJA rate of $175/hour with a $9,700 appeal maximum, and the Harris public-defender share of 17% of appointments are retained as secondary and were not re-read ([Harris County Auditor review, blocked](https://auditor.harriscountytx.gov/portals/auditor/documents/AuditDocs/081426Indigent%20Defense%20Attorney%20Billing%20Review%20Fiscal%20Years%202023%20through%202025.pdf); [Community Impact, not re-read](https://communityimpact.com/houston/spring-klein/government/2026/03/24/harris-county-looks-to-increase-capacity-of-public-defenders-assigned-counsel-in-criminal-courts/)). Private retainer ranges for Texas criminal appeals specifically were again not found in any primary source.

**What they do today, and what is still unknown.** The State Bar's 2026 survey is the first primary evidence of habit: 62% of Texas attorneys use AI, 47% of criminal lawyers do, legal research is the top use at 53%, drafting is 40%, ChatGPT is used by 63% of AI users and Copilot by 44%, and the sample skews toward exactly this persona (median age 58, 31% solo) ([SBOT 2026 AI Survey](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Demographic_and_Economic_Trends&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=71798)). Structural facts stand: briefs are drafted in a word processor because the TRAP 9.4(i)(3) certificate relies on its count; filings go out as PDFs through eFileTexas; records arrive as per-volume PDFs. Whether appellate lawyers annotate in Acrobat or print, whether they build transcript digests, and how many hours go to each phase remain unsourced beyond the Addendum's task buckets, and belong in interviews.

## Persona 2: the writ lawyer gets one shot, and the CCA's tables show what that shot usually buys

**Who they are and the volume they face.** Non-capital 11.07 habeas is a docket of about **2,100 applications a year** at the Court of Criminal Appeals: "Habeas Corpus Received" added 2,940 in FY2020, 2,834 in FY2021, 2,334 in FY2022, 2,040 in FY2023, 2,126 in FY2024 and 2,111 in FY2025, with 264 pending at the end of FY2025, plus about 205–370 supplemental 11.07 filings a year ([CCA Activity Detail FY2025](https://txcourts.gov/media/1462062/cca-annual-reports-fy25.pdf); [FY2020](https://txcourts.gov/media/1450024/cca-annual-reports-fy20.pdf)). OCA's narrative calls FY2024's level "the lowest since at least 2001," and the Harris County PD paper's FY2021 figure of 3,325 new applications — the number the professional-tier document carried — appears to count all habeas matters rather than the "received" line, so the two are not in conflict so much as differently scoped ([OCA FY2024 report p. 67–71](https://txcourts.gov/media/1461502/ar-statistical-fy24.pdf); [Falkenberg p. 12](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf); [professional_tier_expansion.md §8](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/business_case/professional_tier_expansion.md)). Filings are concentrated where the personas are: Harris 334, Dallas 259, Tarrant 209 and Bexar 126 together were 36% of the 2,560 county-attributed habeas filings in FY2025, followed by Montgomery 69, Travis 64, Collin 63 and Jefferson 60 ([CCA FY2025](https://txcourts.gov/media/1462062/cca-annual-reports-fy25.pdf)). The CCA does not report counsel-filed versus pro se applications in any year; the professional-tier document's "84% of applications from people without counsel" figure came from a Texas Defender Service report on 11.073 cases and remains secondary ([professional_tier_expansion.md S9](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/business_case/professional_tier_expansion.md)). Inside the Court, each application "is assigned a cause number and randomly assigned to one of seven staff lawyers who work exclusively on extraordinary writs," who write a memorandum and recommendation before the case is assigned to a judge ([Falkenberg p. 12](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)).

What the docket does with those applications is the fact the earlier edition could only half-read. In FY2025 the CCA's disposition table for non-capital applications totalled 2,341: **470 denied without written order on the Court's own review and 511 denied without written order on trial-court findings (together 41.9%); 411 dismissed under Art. 11.07 §4 (17.6%); 270 dismissed for non-compliance (11.5%); 183 remanded for an evidentiary hearing or affidavits (7.8%); 103 filed and set (4.4%)**; 70 dismissed because a direct appeal was pending; 42 because the sentence was discharged; 36 as moot; 24 because community supervision had not been revoked; 63 under abuse-of-writ orders (estimates of shares computed from the table) ([CCA FY2025](https://txcourts.gov/media/1462062/cca-annual-reports-fy25.pdf)). The pattern is stable: non-compliance dismissals ran 213–299 a year and §4 dismissals 411–538 a year across FY2020–FY2025, while remands fell from 11% of dispositions in FY2020–21 to 8% in FY2025 ([CCA FY2020–FY2024 reports](https://txcourts.gov/media/1459678/cca-annual-reports-fy24.pdf)). The filed-and-set outcomes show what habeas actually wins: of 117 filed-and-set dispositions in FY2025, 57 were relief granted (plus one in part), **21 were out-of-time appeals granted and 27 were out-of-time PDRs granted**, 4 were denials; the out-of-time grants were 45, 40 and 48 in FY2023–FY2025, roughly a third to 40% of filed-and-set dispositions each year ([CCA FY2023–FY2025](https://txcourts.gov/media/1462062/cca-annual-reports-fy25.pdf)). OCA's gloss that "habeas corpus relief was granted in most of the cases that were filed and set" (98%) is true of the filed-and-set subset and should not be read as a grant rate on the docket, which is nearer 4% ([OCA FY2024 report p. 71](https://txcourts.gov/media/1461502/ar-statistical-fy24.pdf)). Capital habeas is a separate and shrinking stream: new 11.071 writs received fell from 25 in FY2020 to 10 in FY2025, and OCFW — 14 attorneys, 10 mitigation specialists, 6 paralegals and 2 accountants, up from 12/7/3 in FY2022–FY2024 — represents "over 70%" of initial 11.071 applicants and 8 forensic-writ clients out of "over 70" Forensic Science Commission referrals since 2020 ([FY25 Judicial Support report pp. 69–70](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1461688/fy-25-annual-report-of-judicial-support.pdf); [FY24](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1459752/fy-24-annual-report-of-judicial-support.pdf)). The innocence organisations are the other institutional actor: IPTX, running the Texas A&M and Texas Tech clinics under TIDC contract, logged **479 new requests in January–May 2025 and 776 in June–December 2025** — 1,255 in the year, roughly four times the ~190 per half-year of 2023–24 — against 106 cases under active investigation, 12 in active litigation and **605 requests awaiting screening** at year end; it won relief in three cases in 2025 (Causey's exoneration, Padron's vacatur and dismissal); UH's Innocence Project reports about 19,800 requests since inception; TIDC funds six law-school projects at $100,000 a year each; UT's clinic takes intake "through the mail" only and "does not accept requests for assistance from third parties, including friends and family members" ([IPTX report Jun–Dec 2025](https://www.tidc.texas.gov/media/dyhfbkld/iptx-tt-and-aandm-activity-report-june-2025-to-december-2025.pdf); [IPTX report Jan–May 2025](https://www.tidc.texas.gov/media/yz2jlee5/tt-and-aandm-january-2025-to-may-2025.pdf); [UH Innocence Project report](https://www.tidc.texas.gov/media/4sijzymu/u-of-houston-biannual-report-060125-123125.pdf); [TIDC innocence projects](https://www.tidc.texas.gov/funding/innocence-projects); [UT AIC report](https://www.tidc.texas.gov/media/ogtozv0y/ut-ip.pdf)). Harris County's Public Defender has a dedicated post-conviction practice whose lawyer authors the CCA-hosted 11.07 paper ([Falkenberg](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)); the Dallas PD's 2008 withdrawal from 11.07 appointments and the private writ bar's size remain secondary and unknown respectively.

**Economics.** There is no constitutional right to habeas counsel (*Pennsylvania v. Finley*; *Ex parte Mines*); a trial court may appoint counsel "when the trial court concludes that the interests of justice require representation" under arts. 1.051(d)(3) and 26.04(c), "typically" does so when the CCA remands for a live hearing, and must do so under Art. 11.074 when the State represents the applicant "is not guilty, is guilty of only a lesser offense, or was convicted or sentenced under a law that has been found unconstitutional"; filing fees are prohibited ([Falkenberg pp. 6, 9–10](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)). When counsel is appointed, the county schedules pay the same lines as appeals: Harris $125 an hour for an 11.07 writ and $150 for 11.071, Travis $155, Tarrant $50–$125, El Paso $85/$100, Collin $250 per writ ([county schedules above](https://tidc.tamu.edu/IDPlanDocuments/Harris/Harris%20District%20Court%20Attorney%20Fee%20Schedule.pdf)). Capital 11.071 counsel appointed outside OCFW is paid by the convicting court, with state reimbursement to the county capped at **$25,000 per application** for fees and expenses together and the excess "the obligation of the county"; at Harris's $150 that cap is about 167 attorney hours including any expert spend (estimate) ([Fair Defense Laws 2025, Art. 11.071 §2A, pp. 12–14](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/2ilf2z0x/final-2025-fair-defense-laws.pdf)). The retained-fee shape from three firm websites — a paid consultation, "at least $10,000" to investigate, "at least $15,000" to file; $15,000–$45,000 with most around $30,000, roughly half investigation — was not re-read and stays secondary ([Bennett & Bennett, not re-read](https://bennettandbennett.com/post-conviction); [Chris Perri Law, not re-read](https://www.chrisperrilaw.com/blog-posts/how-much-does-a-writ-of-habeas-corpus-cost-in-texas)). Two primary facts sharpen the investigation phase's cost. An indigent appellant gets a free record only "[w]ithin the time for perfecting the appeal" under TRAP 20.2; there is no parallel rule for an 11.07 applicant, Texas and Fifth Circuit cases deny a free transcript for collateral attack absent a non-frivolous claim and specific need (secondary), and a 1973 Attorney General opinion noted that Art. 11.07 "fails to specifically provide payment to the court reporter in the case of an indigent petitioner" (secondary) ([TRAP 20.2](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf); [14th COA opinion quoting *Escobar*, blocked](https://txcourts.gov/All_Archived_Documents/14thCOA/Case/Opinions/031501/010232f.PDF)). The only transcript rate located — Tarrant County's $6.00 a page for an original and two copies in indigent cases, effective December 15, 2025 — is a snippet of a local order on the OCA portal the proxy refused; at that rate a 2,000-page record bought new costs about $12,000 (estimate) ([Tarrant order, topics.txcourts.gov, blocked](https://topics.txcourts.gov/LocalRulesPublic/PreviewAttachment/2737)). The practical route is the appellate record already prepared for the direct appeal, which is why the consumer product's records guidance says so ([mvp_v1_prd.md US-2](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/specifications/mvp_v1_prd.md)).

**The workflow and its clock.** The application goes to the convicting court on the CCA's form; after the State's answer the court designates controverted issues or the clerk forwards the record (the answer and designation periods are statute text the proxy blocked and are carried from the earlier edition as secondary); designated issues must be resolved "within 180 days from the date of receipt of the application by the State," and "[o]n the 181st day … the district clerk shall forward the writ record to this Court unless the district court has received an extension of time" ([TRAP 73.4(b)(5), 73.5](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)). The clerk's transmittal must carry a summary sheet stating the court, judge, whether a hearing was held, whether findings were made, the recommendation, and "name of counsel if applicant is represented" ([TRAP 73.4(b)(3)](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)). Fact issues are resolved by "affidavits, depositions, interrogatories, additional forensic testing, and hearings, as well as using personal recollection," a hearing reporter must prepare the transcript within 15 days, and "[w]rit records regularly reach the Court without transcripts from the hearing or any indication that a live hearing occurred" ([Falkenberg p. 10](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)). The Court then votes "to deny, dismiss, remand, hold, file and set, or grant"; "[w]rit applications are routinely remanded for findings and resolution when they allege facts, which, if true, could entitle the applicant to relief," usually with 90 days for the habeas court; applications "that do not 'allege facts' are routinely denied without written order"; a dismissal is non-merits and a denial is "a final adjudication"; TRAP 79.2(d) bars a motion for rehearing, though a "suggestion that the Court reconsider on its own motion" gets a merits ruling ([Falkenberg pp. 12–14](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf); [TRAP 73.6](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)). The CCA "may deny relief based upon its own review of the application" and "may dismiss an application that does not comply with these rules" ([TRAP 73.2, 73.6](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)). Section 4 bars a later application on the same conviction unless the factual or legal basis was unavailable or the innocence gateway is met; a writ seeking only an out-of-time appeal or PDR does not "challenge the conviction" and so does not trigger §4, and when an out-of-time appeal is granted the other claims are dismissed under *Ex parte Torres* ([Falkenberg pp. 15–16](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)). Jurisdiction attaches only when the conviction is final: "[i]f the case is pending on direct appeal or discretionary review, the conviction is not final and will not be final until the appellate mandate issues" (*Ex parte Johnson*), which is why 70 applications were dismissed in FY2025 as "direct appeal pending" ([Falkenberg p. 4](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf); [CCA FY2025](https://txcourts.gov/media/1462062/cca-annual-reports-fy25.pdf)). Federally, the Fifth Circuit's rules remain snippet-sourced because every federal host was blocked: no PDR means finality 30 days after the court of appeals' judgment, the mandate date is irrelevant (*Roberts v. Cockrell*; *Gonzalez v. Thaler*); a PDR refusal means finality when the 90-day certiorari window closes; an 11.07 filed before the mandate is not "properly filed" and tolls nothing (*Larry v. Dretke*, resting on *Ex parte Johnson*); a §4-dismissed subsequent writ that complied with filing rules still tolls (*Villegas v. Johnson*); the cert window after a state-habeas denial does not toll (*Ott v. Johnson*); equitable tolling follows *Holland* but only in "rare and exceptional circumstances" ([Casemine on *Larry*, not re-read](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/proper-filing-of-state-habeas-applications-under-aedpa:-insights-from-larry-iii-v.-dretke/view); [Fifth Circuit opinions, blocked](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/pub/16/16-20651-CV0.pdf)). The combined effect, an inference from the two sets of rules: the federal year starts on day 31 after the court-of-appeals judgment when no PDR is filed, but the state writ cannot be properly filed until the mandate issues, so the first weeks of the federal year run untolled; the PRD's deadline engine already states the tolling direction correctly ([mvp_v1_prd.md FR-5](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/specifications/mvp_v1_prd.md)). The Harris paper also records that some jurisdictions require "a 'shell writ' containing only bare allegations before appointing counsel," that applicants file early placeholders "to avoid missing AEDPA deadlines," and that the CCA "no longer grants motions to dismiss writ applications without question" (*Ex parte Speckman*) ([Falkenberg p. 11](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)). Adjacent vehicles — 11.072 community-supervision writs decided by the trial court and appealed to the court of appeals, 11.09 misdemeanor writs — have no line in any CCA report, and their clocks (60 days from the State's answer in 11.072) stay secondary from the earlier edition ([TDCAA 11.072 checklist, not re-read](https://www.tdcaa.com/wp-content/uploads/Art.-11.072-Writs-Checklist.pdf)).

**Inputs and outputs.** The legally operative document is the CCA's form, and it is still the "Revised 2018" form, the only 11.07 form the CCA's forms page posts; no Miscellaneous Docket order from 2021 through 2026 touches TRAP 73 or the form, though the CCA re-constituted its Rules Advisory Committee in 2025 ([CCA forms page](https://txcourts.gov/cca/practice-before-the-court/forms/); [CCA Misc. Docket Orders](https://txcourts.gov/cca/miscellaneous-docket-orders/); [Misc. Docket 25-009](https://txcourts.gov/media/1461806/misc-docket-25-009-court-of-criminal-appeals-rules-advisory-committee.pdf)). Its instructions are the product's spec: "All applicants and petitioners, including attorneys, must use the complete application form"; "Failure to follow these instructions may cause your entire application to be dismissed"; "Each ground shall begin on a new page, and the recitation of the facts supporting the ground shall be no longer than the two pages provided"; "Do not attach any additional pages for any ground"; legal argument goes in a separate memorandum certified not to exceed "15,000 words if computer-generated or 50 pages if not"; "A factual summary that merely references an attached memorandum or another ground for relief will not constitute a sufficient summary of the facts"; and "If the application form does not include all of the grounds for relief, additional grounds brought at a later date may be procedurally barred" ([11.07 form, rev. 2018](https://txcourts.gov/media/1442926/1107-form-rev-2018.pdf)). Its eighteen items include what the application concerns (conviction, sentence, time credit, parole, "out-of-time appeal or PDR"), the plea and trial type, the direct appeal and PDR history, prior 11.07 applications with "why the current grounds were not presented and could not have been presented in your previous application," and time-credit exhaustion through TDCJ's dispute-resolution system; verification is by unsworn declaration or notarised oath, with a State Bar number for attorney petitioners and an aggravated-perjury warning ([11.07 form](https://txcourts.gov/media/1442926/1107-form-rev-2018.pdf)). TRAP 73.1 adds the 14-point typeface, the certificate of compliance, and "[a]ny ground not raised on the form will not be considered" ([TRAP 73.1](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)). The Harris paper confirms the consequence: "Failure to certify to the word count in the memorandum and exceeding the two page per ground limit will cause a writ application to be peremptorily dismissed, as will not using the form altogether," except where the trial court recommends an agreed grant ([Falkenberg p. 7](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)). The "non-compliant white card" is practitioners' name for the card-form notice of that dismissal; the phrase appears only in a UT CLE session title and in no CCA document (secondary) ([UT CLE CR18 brochure, blocked](https://utcle.org/conferences/CR18/brochure)).

The inputs are the trial and appellate record, trial counsel's file and affidavit, the prosecutor's file, and expert material, and the primary sources now say how each is obtained. Trial counsel's file belongs to the former client, but Ethics Opinion 657 (secondary; the opinion host was blocked) says counsel "may not deliver to a former client documents provided to the lawyer pursuant to article 39.14(a) (other than the defendant's own statement)" and must redact 39.14(f)-protected information, so the State's discovery must be obtained anew from the prosecutor ([Opinion 657, blocked](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/opinions/opinion-657)). The Michael Morton Act applies to offenses committed on or after January 1, 2014 (*Garza v. State*, secondary), leaving older files to office discretion, Public Information Act requests (where, after the post-*Holmes* amendment, a conviction's closed file is not covered by §552.108(a)(2) — secondary), or habeas-court discovery orders after issues are designated ([UT CLE discovery paper, blocked](https://utcle.org/ecourses/OC7249/get-asset-file/asset_id/41140); [Falkenberg pp. 10, 25](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)). Crime-lab records will have a new channel: the DPS "Texas Crime Lab Records (CLR) Connect portal," created after OCFW's forensic litigation, "is expected to go live during August 2026" ([FY25 Judicial Support report p. 70](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1461688/fy-25-annual-report-of-judicial-support.pdf)). The client channel runs through TDCJ: attorney-of-record calls "shall be confidential," do not count against monthly minute caps, are "subject to monitoring and recording, except calls to the offender's attorney(s) of record providing legal services," and are requested and approved under BP-03.81 rather than placed at will; the directive read is dated 2009 and may be superseded, and BP-03.81 itself, the I-162/I-163 forms and the 2023 legal-mail exemption from digitisation stay secondary ([TDCJ ED-03.32, hosted by TCDLA](https://www.tcdla.com/Images/TCDLA/Resources/tdcjtelephonereg.pdf); [TDCJ attorney forms, blocked](https://www.tdcj.texas.gov/policy/atty_forms.html)). The doctrines the engine screens for are stated in primary practice materials: *Elizondo*'s "clear and convincing evidence that no reasonable juror would have convicted him in light of the new evidence"; Art. 11.073's "contradicts scientific evidence relied on by the state at trial" with relief on a preponderance that the applicant "would not have been convicted," the *Robbins* holding that an expert's changed opinion is new scientific knowledge (codified in 11.073(d)), and its inapplicability to punishment evidence (*Ex parte White*); the out-of-time PDR line (*Wilson*, *Crow*, *Florentino*, *Owens*, *Riley*) and out-of-time appeal line (*Axel*, *Garza v. Idaho*, *Castillo*); and the form of relief in *Ex parte Thompson*, where "[a]ll deadlines shall be calculated as if Applicant was sentenced on the date of this Court's mandate" ([Falkenberg pp. 23–29](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf); [Udashen slides](https://txcourts.gov/media/1203204/Udashe_IPOT-PowerPoint-on-Legal-Issues1-and-Standards-in-Texas-Innocence-Cases-2.pdf); [*Ex parte Thompson*](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1447603/wr-91-220-01-thompson-opinion-061720.pdf)). A secondary Texas Defender Service figure — 15 of 74 Art. 11.073 applications (20%) won relief between 2013 and 2023 — is the only 11.073 outcome rate found ([DPIC summary, blocked](https://deathpenaltyinfo.org/new-report-reveals-texas-junk-science-statute-fails-to-adequately-provide-relief-for-innocent-prisoners-including-robert-roberson)).

**The jobs to be done.** The professional-tier document's five jobs — triage before quoting a fee, the record review for a retained matter, fitting the form, the affidavit bottleneck, and the clock — survive and gain sharper evidence ([professional_tier_expansion.md §4.2](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/business_case/professional_tier_expansion.md)). Three refinements follow from the primary tables. The triage job is bigger than the firm: IPTX's screening backlog grew to 605 while its litigation docket stayed at 10–12, so a structured, grounded pre-review is as valuable at an innocence organisation's intake as at a solo's fee quote. The form-fit job is measurable: 270 non-compliance dismissals in FY2025 is 11.5% of the docket, and the dismissal rule is mechanical. And the appeal-restoration job is larger than the earlier edition knew: with 48 out-of-time appeals and PDRs granted in FY2025 against 57 merits grants, the question "did appellate counsel send the TRAP 48.4 letter and did trial counsel perfect the requested appeal" is the single most winnable 11.07 inquiry, which is why the consumer PRD already built FR-11 around it and why this plan moves the post-affirmance checklist into Phase 1 ([mvp_v1_prd.md FR-11](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/specifications/mvp_v1_prd.md); [CCA FY2025](https://txcourts.gov/media/1462062/cca-annual-reports-fy25.pdf)).

## Development plan: three phases from Counsel Review to Chambers

The plan keeps the professional-tier document's shape and sequencing — per-record product first, seats second, offices third, no v3 workspace — and fills in the workflows, screens, inputs and outputs the primary sources support. Four design principles run through every phase. The artifact is the pitch: lawyers buy on a real Part B page with a volume-page-line cite and verbatim excerpt. Verifiability is the product: every finding shows the page it came from, and the product never emits a case citation it has not been given — it cites statutes and doctrine categories, and the research pitch stays forbidden. The lawyer is the reviewer: the template says "issues identified for counsel's evaluation," never an outcome probability, and the *Anders* appendix documents what was read rather than certifying there is nothing to find. And deadlines are labelled by consequence — jurisdictional (notice of appeal, PDR window, the 181st-day transmittal) versus discretionary (the brief, extensions "usually granted") — chained from the dates the matter form captures ([CCA Internal Operating Procedures](https://txcourts.gov/media/1455241/cca-internal-operating-procedures.pdf)). Two principles are added by this pass. The product adopts the Addendum's record buckets (<100, 100–500, 500–1,500, >1,500 pages) as its size taxonomy in pricing, eval and reporting, because that is how Texas regulators and defender offices already measure the work. And the product produces no brief text: Bexar's rule that anyone who "assisted the attorney of record" must be named on the voucher and Harris's "I wrote this brief and I am solely responsible for its contents" certification are the disclosure lines a drafting feature would cross, and an inventory with excerpts does not ([Bexar order, Guideline 13](https://tidc.tamu.edu/IDPlanDocuments/Bexar/Bexar%20District%20Court%20Attorney%20Fee%20Schedule.pdf); [Harris 2007 voucher](https://tidc.tamu.edu/IDPlanDocuments/Harris/Harris%20District%20Court%20Attorney%20Fee%20Voucher%20-%20Appeals.pdf)).

### Phase 1 — Counsel Review on existing rails (first quarter of professional launch; the document's month 4)

**The workflow.** A lawyer lands on `/for-counsel`, signs up with email, password, bar number and practice type (private, PDO, clinic), which creates an ATTORNEY in a new tenant with the bar number queued for manual verification. The consumer eligibility wizard is replaced by a **matter form**: vehicle (direct appeal, PDR-clock-only, 11.07, 11.072, 11.09, §2254 follow-on), lane (trial or plea), county and court, offense and sentence, sentencing date, motion-for-new-trial date if any, record-filed dates, court-of-appeals judgment and mandate dates if any, prior writ history, and a free-text "evidence outside the record" note; capital cases are refused at this form exactly as the PRD refuses them at US-0. Upload reuses the presigned-S3, resumable, malware-scanned path, with one addition: **TAMES-volume recognition** that reads the cover pages to label each PDF as clerk's record, reporter's record volume N of M, exhibit volume or supplemental record, echoes the inventory back ("RR vols. 1–7 of 7, CR, exhibit vol. 6 — no audio/video exhibits received"), records what was not received, and reports the record's Addendum bucket. The progress page is the existing status page with the professional voice; the no-finding-counts-before-QA rule stays. The pipeline runs the lane's screens weighted by vehicle — direct appeal weights preserved error, voir dire and identification; 11.07 weights IAC, Brady, 11.073 and sentencing — and sampled rather than full QA applies to prose while citation re-verification stays 100% and blocking. Purchase is the existing Stripe Checkout one-time flow at **$199** with the 5,000-page cap and the $49 overage; the cap is generous against a docket in which 99% of appointed appeals had records under 1,500 pages ([Addendum Table 5-2](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)). A promo code covers pilot offices.

**The output.** A Part B-first PDF, with Part A available as an optional, off-by-default client letter. Part B gains six sections on top of the findings, the priority list and the investigation checklist that already render. A *record inventory* (volumes, pages, OCR confidence per volume, exclusions, Addendum bucket, and the Delphi record-review hours the bucket implies, cited to the Addendum as context rather than as a promise). A *deadline posture* block that adds the **TRAP clock** from the rule text — notice of appeal 30/90 days from sentencing with the 15-day extension window (26.2, 26.3), record 60/120 (35.2), brief 30 days after the later record (38.6), rehearing 15 days after judgment (49.1), PDR 30 days plus 15 (68.2), and for 11.07 matters the 180-day resolution and 181st-day transmittal (73.4(b)(5), 73.5) — alongside the existing 11.07/AEDPA/laches posture, each date labelled jurisdictional or discretionary and stamped "as of" ([TRAP](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)). An ***Anders* diligence appendix** listing every screen run, every volume and page range read, and every item not reviewed, phrased as a record of process mapped to Guideline 9.5(F)–(G), never a conclusion ([Performance Guidelines](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Texas_Bar_Journal&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=14703)). A **post-affirmance checklist** keyed to TRAP 48.4 and Guideline 9.6 — opinion and pro se PDR notice sent within five days by certified mail, return receipt, certification filed — because the CCA grants out-of-time PDRs for its absence "on a weekly basis" ([Falkenberg p. 25](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)). For 11.07 matters, **findings grouped by candidate ground** with the vehicle label and §4-exception tag the consumer's subsequent-writ mode already computes, each ground's facts summarised to the two-page budget, and the form's items 1 (including "out-of-time appeal or PDR"), 13–15 (appeal, PDR, prior writs and why grounds were not raised before) and 17 (time-credit exhaustion) pre-populated from the matter form, plus an **affidavit-topics list** — the IAC findings' `develop` steps rewritten as the specific questions trial counsel will be ordered to answer ([11.07 form](https://txcourts.gov/media/1442926/1107-form-rev-2018.pdf)). And a per-finding **"verified against record"** mark backed by the existing hash-anchored re-verification. The UPL template variant — "issues identified for counsel's evaluation" — applies throughout, and the packet footer states it was prepared at counsel's direction for counsel's use.

**Inputs and outputs by vehicle.**

| Vehicle | Inputs the matter form and upload collect | Outputs specific to the vehicle |
|---|---|---|
| Direct appeal | CR, RR volumes, exhibit volumes; judgment and sentence; charge; notice of appeal; sentencing, MNT and record-filed dates | Preserved-error ledger (objection → ruling → page → preserved → harm standard under 44.2(a)/(b)); issues by priority with vehicle labels; *Anders* diligence appendix; TRAP clock; *Kelly* checklist; TRAP 48.4 post-affirmance checklist |
| PDR (clock only) | Court-of-appeals judgment date, rehearing motion dates | PDR and extension deadlines (68.2); 4,500-word limit note (9.4(i)(2)(E)); 48.4 letter reminder; no merits analysis |
| 11.07 | Record; judgment; appellate opinion and mandate dates; prior writ history; trial-counsel file if held; new-evidence note | Findings by ground with §4 tags and form-item mapping; affidavit topics; investigation checklist with `dependsOn` records; 11.07/AEDPA/laches posture; "not final until mandate" warning; complete-inventory framing |
| 11.072 / 11.09 | Record or plea papers; community-supervision or misdemeanor status | Same screens; trial-court clock (secondary rule text, flagged); note that the ruling is appealable to the court of appeals |
| Plea convictions (any vehicle) | Plea papers, admonishments, judgment | Plea-lane screens as in the consumer product |

**Build and gate.** Roughly four engineering-weeks per the professional-tier document's rows for tenancy surfaces, sign-up, matter form, billing reuse, template variant, ops and analytics, plus the volume-recognition, TRAP-clock and 48.4-checklist increments. The gate to Phase 2 is the document's own: ten paid Counsel Reviews, at least two from the consumer referral loop, and attorney feedback on what the engine missed captured per finding ([professional_tier_expansion.md §5.3](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/business_case/professional_tier_expansion.md)).

### Phase 2 — the Record Desk: verify, cite, export (months 4–8)

**The workflow.** The lawyer opens a delivered matter in the browser and works a **verification view**: the findings list on the left in priority order, filterable by screen, vehicle and preservation; on the right the record page the selected finding cites, with the verbatim excerpt highlighted; one click accepts, rejects or annotates. This is the internal QA side-by-side workspace the PRD already describes, exposed read-only to the ATTORNEY role and scoped to findings and pages — not the Neo4j graph, not chat, not drafting ([mvp_v1_prd.md US-8](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/specifications/mvp_v1_prd.md); [professional_tier_expansion.md §6.4](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/business_case/professional_tier_expansion.md)). Every accept/reject is an attorney-labelled data point that flows into the eval ledgers — the second evidence stream the business case wanted for Gate 3.

**The exports.** A **statement-of-facts cite table** (DOCX and CSV): chronological record events with Texas-form citations, witness, exhibit and excerpt, built from the entity index and timeline Part B already carries, so the brief's factual sentences start with their cites attached and TRAP 38.1(i) is satisfied by construction. A **preserved-error table** for the argument section: objection, ground, ruling, page, preservation, harm standard. An **11.07 form-fit export**: accepted findings mapped to numbered grounds, each ground's supporting-facts statement checked against the two-page budget, the memorandum against the 15,000-word budget, and a TRAP 73.1 checklist (every ground on the form, each on a new page, 14-point type, certificate of compliance, verification page, State Bar number); the form itself is the CCA's 2018 form, and the product fills a structure, not an argument ([TRAP 73.1](https://txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf)). A **TRAP 9.4(i) word-budget helper** that computes countable words for a pasted draft using the rule's own exclusion list and emits certificate-of-compliance text — trivial, sticky, and offered by no vendor the research found. A **verification log** that records, per export, which record citations were viewed and accepted by the signer, drafted to the proposed 9.1(d) representation that "legal citations, quotations, holdings, and record citations are not fabricated," and labelled as supporting the lawyer's own review rather than replacing it ([Misc. Docket 26-9054](https://txcourts.gov/media/1462936/269054.pdf)). A **deadline calendar** per matter with ICS export and the jurisdictional/discretionary labels. **Checklist artifacts**: the *Anders* packet (motion to withdraw, client notice, form motion for pro se record access per *Kelly*) and the TRAP 48.4 letter, as templates a licensed user completes. And an **activity log export** of the lawyer's own verification sessions in six-minute increments, Harris's billing unit, for the itemisation form Art. 26.05(c) requires — the product never bills or suggests billing AI-saved time, which Opinion 705 forbids ([Harris District fee schedule](https://tidc.tamu.edu/IDPlanDocuments/Harris/Harris%20District%20Court%20Attorney%20Fee%20Schedule.pdf); [Fair Defense Laws 2025](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/2ilf2z0x/final-2025-fair-defense-laws.pdf); [Texas Bar Blog on Opinion 705](https://blog.texasbar.com/2025/04/articles/ethics/ethics-opinion-offers-principles-for-lawyers-ethical-use-of-ai/)).

**Billing.** The **Advocate** seat at $99 a month with one Counsel Review included, via Stripe Checkout in subscription mode and the customer portal, plus the matter list with deadline posture on every matter. The $99 re-run with a supplemental record already exists and diffs against prior findings.

**Build.** Roughly four engineering-weeks: the viewer exposure and feedback capture (1.5), the exports (1.5), the calendar, helper and log (0.5), seats (0.5 beyond the document's billing estimate). The gate to Phase 3 is per-case buyers converting to seats and at least one pilot office running five or more records.

### Phase 3 — Chambers for offices, and the writ investigation layer (months 9–12 and beyond)

**Office tenancy.** The **Chambers** seat at $249 a month per attorney with free investigator and viewer seats, office admin, shared matters and a pilot-office flag — the existing multi-role RBAC. Three public defender pilots start free in Phase 1 on the county budget calendar with the conversion ask framed as a TIDC Improvement Grant "process improvement" line, and the pilot's **outcome metric is taken from the Addendum**: hours from record-filed to brief-filed, split into record review and brief writing, by record bucket, against the current-practice baseline (17.2 / 31.4 / 49.5 / 97.3 total; 3 / 9 / 17 / 40 Delphi review hours) — the one measurement a chief defender, a TIDC reviewer and a commissioners court all already recognise ([Addendum Figs. 3-1, 4-2b](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)). The Harris and Dallas appellate PDOs, which supplied the Addendum's data, are the natural first two; Bexar's two-lawyer division is the third ([professional_tier_expansion.md §3.1](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/business_case/professional_tier_expansion.md)). Coexist with LegalServer by export, not integration. Ship the county security packet: SOC 2 timeline, CJIS-aligned controls, US data residency, zero-retention provider attestations, a DPA, and a TRAIGA statement, with the caveat that TRAIGA's enrolled text and its reach to legal-services vendors remain unverified (secondary) ([Perkins Coie on TRAIGA, blocked](https://legacy.perkinscoie.com/insights/update/texas-enacts-responsible-artificial-intelligence-governance-act)).

**The investigation layer for writ practitioners and clinics.** A **records tracker** on the investigation checklist: each `dependsOn` item carries requested/received/not-available states, a date, and a source vocabulary drawn from the primary findings — trial counsel's file (minus 39.14 discovery), the prosecutor's file (Morton Act for post-2014 offenses, PIA or discretionary request otherwise), habeas-court orders under §3(d), the DPS CLR Connect lab portal from August 2026, TDCJ time-credit records — so the lawyer sees which findings are blocked on which records and the re-run picks up what arrives ([FY25 Judicial Support report](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1461688/fy-25-annual-report-of-judicial-support.pdf); [Falkenberg](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1457413/1107-paper-53123-update.pdf)). The **A/V add-on** the roadmap already prices for v1.1 — jail calls, bodycam, recorded hearings — transcribed and tied to the same cite-and-verify loop. A **§2254 exhaustion map**: for each ground, where it was raised (direct appeal, PDR, 11.07) and the federal clock position, with the tolling-direction rule the PRD already states and the "not properly filed before the mandate" warning. **Clinic intake mode**: consumer referral packets (US-5, consent-based) arriving in a clinic's Chambers tenant as pre-triaged matters, matched to the clinics' own gate ("post-conviction, felony, completed direct appeals, actual innocence") so that the 605-case screening backlog gets structured input rather than more mail ([IPTX Jun–Dec 2025](https://www.tidc.texas.gov/media/dyhfbkld/iptx-tt-and-aandm-activity-report-june-2025-to-december-2025.pdf); [business_case_12mo.md §8](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/business_case/business_case_12mo.md)).

**What is deliberately excluded.** Case-law research and citator claims; drafting of argument or any brief text; PDR merits analysis; capital 11.071 matters; the knowledge-graph and chat workspace. Each either competes with a free or entrenched incumbent, is argued on documents the engine does not read, crosses a county disclosure line, or expands UPL and verification exposure without serving one of the five jobs.

## Risks: UPL, confidentiality, the pending TRAP 9.1(d) rule, and the one-shot writ

**Unauthorized practice.** Selling to licensed lawyers lowers UPL exposure relative to the consumer tier but does not remove it. The risk zones are three: the optional Part A client letter, which is family-facing and must keep the consumer disclaimers and never reach the family except through the lawyer; the checklist templates and form-fit export, which must fill structure rather than select strategy; and the referral directory, which the PRD flags as a lawyer-referral service under Occupations Code chapter 952 unless it is a neutral directory with no matching and no per-referral consideration ([mvp_v1_prd.md R-6](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/specifications/mvp_v1_prd.md)). The *Parsons Technology* history and HB 1507's software carve-out are carried from the earlier edition as secondary ([Fifth Circuit, 179 F.3d 956, not re-read](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/179/179.F3d.956.99-10388.html)).

**Confidentiality and billing.** The State Bar's own summary of Opinion 705 (February 2025) sets four duties: competence ("attorneys cannot blindly rely on generative AI outputs but must critically assess and verify the accuracy of generated content"), confidentiality ("thorough vetting of generative AI tools for confidentiality safeguards" and staff training), verification and supervision (unverified output "could expose attorneys to potential violations of rules related to fairness, honesty, and candor to the court"), and billing (no billing for hours not worked; direct AI costs passable "with appropriate prior agreement"); the State Bar's AI Toolkit adds sample client disclosures and a tool-evaluation checklist ([Texas Bar Blog on Opinion 705](https://blog.texasbar.com/2025/04/articles/ethics/ethics-opinion-offers-principles-for-lawyers-ethical-use-of-ai/); [SBOT AI Toolkit](https://www.texasbar.com/Content/NavigationMenu/ForLawyers/State_Bar_of_Texas_AI_Toolkit/default.htm)). Nothing in the Bar's summary makes client consent a condition; the duty is tool-vetting, which the product's zero-retention providers, no-training promise, deletion on request and RLS tenancy answer, provided the DPA and the `/for-counsel` trust section say so in the vendor's own words and the full opinion (unread; host blocked) says nothing stricter. Two conflicts are specific to this business and unchanged: the same record can exist in a family's consumer tenant and a lawyer's professional tenant, and the vendor holds records from both sides of a channel conflict; the engagement terms must address both. County buyers add CJIS and NIST expectations; those are a Phase 3 cost.

**The citation-verification rule.** The decisive Texas development is primary and still pending. On June 26, 2026 the Supreme Court of Texas gave preliminary approval to amendments to TRCP 13 and TRAP 9 and 52 under which "[b]y signing a filed document, the signer represents to the court that the signer has: (1) reviewed the filing and verified its accuracy, including that the legal citations, quotations, holdings, and record citations are not fabricated," with sanctions "proportionate" to the violation, lesser sanctions before striking or dismissal, a good-faith presumption, and a comment that the paragraph "is adopted principally to address concerns about the improper use of artificial intelligence in filings … Verifying a filing for accuracy is important, regardless of whether artificial intelligence was used"; comments closed September 1, 2026; the Court "expects the amendments to take effect on October 1, 2026"; as of October 18, 2026 no final-approval order appears on the Court's 2026 orders page or the Rules Advisories list, the TRAP compilation is still dated February 6, 2026 with the old Rule 52.11, and the CCA has issued no joint order ([Misc. Docket 26-9054](https://txcourts.gov/media/1462936/269054.pdf); [Administrative Orders 2026](https://txcourts.gov/supreme/administrative-orders/2026/); [Rules Advisories](https://txcourts.gov/rules-forms/rules-standards/rules-advisories/); [CCA Misc. Docket Orders](https://txcourts.gov/cca/miscellaneous-docket-orders/)). Two consequences for the product. The appellate text names "record citations," which the civil TRCP 13 text omits — record-cite verification is explicitly in scope, and a page-anchored verification view is the direct answer. And because the order is a Supreme Court order whose criminal application depends on a CCA joint order that has not issued, marketing must say "proposed; finalisation pending" until a final order is read. The federal and earlier-Texas sanctions trail — *Ex parte Lee*, the Dallas and Travis County orders, the Fifth Circuit's *Fletcher* sanction for sixteen fabricated quotations, Judge Starr's 2023 certification order (current status unconfirmed), the Fifth Circuit's June 2024 decision not to adopt Rule 32.3, and E.D. Tex.'s December 2025 CV-11(g) amendment — remains secondary because every court host was blocked ([Fifth Circuit, *Fletcher v. Experian*, not re-read](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/25/25-20086-CV0.pdf); [King & Spalding on Rule 32.3, not read](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/us-fifth-circuit-decides-against-its-proposed-rule-amendment-on-ai-use-in-legal-filings)). The failure mode courts punish is fabricated quotation, which is precisely what the engine's verbatim-excerpt hard filter prevents for record cites; the product's posture should be explicit that it emits record citations it can show on the page and doctrine categories, never case citations, and that the marketing never claims to eliminate verification, only to make it fast.

**The 11.07 §4 one-shot problem, and its *Anders* twin.** The gravest harm this product could do to a writ client is to make a lawyer believe the inventory is complete. The CCA dismissed 411 applications under §4 in FY2025 and bars rehearing under TRAP 79.2(d); the form warns that "additional grounds brought at a later date may be procedurally barred" ([CCA FY2025](https://txcourts.gov/media/1462062/cca-annual-reports-fy25.pdf); [11.07 form](https://txcourts.gov/media/1442926/1107-form-rev-2018.pdf)). The PRD's complete-inventory framing (FR-8) and subsequent-writ tagging (FR-9) already say that claims omitted from a first writ are ordinarily lost; the professional packet must say it more plainly, and the Record Desk must make "what was not reviewed" as prominent as what was found ([mvp_v1_prd.md FR-8, FR-9](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/specifications/mvp_v1_prd.md)). The direct-appeal twin is *Anders*: Guideline 9.5(G) places the full review on counsel, and an *Anders* brief or an ineffective-assistance-of-appellate-counsel claim that rests on a tool's summary creates exposure for the lawyer and reputational risk for the vendor ([Performance Guidelines](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Texas_Bar_Journal&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm&ContentID=14703)). The diligence appendix is therefore a record of process that supports the lawyer's own reading and never a certification of no error. The engine's own models disagreed on whether the Gary cumulation order was dispositive or background, which is the right illustration for a lawyer: the product ranks, the lawyer decides ([manual_vs_engine_gary_2026-09-27.md §5](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/evaluation/manual_vs_engine_gary_2026-09-27.md)).

**Other material risks.** The professional-tier document's register — two go-to-markets on a two-engineer team, channel conflict at TCDLA, PDO procurement stalls, brand blocking Chambers, the eval gate, sampled QA missing a citation error, work-product objections, an adjacent vendor adding a criminal-record screen — stands ([professional_tier_expansion.md §7.5](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/business_case/professional_tier_expansion.md)). The primary pass adds two. The economics differ by county more than the earlier edition allowed: in the hourly-uncapped counties the individual lawyer gains nothing per case from saved hours, so the Advocate seat's appeal there rests on caseload capacity and on retained work, and the office is the buyer; the `/for-counsel` copy must not promise per-case income. And the market is smaller at the top than the earlier edition's extrapolation: 3,600 criminal appeals, not 4,000–6,000, and an appointed segment of a few dozen high-volume lawyers, which makes the PDO pilots proportionally more important ([COA Activity Detail 2025](https://txcourts.gov/media/1461515/coa-activitydetail2025.xlsx); [Addendum p. 3](https://tidc.texas.gov/media/xjvdibv2/guidelines-for-indigent-defense-caseloads-appellate-addendum-12142016.pdf)). The eval risk keeps its specific face: the Brian ledger was 2/4 on the latest fresh run with run-variance remediation pending, and the production model has not been re-scored, so Phase 1 cannot ship on the Gary ledger alone ([business_case_12mo.md §0](/home/user/legal-ai/docs/business_case/business_case_12mo.md)).

## Conclusion

Reading the primary documents changes the professional-tier thesis in one direction: it moves the argument from analogy to arithmetic. The earlier edition had to reason from California's fifty pages an hour and Washington's 51-hour weeks; this one can say that Texas's own indigent-defense commission allots nine to forty hours of record review per appeal depending on length, that the counties pay for it at $100–$155 an hour or inside a $1,800–$7,500 cap, that two of them say in writing that research tooling is overhead, and that the courts' own tables show what the work yields — a 2.6% full-reversal rate on appeal, a 4.4% filed-and-set rate on habeas, and a habeas docket in which restoring a lost appeal or PDR is nearly as common as winning on the merits. Those numbers make the product's value proposition narrower and more defensible at once. For the appointed appellate lawyer in a flat-range county, the product converts hours over the cap into effective rate; for the lawyer in an hourly county and for the public defender office, it converts the same hours into caseload inside TIDC's own ceiling; for the writ lawyer, it converts a $10,000 screening phase into a $199 inventory and a specific affidavit list, and tells the family's lawyer first to check whether the 48.4 letter was ever sent.

What the primary sources could not reach also matters, and the honest statement of it is part of the plan: the statutes, the vendor pages, the federal cases and the county budgets are still behind blocked hosts, and the single rule that would make record-cite verification a signed representation in every Texas appellate filing was proposed in June, expected in October, and unconfirmed in mid-October. The product should be built as though that rule will issue — a verification view, a verification log, excerpts that can be shown on the page — because every other primary document read this pass points the same way: the least AI-adopting segment of the Texas bar says it will use these tools when citation accuracy is solved, and a product that shows its page can say it has solved it for the one kind of citation it makes.
